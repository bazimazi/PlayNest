# Prove PlayNest's promises before shipping more

PlayNest's next improvement should be proof and focus, not more features or more content. The working tree already holds first versions of nearly everything the phase 5 notes ranked highest: safer storage, search, a dim night screen, kid mode, printing, hard-moment cards, first aid, crying support, songs, festivals, adaptations and activities to age 12. What it still lacks cannot be produced at a keyboard: clinician sign-off on first aid, test calls to every helpline, a day on real iPhones and low-end Androids, paid readers from the cultures it names, and time watching real families use it. On UI, the top priority is pulling Today back to one decision, because it has regrown to about 20 possible blocks, against the 11-section feed that v1.2 removed. On content, the priorities are depth for ages 8–12 where the child leads, one card per game family with each community's own chant, a story pack gated by community standing, and Persian and Arabic with right-to-left layout, written by native writers. This order matters because PlayNest's differentiators are promises (private, offline, safe, respectful), and each one fails in a way parents will not forgive: a wrong emergency step, a dead crisis number, a lost garden, a misattributed song. Nothing here has been tested with parents, and the underlying research ran out of search budget partway, so limits are stated where they apply.

## First versions exist, so the bottleneck has moved from building to proving

Read the notes with one caution: their repo checks describe the tree before the current edits. Note 01 says there is no share, print or speech code in `src/`, and at the time of this report there is. The tree was also changing during this synthesis. **Between 20:49 and 21:00 on 4 November 2026, activities went from 784 to at least 884, hard-moment situations from 18 to 41, first-aid cards from 0 to 25, stories from 0 to 23 and songs from 27 to at least 45.** All of it is uncommitted. Treat every count below as a floor and re-run `scripts/content-stats.ts` before acting on one.

## Today has regrown past the feed that v1.2 cut

The clearest UI problem is one the project already solved once. `docs/PLAN.md` §11 found that an 11-section Today made the one job compete with everything else. Today now has a mood picker, hero, situation chips, help and celebration nudges, alternatives, plan, garden, milestone nudge, widgets for stage, stories, talk, songs and routines, a together nudge, two carousels, a tip, a backup card and an install card. Several are conditional, but this is a dashboard. It also runs against the market. Rivals are converging on everything apps: **Kinedu just added a full baby tracker** ([listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kinedu-baby-development/id741277284)) and Nara added guides ([listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nara-baby-tracker/id1444639029)). The products getting attention for calm do the opposite: set up once, then small daily use, as with [Tiimo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tiimo-daily-ai-planner-to-do/id1480220328), [Readmio](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/readmio-read-aloud-books/id1473021827) and [Yoto's Green Button](https://pr.taosnews.com/article/Yoto-unveils-its-biggest-hardware-update-yet/6aa2726a06b0f49a929976a1) (a paid press release, with no usage data). My recommendation is to keep mood, one pick, situation chips, two alternatives, today's plan and the garden, allow at most one conditional nudge at a time, and move the stage, story, talk, song and routine widgets into a single "More for Noor" row. This is judgement rather than evidence: no screen-level comparison of rival home screens was possible, so test it with the 5–8 families note 01 proposes.

Retention should stay guilt-free, and the trade-off should be accepted openly. Across seven studies, **streak effects come mostly from how the log displays behaviour, are stronger when people blame themselves for a break, and are weaker when they can repair it** ([Silverman & Barasch](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/49/6/1095/6590647)). Duolingo's own experiments show small gains: +1.7% day-7 retention from streak animations and +0.38% daily actives from freezes ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/)). Finch's angriest reviews concern a 234-day streak reset and a two-year streak lost after a logout ([Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/finch-app-reviews-emotional-attachment-user-retention-product-loyalty/)). Apple retrofitted pausing without penalty ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/06/watchos-11-brings-powerful-health-and-fitness-insights/)). For prompts, one early-childhood text experiment found that **one or five texts a week each did worse than a set of three** ([Cortes et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24827)), so three optional prompts is a starting dose to test, not a settled number. In the tree, the album that only grows is right and `playStreak` is no longer shown, so delete it. Still needed are a "resting" garden state after silence, a welcome-back line with no numbers, and a copy audit so Pip is never sad, hungry or waiting. No evidence quantifies what dropping streaks costs a parent app, and local-only analytics cannot measure it well, so this remains a values decision.

Design for attention lapses, not "decision fatigue". In sleep deprivation, **lapses in simple attention are the largest effect (g = −0.776)** ([Lim & Dinges](https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/LimDinges2010MetaAnalysis.pdf)), new mothers' performance worsens over weeks ([Insana](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3524545)), and a 36-lab ego-depletion test found d = 0.06 ([Vohs et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34520296/), seen through a search summary only). Lim & Dinges measured total deprivation rather than the broken sleep parents have, so read it as direction, not magnitude. The 10-second Undo is done. Next come a "recent actions" list, a 400 ms double-tap guard and drafts that survive a phone call. For accessibility, the `text-scale` meta tag and `prefers-contrast` are in, and they matter because between a fifth and 37% of people resize text ([Appt](https://appt.org/en/stats/font-size), [Tumath](https://www.joshtumath.uk/posts/2026-07-02-lets-fix-the-webs-text-size/)). What remains is the 200% audit at 320 and 360 px, a manual pass on the [WCAG 2.2 additions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/) that axe does not fully cover, and a grade-6 reading check in CI, since read-aloud and short lines are the low-literacy path.

One half-day of device testing would settle more than any further desk research. The open questions are `window.print()` and `<a download>` from a Home Screen app, lock-screen audio after a 30-second pause, timers and `ended` events while locked, opening an `.ics` file, speech with the mute switch on, and the `persist()` result in each mode. These decide whether print needs a PDF fallback, how far the noise timer can be trusted and whether calendar reminders work at all. The notes found no measured eviction rate for installed iOS web apps. WebKit offers only "we do not expect" deletion ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/)) and still evicts per origin under storage pressure ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)). Add a low-end Android to the same day: the 75th-percentile budget device is a Galaxy A24 4G on 9 Mbps, and low-end single-core performance is about nine times slower than a current iPhone ([Russell](https://infrequently.org/2025/11/performance-inequality-gap-2026/)).

Some platform limits should simply be stated in the product. A no-server web app cannot deliver a reliable timed reminder, because Periodic Sync is best-effort and Chromium-only ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Periodic_Background_Synchronization_API)) and Declarative Web Push still needs a push server ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/16535/meet-declarative-web-push/)). So use a "what's next" card on open plus a calendar file, and say "PlayNest can't remind you when it's closed". A web app also cannot lock a phone, so [Guided Access](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111795) is the real boundary and the in-app gate only stops accidental exits. Kid targets should stay at about 2 cm ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/children-ux-physical-development/), qualitative), and the notes found nothing direct for two-year-olds. Motion polish is now cheap but lower value: view transitions work on iOS 18 and later ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/view-transitions)), and spring easing through `linear()` on iOS 17.2 and later ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_types_easing-function_linear-function)). The one new feature worth adding is family voices, since rivals sell recordable voices as a paid accessory ([Eddi](https://storypod.com/)) and a parent's recording captures the emotional part at low cost, once iOS storage limits are checked.

## Emergency and crisis content needs named reviewers before parents rely on it

First aid cannot be written once and shipped everywhere, because the bodies genuinely disagree. **AHA/AAP 2025 now alternates 5 back blows with 5 abdominal thrusts in children and uses the heel of one hand for infant chest thrusts** ([AHA interim materials](https://cpr.heartandstroke.ca/resource/Guidelines2025InterimBLSProviderManualEN)). Resuscitation Council UK uses two-thumb encircling and starts CPR with 5 rescue breaths ([RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)). ANZCOR uses chest rather than abdominal thrusts in children ([ANZCOR](https://www.anzcor.org/home/basic-life-support/guideline-4-airway)). NHS pages still teach two fingers and say the order of methods does not matter ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/how-to-stop-a-child-from-choking/)). Honey for a swallowed battery is another split: AAP allows it over 12 months ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/what-parents-need-to-know-about-button-and-lithium-coin-batteries.aspx)), ANZCOR uses jam under one ([ANZCOR](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-5-1-first-aid-management-of-poisoning)) and the NHS page does not mention it ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/what-to-do-if-your-child-has-an-accident/)). The tree's region picker is the right structure. The rule to protect is that a card must never blend bodies, because a blend is a protocol nobody published. Each card needs one body's steps, up to six imperatives, a call banner, one "do not" line and a footer with body, version and review date. It also needs original artwork, since infant hand position differs by region and the sources' images cannot be reused. The notes read AHA only through interim materials, because the full guideline returned 403, so a clinician must check the wording against the primary text.

Stay on the reference side of the regulatory line. The FDA lists first-aid encyclopedias and hotline lists as examples of non-devices ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications/examples-software-functions-are-not-medical-devices)). MHRA says a disclaimer does not rescue an app that qualifies ([MHRA](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6abf6c8c943e890cb55e5e52/Medical_device_stand-alone_software_including_apps__including_IVDMDs.pdf)) and that lay symptom entry leading to first-aid recommendations may be a device ([MHRA appendix](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64a7d235c531eb000c650051/Appendix_1_-_symptom_checkers.pdf)). So choose cards by named emergency, build no quiz that outputs a colour, and build no dose calculator. Apple and Google each require specific reminders ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511)). Take regulatory advice before any red, amber and green feature.

Phone numbers need a human, and PlayNest should plan for one. **In a review of 69 mental-health apps, 6 (9%) listed crisis numbers that were non-existent, non-functional or tied to a non-evidence-based organisation** ([Martinengo 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6921471/)). PlayNest's global emergency table was merged from [UK FCDO](https://www.gov.uk/api/content/foreign-travel-advice) and [Canadian](https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/france) travel pages. It has 239 rows in the notes, 30 of which conflict and 34 of which mix general and service numbers, and 228 rows in the tree, so find out which rows dropped and why. The helpline pack reached 36 countries or territories, but an operator or government page confirmed a mental-health or parent line for only 21. Egypt has nothing verified and Saudi Arabia only a general medical line. Iran's 1480 is listed as an addiction helpline, not general counselling ([Find A Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/countries/ir)). Before any release, someone must test-call every entry, store a "last verified" date and an owner, and re-test at least quarterly. Crisis lines must stay separate from support lines that say they are not crisis services.

The crisis screens should stay buttons, with no scoring and no memory. An unscored check-in fits the evidence better than the validated tool RESEARCH-2 proposed: the two-question Whooley screen has 96% sensitivity but only 57% specificity ([Whooley](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9229283/)), so many positives would be false in people already short of sleep, and SIGN finds insufficient evidence for it as an accurate tool ([SIGN 127](https://www.sign.ac.uk/media/1065/sign127_update.pdf)). Normalising matters too, since **up to 80% of new mothers and over two-thirds of new fathers report unwanted infant-related thoughts** ([IOCDF](https://iocdf.org/expert-opinions/perinatal-ocd/)), but the app cannot tell OCD from psychosis, so safety steps come first and reassurance after. The notes' wording for "I might hurt my baby" and "I want to die" is explicitly a draft, and no evidence comparing crisis-screen wordings was found, so it needs a perinatal clinician and a suicide-prevention body before release. Keep the "nothing is stored or sent" line only if a build test proves it. The FTC's $7.8 million BetterHelp order shows how this data class is treated ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/03/ftc-ban-betterhelp-revealing-consumers-data-including-sensitive-mental-health-information-facebook)).

Crying copy should promise knowledge rather than prevention. In a trial of 1,279 mothers, PURPLE materials raised walk-away behaviour and sharing the advice with other carers (51.5% v 38.5%) ([Barr 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2659818/)), yet a Pennsylvania programme covering 1.59 million infants found no reduction in abusive head trauma ([Dias 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28135348/)). So say "this helps parents know what to expect", add a prompt to share the plan with every carer, and word the walk-then-sit method as "many babies settled in one study", because it rests on 21 infants ([RIKEN](https://www.riken.jp/en/news_pubs/research_news/pr/2022/20220914_1/index.html)). All 14 sleep machines tested exceeded the 50 dB(A) nursery limit at 30 cm at full volume, and three exceeded 85 ([Hugh 2014](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2013-3617)). A phone cannot know its own loudness, so the Sounds page should keep its low default and timer and never state a decibel value.

## Hard moments and ages 8–12 need firmness and independence, not more volume

The coach's wording should now reflect a change in the evidence. A meta-analysis of 244 trials found that programme effects fell early and then levelled off ([VU](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/have-parenting-programs-for-disruptive-child-behavior-become-less/)). An individual-participant analysis of 14 European trials found **reduced harsh verbal discipline and more following through were the strongest mediators, and praise was the only measured mediator that was not unique** ([JAACAP record](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1737034/individual-participant-data-meta-analysis-individu)). Warm scripts that never hold a limit therefore fit the weakest part of the evidence. Every card should end in a calm, doable follow-through, and each card should be audited for that, not only for tone. Gentle parenting has one descriptive study with no child outcomes ([Pezalla & Davidson](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0307492)), so PlayNest can be warm without adopting the label. No trial tests a mid-meltdown phone card, so copy should say "draws on" tested programmes, never "proven". The time-out rule in `coach.ts` follows the conservative path the notes recommend, since [CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/positive-discipline-for-young-children) says not under 3 while AAP and CDC examples start at 2. Cards with no dedicated source, including car seat, hair wash, "I hate you", backtalk, restraint collapse and interrupting, should say "general guidance" and go to a clinician first. The biggest hole is culture. The notes opened no source on how authority, shame or co-sleeping advice changes across the Middle East, Asia, Africa or Latin America, so lines such as "what if grandma disagrees" and "get close and get their attention" are hypotheses for local parents to test.

Ages 8–12 is where PlayNest has the clearest open field, and the activity list should change in kind, not just difficulty. Children in Britain are now allowed out alone at an average of 10.8, against 9.7 for their parents' generation, and spend under 25 minutes a week playing in nearby streets ([Play England](https://www.playengland.org.uk/newsblog/british-childrens-play-survey-exposes-the-generational-gap-in-childrens-freedom-to-play)). The average child quits a sport by 11 ([Project Play](https://projectplay.org/youth-sports/facts/challenges)), girls are less active ([Sport England](https://www.sportengland.org/news-and-inspiration/childrens-activity-levels-rising-inactivity-remains-too-high)), and reading enjoyment is the lowest in 20 years ([National Literacy Trust](https://literacytrust.org.uk/research-services/research-reports/children-and-young-peoples-reading-in-2025/)). The note's bank of 269 ideas has 69 rows where the child runs things as planner, chef, map-reader or quiz-master, and those rows should come first. Design around one real conflict: Safe Kids says under-10s need an adult to cross streets ([Safe Kids](https://www.safekids.org/tip/pedestrian-safety-tips)), while independence advice encourages solo errands at 8–9, so errands at that age should avoid crossings or have a parent watching from a distance. Avoid brain-boost claims: music training's effect on cognition is about zero once study quality is controlled ([Sala & Gobet 2020](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-020-01060-2)), and kindness supports being liked more, not being happier than other pleasant activities ([Layous](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0051380)). The evidence here is thinner than elsewhere. Search ran out before this part, no survey asking 9–12s what they want from parents' time could be verified, and forums could not be read, so a short owner-run parent survey would be cheap and valuable. The tree also had no tips past 96 months at the snapshot.

Screens guidance should present a range and offer an alternative. AAP's January 2026 statement replaces its earlier guidance, and its public summary gives no time limits ([HealthyChildren](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/creating-a-child-friendly-digital-world-AAP-releases-new-media-recommendations.aspx)). For ages 6–12, guidance runs from under 1 hour including schoolwork in Spain ([AEP](https://www.medicosypacientes.com/articulo/uso-pantallas-aep/)) to 1–2 hours excluding it in Sweden ([1177](https://www.1177.se/Vastmanland/liv--halsa/sunda-vanor/barn-och-skarmar/)), with no daily cap in Canada ([CPS](https://www.cps.ca/en/documents/position/digital-media)). The tree's guide already says this. The most actionable finding is the uncomfortable one: parents' phone use in front of under-5s links to poorer cognition and more behaviour problems, with small correlational effects ([Toledo-Vargas 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/article-abstract/2833506)), so write it as a two-way family rule, not blame. Australia's first ban report supports PlayNest's role: accounts fell from 52% to 42%, daily use barely moved, and time on sport, arts and family barely changed ([ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-31/most-australian-teens-still-on-social-media-after-ban/106983414)). Access rules do not supply something else to do, and PlayNest can. For first phones, offer readiness signs rather than an age, since AAP gives none ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/Tips-for-Parents-Digital-Age.aspx)) and researchers still disagree ([Odgers](https://candiceodgers.substack.com/p/no-i-was-not-paid-to-critique-the)). Add explicit AI lines: none for 5 and under, limited use alongside a parent at 6–12, and no emotional support or "friendship" under 18 ([Common Sense](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/articles/ai-chatbots-and-your-child-age-and-stage-guidance)), with voice toys named because parents may not count them as screens ([Common Sense](https://about.commonsensemedia.org/press-room/press-releases/common-sense-media-warns-against-ai-toy-companions-after-research-reveals-safety-risks)).

Stage and routine copy needs a few precise wording rules. Say "most children", not "75%", and treat the CDC age as "talk now if missing", never "normal if reached". CDC chose 75% to discourage waiting, while the authors state the lists were never tested for improving early identification ([Zubler 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9680195)) and ASHA objected ([ASHA](https://www.asha.org/practice/asha-comments-on-aap-and-cdc-developmental-milestones-updates)). Screening reminders must say "ask whether a check is due here", because USPSTF finds the evidence insufficient for universal autism screening ([USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/autism-spectrum-disorder-in-young-children-screening)), Canada recommends against population developmental screening ([CTFPHC](https://canadiantaskforce.ca/guidelines/published-guidelines/developmental-delay/)) and the UK does not screen for autism ([UK NSC](https://view-health-screening-recommendations.service.gov.uk/autism/)). Routines should use "more nights counts": across 13 countries, more routine nights a week went with better sleep in a dose-response pattern ([Mindell 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4402657/)), which is association only. Limit the visual-schedule claim to autistic children ([AFIRM](https://afirm.fpg.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/Visual-Supports-Brief-Packet.pdf)). Talk cards must stay original, because the Family Dinner Project bars derivative works ([terms](https://thefamilydinnerproject.org/terms-of-use/)). Printed visit summaries must carry observations, never scores or words like "behind".

## World content should credit living communities, which means fewer cards and more readers

The notes gathered 176 candidate games, and their weakness is sourcing. The MENA, Asia and Africa set has 4 entries graded high, 35 medium and 44 low, and about two-thirds of the East Asia, Americas, Europe and Pacific set rests on Wikipedia. Rules are often near-identical across places, while the chant, rhythm and humour are what differ. So write **one card per mechanic (drop-the-cloth, arch, five stones, mancala) with each community's own name and chant**, rather than a separate game per country or "the Iranian version of duck-duck-goose". Start with the strongest baby finds, such as Saudi *Battikh Battikh*, Persian *Tab Tab Abbasi* and Bengali *Agdum Bagdum*, because lap play is where PlayNest differs most from other apps. Claims about these should rest on general sources, because no peer-reviewed study of warabe uta, Korean baby moves or Kniereiter was found.

The framing matters as much as the rules, because adult-child play is not a universal norm. Mothers acted as playmates 47% of the time in a US sample, 24% in India and 7% in Guatemala, in a small-scale comparison ([Roopnarine & Davidson](https://www.museumofplay.org/app/uploads/2022/01/7-2-article-parent-child-play-across-cultures.pdf)). In many communities older siblings and peers are the main partners ([Vandermaas-Peeler](https://doi.org/10.9707/2307-0919.1054)), as Lancy describes among the Kpelle ([Lancy](https://www.davidlancy.org/s/Kpelle_Children_at_Play.pdf)), and deficit claims about the global South have been challenged directly ([Scheidecker et al.](https://www.davidlancy.org/s/Ethos-2023-Scheidecker-Poor-brain-development-in-the-global-South-Challenging-the-science-of-early-c.pdf)). So name a people and a place, say "played in" rather than "invented in", separate "traditionally" from "at home you could", and treat a wire car or rag doll as engineering rather than making do. Indigenous material needs permission as well as credit. Yulunga adaptations require written approval from the Australian Sports Commission and recommend involving Elders ([Yulunga](https://www.ausport.gov.au/yulunga)), and snow snake carries medicine and gender rules ([Oneida Indian Nation](https://www.oneidaindiannation.org/snow-snake-traditional-winter-game-of-the-haudenosaunee)). Existing items also need fixing. The tree still calls kendama "Japan's famous" toy, while Japan's own foreign ministry site says records point to a 16th-century French origin ([Kids Web Japan](https://web-japan.org/kidsweb/virtual/kendama/kendama02.html)). "Nalukataq-inspired blanket swing" should become a plain blanket swing with a separate note and an Iñupiat reviewer, though the note writer read no source on Nalukataq itself. "Mbube Mbube" should be checked against Solomon Linda's song ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_Sleeps_Tonight)). Ladushki and gonji-gonji do not repeat the disputed origin stories, and should stay that way.

Songs should favour repetition over breadth. An exploratory 10-week randomised trial of 110 families found more infant-directed singing improved infant mood, not caregiver mood ([Cho et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.14246)), familiar songs reduced distress most ([Cirelli & Trehub](https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0000917)), and a melody heard from a toy carried no social meaning, unlike the same melody from a parent ([Mehr et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797615626691)). So "plays once, then you sing" is the right design, and the product should nudge parents to a few favourites rather than a big library. The catalogue is the risk. Of 93 entries, 76 have a notated melody, about 37 have a firm public-domain basis, and **only 24 are melody-verified with a firmer basis and no sensitivity flag**, and those are thin on Africa, the Middle East and South Asia. The tree's 45 songs therefore outrun the firm list, and several rights fields read "Traditional, per Mama Lisa", which the notes call weak evidence. Lyrics were not captured in the notes at all, so each song's words need proofing against its source, with native-speaker and legal review. Some famous songs are not traditional: "Nani Teri Morni" is from a 1960 film ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoom_(1960_film))) and "San-toki" was written in 1928 ([Korean Wikipedia](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/산토끼_(동요))). Fixed-pitch tones cannot carry maqam, raga or pelog, so those should be labelled approximate or stay words-only.

Stories should launch small and gated. The notes hold 104 records with OPEN, CARE, ASK and NO stances, and the `Story` type should gain that stance as a required, validated field, because an expired copyright does not end a community's cultural rights. ASK and NO tales do not ship without review or a licence, and an open Gutenberg text is not permission. The tree's 23 tales already include ones the notes flag for care, such as the Enchanted Horse and the Wise People of Chelm, so each needs its stance recorded and a reader assigned. The notes' launch dozen includes Kasa Jizō, Sweet Porridge, The Gigantic Turnip, Stone Soup, Monkey and Crocodile, and Zāl and the Simorgh, all tellable from memory in under 500 words. Where softening reverses an ending, tell parents. On evidence, parent-training trials show expressive language gains of d = 0.41, low dose has minimal impact, and caregiver education is unrelated to outcome ([Dowdall 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/cdev.13225)), which favours a small daily habit and supports parents who do not read confidently. Screens can cost talk and warmth: parents made fewer dialogic comments with e-books ([Munzer](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2018-2012)), and warmth was lower on tablets though comprehension matched ([Yuill & Martin](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01951/full)). **No study covers PlayNest's actual case, a parent reading from a phone the child does not look at**, so the design choices are sensible practice rather than findings: story text only, a five-beat outline so the phone can go down, and two or three prompts at the end. A "find more books" list can start with [African Storybook](https://www.africanstorybook.org) (CC BY 4.0, 262 languages).

Festivals need three small changes. Dates should ship as a reviewed table, as the tree does, because `Intl`'s Chinese calendar gave wrong Lunar New Year dates for 2027 and 2030 against the [Hong Kong Observatory](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/gts/time/calendar/text/files/T2027e.txt), and Ramadan starts twice in 2030. Islamic dates need a visible range and a one-tap "It's today for us", because only sighting settles observance. The single "We mark this" toggle should grow into three states: Ours, Curious (browsable without rituals) and Hidden, with hidden as the default and no inference from language or locale. Community review should start with the entries families will meet first: Hanukkah, Yalda and Kwanzaa, if the app ships before December. Do not claim UNESCO status for Holi or Mid-Autumn, which the notes could not confirm.

## Inclusion should ask about the barrier, and language should follow reviewers

The notes' strongest inclusive idea is already half-built: ask what is in the way today, not what is wrong with the child. Chips such as "Can't hear it" or "Too much" can apply to a child, a grown-up or the room, stay session-only by default, and are never inferred, so nobody has to disclose anything. Adaptations must stay separate from easier and harder, because blending them tells families their child is the problem. The need is real: children with disabilities are 25% less likely to receive early stimulation and responsive care and 16% less likely to be read to ([WHO/UNICEF](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/B09617)). The content must stay modest. The largest review of autism interventions found adverse events poorly monitored and few popular programmes supported by the strongest evidence ([Sandbank 2023](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-076733)), so teach "notice, join, wait, respond" and never write "JASPER-style". Parent-implemented language work has real support, with effects that vary ([Roberts & Kaiser](https://doi.org/10.1044/1058-0360(2011/10-0055))). Harmless stimming needs no stopping ([NAS](https://www.autism.org.uk/advice-and-guidance/topics/behaviour/stimming/all-audiences)), and brain-training claims should stay out ([Simons 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100616661983), [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program)). Autistic adults strongly prefer identity-first language ([Taboas 2023](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613221130845)), so guides should say so and activity copy should name needs rather than labels. The toolkit's lines marked "X" in note 12 have no direct source. An occupational therapist, a teacher of the deaf, a teacher of the visually impaired and disabled adults and parents should read them, paid, before launch.

Languages are the largest remaining reach gain, and the choice should follow who can review. Bebbo's iOS build has no Arabic, Persian or Spanish ([listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bebbo-the-parenting-app/id1588918146)), and the Persian and Arabic products found mostly serve the child or lack play and traction, though that sample is thin. **Ahlan Simsim's remote programme gave gains comparable to a year of preschool regardless of caregiver literacy** ([NYU](https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/news/children-affected-syrian-refugee-crisis-are-effectively-learning-numbers-letters-and-emotional)), which supports audio-friendly delivery, though that programme used WhatsApp and home kits rather than an app like this. The notes have no sourced data on demand by language, so Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, Arabic, Indonesian and French are a hypothesis to check against births data and store statistics, with Persian added if there is a reviewer network. They also could not verify anything on Iran's market, PWA distribution or shutdowns. The groundwork is cheap and worth doing first: convert the 46 or so physical CSS lines my count found to logical properties, add a `?dir=rtl` e2e run, use `Intl` for week start and calendars, and precache a compact Arabic-script font only for those users. Have native writers write the stories and scripts from the facts, since machine translation would lose the voice and local tellings are not translations of the English.

## A sequence that protects trust first

The ranking below is my judgement, built on the notes' own rankings and on my reading of the working tree. No user research sits behind it.

The notes also agree on what not to build: streaks of any kind, a needy Pip, points or screen-time rewards for play, cloud AI chat now, a symptom quiz or colour triage, dose calculators, scored mood checks, voice commands in the web app, a weather API, community forums, a PDF library in the core bundle, decibel promises and an in-app "lock" that claims to hold a child inside.

The evidence has real limits. A shared search budget ran out partway through most notes, so later work relied on direct fetches, often through a summarising tool, and quotes need rechecking. Reddit could not be read, so parent voice comes from store reviews, one review analysis and Mumsnet. Raising Children, NSPCC, AAP journal pages and many others blocked access, and Raising Children is likely to matter most. Most non-English market evidence is store listings or company statements. No clinician, lawyer, native speaker or disabled reviewer has read any of this, and effort figures in the notes are estimates.

## Conclusion

The research changed what kind of problem this is. RESEARCH-2 asked what PlayNest could add to span 0–12, and the working tree shows that adding is now the cheap part: it gained a hundred activities in the four minutes between two of my counts. Verified trust is the scarce resource, and PlayNest should start measuring it. The useful number is no longer how many games, cards or songs exist. It is the share of sensitive records with a named reader and a date, the share of phone numbers called this quarter, and the number of real families observed.

That also suggests a positioning rivals will struggle to copy. They are converging on trackers and cloud chat behind accounts, while a private, offline app can show its working on every card: whose guidance this is, who read it, when it was last checked and how to correct it. For a parent at 3 a.m., and for a community whose song or story is on the screen, that visible accountability may do more than the next hundred activities. It is untested, and it is the bet I would test next.
