## What do the best-evidenced programmes teach, and what is the state of the evidence (2020-2026)?

### Takeaway

Behavioural parenting programmes have solid but no longer growing effects: they rest on warm child-led play, specific praise, clear instructions, calm follow-through and less harsh talk. The strongest recent signal is that **cutting harsh verbal discipline and actually following through** explain the benefit better than praise does. Gentle parenting has one descriptive study and no outcome trial, and Good Inside and Hand in Hand have no peer-reviewed outcome evidence that I could find.

### Cited Findings

#### Across programmes: recent syntheses

- Meta-analysis of 244 RCTs (1,100 effect sizes; 28,916 families; 36 countries): effects of behavioural parenting programmes fell early on and then levelled off. Newer trials had stronger methods, older samples, more girls, fewer sessions, more independent delivery and **less use of time-out**, but none of these explained the early decline. The authors say effects are no longer shrinking and show no sign of rising. Published online 18 Sep 2025; J Child Psychol Psychiatry 2026;67(1):127-137 — [VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/have-parenting-programs-for-disruptive-child-behavior-become-less/). The abstract page gives no pooled SMD by period; I could not open the full text.
- Individual-participant-data meta-analysis of 14 European RCTs (3,252 families, children aged 1-13): changes in parenting partly explained programme effects. **All measured mediators except praise were unique mediators**; reduced harsh verbal discipline and increased following through were the strongest. Most families benefited, partly through following through; families with the most and the least difficulties benefited less or not at all. JAACAP 2025;64(5):564-576. The accessible page gives no coefficients — [EM Consulte record](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1737034/individual-participant-data-meta-analysis-individu).
- Two meta-analyses (IPD: 13 Incredible Years trials, n=1,696, ages 2-11; trial-level: 156 trials, n=13,378) found **no evidence that earlier intervention works better**, and narrower age targeting did not help. Child Development 2019;90(1):7-19 — [Bangor record](https://research.bangor.ac.uk/cy/publications/the-earlier-the-better-individual-participant-data-and-traditiona/). This is outside your 2020-2026 window but is the main source on age.
- WHO published parenting-intervention guidelines for ages 0-17 on 10 Feb 2023. The landing page does not list the recommendations or certainty ratings, and I did not read the PDF — [WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240065505).

#### Parent-Child Interaction Therapy (PCIT)

- Ages 2-7; highest CEBC rating (1) in its disruptive-behaviour and parent-training topic areas. Two phases: child-directed interaction (follow the lead, labelled praise, reflect, no commands, questions or criticism; daily 5-minute home practice) and parent-directed interaction (clear direct commands, praise for compliance, structured time-out for non-compliance). Parents move to the second phase only after mastering the first. Therapists coach live — [CEBC](https://www.cebc4cw.org/program/parent-child-interaction-therapy/). CEBC's review date was not in the portion read.
- PCIT International describes the same two phases and ages — [PCIT](http://www.pcit.org/about).
- **PRIDE acronym:** the pages I could open do not spell it out. CDC's child-led play page teaches the same five actions (praise specific behaviour, imitate, describe, show enthusiasm, reflect) and the same three avoids (questions, directions, criticism) under its own wording — [CDC child-led play](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/child-led-play.html). Use CDC as the citable source.
- **Time-out steps (as taught with a therapist):** a private practice citing the PCIT Protocol describes: for non-compliance, wait 5 seconds, warn, wait 5 seconds, then take the child to the chair; sturdy adult-size chair facing a wall; 3 minutes of quiet with no attention; if the child leaves, return them, with a backup of 1 minute in a safe, toy-free room or loss of a privilege; afterwards the child does the original command and gets prompt praise. It stresses learning this from a trained professional — [Manhattan Psychology Group](https://www.manhattanpsychologygroup.com/blog/time-out/).
- A UC Davis PCIT handout gives an "automatic time-out" for broken house rules such as aggression or destruction, with no warning once the rule has been taught: explain the rule, label the positive opposite, state the reason, take the child straight to the chair, 3 minutes plus 5 seconds of quiet, then a corrective action, thanks, and attention to positive behaviour — [UC Davis, Urquiza et al. 2011](https://pcit.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/62_automatic.timeout.pdf).
- PCIT International's task force statement says parents deliver time-out with therapist support, and the backup space is brief (1 minute with 5 seconds of quiet) and needed less once the child learns to sit **[search snippet; PDF 404 on direct fetch]** — [PCIT statement](https://www.pcit.org:443/uploads/6/3/6/1/63612365/pcit_international_task_force_position_statement_on_the_use_of_time_out_3.6.18.pdf).
- Older children: an adaptation covers ages 7-10, keeps time-out for suitable children and adds privilege-loss procedures. Standard PCIT has been used up to 12 with mixed results, and the research base for older children is limited — [WVU PCIT lab](https://sites.google.com/view/wvupcitlab/pcit-resources/pcit-for-older-children).

#### Triple P

- Five principles: safe engaging environment, positive learning environment, assertive discipline, realistic expectations, parental self-care. Seventeen strategies. A state summary gives recommended ages: ground rules 3-12, directed discussion 3-12, planned ignoring 1-7, clear calm instructions 2-12, logical consequences 2-12, quiet time 18 months-10 years, time-out 2-10 — [North Carolina Triple P summary](https://ncdhhs.gov/media/26017/open) (secondary source).
- Triple P's 2024 tantrum article: stay calm and pause; hold your decision; acknowledge feeling and reason in few words; no reasoning mid-tantrum; teach feelings in calm moments; plan ahead; warn before transitions; praise specifically; limit devices as calming tools — [Triple P, 8 Apr 2024](https://www.triplep-parenting.net.au/au/articles-and-news/post/how-to-handle-tantrums-try-these-positive-parenting-tips/).
- 2021 meta-analysis (37 studies, 2013-2020, 3,691 families): child behaviour problem count SMD -0.467 (CI -0.631, -0.303), intensity -0.378 (-0.501, -0.255), emotional problems -0.254 (-0.476, -0.031), social competence 0.274 (0.025, 0.523), parenting style -0.460, parenting confidence 0.419, parent-child relationship -0.714, marital satisfaction not significant. Total behaviour effect -1.380 had I²=97.3%. Level 4 was most effective; community-only delivery was not significant for behaviour; publication bias on some outcomes — [Li, Peng & Li 2021, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.709851/full).
- Independence critique: a 2012 review said most papers relied on maternal report and the paternal-report effect was 0.42 (CI -0.02, 0.87) **[search snippet]** — [Wilson et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3532197/). Earlier meta-analyses found small-to-moderate (2008) or moderate-to-large parent-reported (2007) effects **[search snippet]** — [Nowak & Heinrichs](https://thim.mijn.bsl.nl/a-comprehensive-meta-analysis-of-triple-p-positive-parenting-pro/524616); [Thomas & Zimmer-Gembeck](https://mijn.bsl.nl/behavioral-outcomes-of-parent-child-interaction-therapy-and-trip/537252).

#### Incredible Years (IY)

- Parent programmes by age: Toddler 1-3 (13-14 sessions), Preschool 3-6 (14-20), School Age 6-12 (12-16, including homework support and after-school monitoring) — [IY](https://www.incredibleyears.com/early-intervention-programs/parents).
- Blueprints rates IY Parent "Promising" and lists child-directed play, praise and incentives, clear limits and non-physical discipline as core. Of 42 studies reviewed, only one met its standards (Gross 2003, 208 parents; parenting-outcome effect sizes .30-.40), and several non-certified studies showed mixed or null child-behaviour effects — [Blueprints](https://www.blueprintsprograms.org/programs/29999999/incredible-years-parent/print/).
- **Conflict:** IY's own research page lists CEBC as "Well-Supported" and EIF Level 4+ for Preschool Basic — [IY research](https://www.incredibleyears.com/research). The CEBC URL it links showed a classroom-delivered child programme for ages 3-8 (apparently Dinosaur School) at rating 3 when fetched, not the parent programme — [CEBC](https://www.cebc4cw.org/program/the-incredible-years/). I could not reach EIF (522). Treat IY's self-reported ratings with care.

#### Parent Management Training / Kazdin Method

- Core idea on Kazdin's site: talking and explaining are weak ways to change behaviour; define a "positive opposite" and reinforce it consistently until it is the default. Praise can improve or worsen behaviour depending on quality, timing and delivery. The site cites no studies on the pages I read — [Kazdin](https://alankazdin.com/); [praise article](https://alankazdin.com/praise-is-one-of-the-strongest-ways-to-influence-your-childs-actions/). A free course ("Everyday Parenting: The ABCs of Child Rearing") is linked from the same site.
- Child Mind gives a citable ABC summary: antecedents to fix (instructions across the room, no warning, several instructions at once), specific behaviour definitions, and consequences (labelled praise, active ignoring for minor behaviour only, reward menus, brief immediate time-outs) — [Child Mind, 7 Nov 2025](https://childmind.org/guide/parents-guide-to-problem-behavior/).

#### Tuning in to Kids and Gottman emotion coaching

- Gottman's five steps: be aware of the emotion, see it as a moment for closeness and teaching, listen with empathy and validate, help label it, set limits while solving problems — [Gottman, updated 16 Jan 2026](https://www.gottman.com/blog/an-introduction-to-emotion-coaching/).
- Tuning in to Kids teaches five similar steps: notice, connect, empathise, name, then help solve or set limits. RCT of the online version (TIKOL; 151 analysed parents of 4-10-year-olds; about 9 months post-baseline): emotion-coaching behaviours d=0.41, dismissing beliefs d=-0.64 and behaviours d=-0.57, parent-reported child anxiety d=-0.44. **Child behaviour problems were not significantly different between groups** (d=-0.09, -0.22). Parent report only; high-income sample — [Havighurst et al. 2024, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1393708/full).
- Earlier TIK RCTs in 4-5-year-olds found more coaching and less dismissing **[search snippet]** — [2009 trial](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/316708-tuning-in-to-kids--an-emotion-focused-parenting-program-initial-findings-from-a-community-trial).
- Programme-reported reach: over 650,000 parents, no specific research on autistic families — [TIK](https://tuningintokids.org.au/).

#### CDC Essentials for Parenting

- Covers ages 2-4; all pages reviewed 30 Apr 2026. Modules: emotion coaching, child-led play, giving directions, rules and routines, praise and rewards, ignoring, time-out, time-in. Details are used throughout this pack — [CDC index](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/site.html).
- CDC's five steps for misbehaviour: name the behaviour, warn with if-then (only if you will follow through), apply a consequence straight away, say why briefly, return to positive — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/index.html).

#### WHO/UNICEF Parenting for Lifelong Health (PLH) and COVID tips

- The six tip topics were: one-on-one time, staying positive, daily routine, avoiding bad behaviour, managing stress, talking about COVID. Produced by PLH and the Accelerate Hub with WHO, UNICEF and others — [UCT](https://news.uct.ac.za/article/-2020-03-26-parenting-out-of-school-children-during-covid-19). **I could not open the sheets themselves.** A summary of the one-on-one sheet says: regular time with each child, free and playful, phones and TV off, to help children feel safe and loved — [ForChildren](https://www.forchildren.com/discover/relationships/covid-19-parenting-one-on-one-time-eng).
- PLH reports 22 RCTs (self-reported) and work in 15 countries including Philippines, Thailand, South Africa, China, Malaysia, Kuwait and Palestine — [PLH](https://parentingforlifelonghealth.org/). Its 2026 Tanzania cluster RCT of ParentApp for Teens (2,396 adolescents aged 10-17, 14 months) reports less sexual violence against girls (67%) and less maltreatment, at about US$6 per family — [PLH impact](https://wordpress.plhdashboard.org/impact). This is programme-reported and mostly older than your target band.

#### Collaborative & Proactive Solutions (Greene)

- Premise: "kids do well if they can"; behaviour signals difficulty meeting an expectation, and skills lag. Greene explicitly rejects labels such as attention-seeking and limit-testing and lists "take a break" and calming corners among late, reactive strategies. Plan A is adult-imposed, Plan B collaborative, Plan C sets the expectation aside for now — [Greene slides, 17 Nov 2022](https://www.actcommunity.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Ross-Greene-2022-Vancouver-PDA-2slides.pdf).
- Plan B has three steps, done proactively. Empathy: open with what you've noticed plus a gentle "what's up?". Adult concerns: "my concern is...". Invitation: "I wonder if there's a way..." with both concerns, child suggests first, solution must be realistic and mutually satisfactory — [same slides](https://www.actcommunity.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Ross-Greene-2022-Vancouver-PDA-2slides.pdf).
- Evidence: I could not open the RCT literature. A review (Greene & Winkler 2019) and a 2004 ODD study are cited elsewhere, and limited evidence on mechanisms is noted **[search snippet]** — [Texas school mental health summary](https://schoolmentalhealthtx.org/?p=1552).
- **Conflict:** CDC and AAP use attention and ignoring language that Greene rejects. Slides are a practitioner source, not a trial report.

#### SPACE (Lebowitz)

- Parent-only: respond more supportively and reduce accommodation of the child's anxiety. It targets separation, social and generalised anxiety, phobias, panic, selective mutism, OCD and ARFID — [SPACE](https://www.spacetreatment.net/).
- RCT of 124 children aged 7-14 with anxiety disorders, 12 weekly sessions, SPACE versus child CBT: noninferior; about 60% in both arms no longer met criteria; significant improvement 87.5% versus 75.5%. JAACAP 2020;59(3) — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/616040); [Yale News](https://news.yale.edu/2019/03/12/new-way-combat-childhood-anxiety-treat-parents).
- Supportive statement form: acknowledge the fear and state confidence the child can cope. The programme also prepares parents for strong reactions when accommodation falls, including explosive behaviour or threats of self-harm — [CHADD, June 2018](https://chadd.org/attention-article/space-a-parent-based-intervention-for-reducing-childhood-anxiety/). It is therapist-led over 10-12 sessions.

#### Hand in Hand and Good Inside (market reference, not peer-reviewed)

- Hand in Hand's Special Time: adult sets time, length (one author used 10-20 minutes with a timer) and place; child chooses; adult follows; stay close if feelings come up when it ends — [Hand in Hand](https://www.handinhandparenting.org/2017/04/special-time-heals-fears/). A morning version is a parent testimonial — [Hand in Hand](https://www.handinhandparenting.org/2023/07/a-dose-of-special-time-in-the-morning/). A search found no controlled trial of Special Time or Playlistening **[search result]**. I could not verify a primary description of Staylistening.
- Good Inside: app from pregnancy to 18, "exact scripts", an AI chatbot (GiGi), a five-step method (Chaos, Understand, Sturdy moment, Repair, Reset). It calls itself "sturdy parenting", not gentle, and "science-backed" while citing no studies — [Good Inside](https://www.goodinside.com/). Testimonials are anonymised. **Non-peer-reviewed; market reference only.**

#### "Gentle parenting"

- The first empirical study (100 US parents of 2-7-year-olds, 49 self-identified gentle parents; mostly White and degree-educated): boundaries featured in about 28% of gentle parents' misbehaviour responses, with enforcement varying (about 37% clear parent-set rules, 40% child-directed). About 35% of responses contained self-critique, and self-critical gentle parents reported lower efficacy (3.96 vs 4.44, p=.01). **No child outcomes, no observation, cross-sectional** — [Pezalla & Davidson 2024, PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0307492).
- I found no RCT of gentle parenting as a package **[search result]**. Commentary says the component principles have indirect support **[search snippet]** — [ParentData](https://parentdata.org/ask/is-gentle-parenting-evidence-based-does-it-actually-work).

#### 2024-2026 evidence on time-out, spanking, praise, consequences

- **Time-out:** Survey of 474 Australian caregivers of 6-8-year-olds (fieldwork 2020): 72.4% had used time-out; appropriate implementation was associated with fewer internalising symptoms (B=-0.263) and less insecure attachment (B=-0.098). Externalising association lost significance after covariates. Cross-sectional, parent-report; two authors earn royalties from a programme with time-out; some reported intervals look erroneous, and the "high-adversity children benefit most" claim rests on a borderline interaction. Inappropriate use was common: 49.8% used locations with distractions, 33.1% used it when anxious — [Roach et al., BJPsych 2025;227(2):538-544, online 5 Dec 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/979BF190B7E433C6BEFFA25A2245BABF/core-reader).
- Older but widely cited: parent-reported time-out use at 36 months was not linked to later anxiety, depression, aggression, rule-breaking or self-control through fifth grade in Early Head Start data (Knight, J Dev Behav Pediatr 2019) — [ScienceDaily, 12 Sep 2019](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/09/190912124828.htm).
- **Spanking / corporal punishment:** WHO report (20 Aug 2025; press coverage calls it WHO's first on the topic): about 1.2 billion children aged 0-18 punished at home yearly; past-month rates for ages 2-14 from 30% (Kazakhstan) to 77% (Togo); in 49 LMICs exposed children about 24% less likely to be developmentally on track; laws alone are not enough — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/20-08-2025-new-report-demonstrates-that-corporal-punishment-harms-children-s-health). WHO fact sheet dated 11 May 2026 adds that ages 2-4 are as likely, sometimes more likely, to be exposed — [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/corporal-punishment-and-health).
- **Praise:** not a unique mediator in the 2025 IPD analysis (above). CDC and others still recommend labelled praise. See "phrasing" below.
- **Consequences:** increased following through was among the strongest mediators (above).

### Inferences

- The app can honestly say its advice draws on the shared core of proven programmes. It should not imply any single 3-step card has been tested. No trial tests a mid-meltdown phone helper, and most programme effects come from multi-week delivery with practice and coaching.
- The IPD result suggests every card should stress calm follow-through and lower harshness, not only praise. Scripts that are warm but never hold a limit fit the weakest part of the evidence.
- Effects shrink in rigorous trials, so avoid "proven to work" marketing.

### Gaps

- 2020-2026 pooled effect sizes for PCIT and IY, and any Cochrane review, could not be opened. **Leads to verify when search is available:** Valero-Aguayo 2021 PCIT meta-analysis; Leijten et al. 2019 JAACAP on key components; Kaminski et al. 2008 J Abnorm Child Psychol on components (Springer redirect blocked); Ollendick et al. 2016 CPS vs PMT RCT; Heilmann et al. 2021 Lancet (403); Gershoff & Grogan-Kaylor 2016; Dadds & Tully 2019 American Psychologist; Cuartas et al. on LMIC corporal punishment.
- Search results mentioned a 2024 European young-adult time-out study and an American Psychologist piece; neither was verified.
- Triple P's official printed tip sheets were not accessed.
- Full WHO guideline recommendations and GRADE ratings were not read.

## What do AAP and other national bodies say?

### Takeaway

Every body reached rejects physical punishment and shaming and recommends warm, consistent limits. They disagree on the minimum age for time-out (AAP and CDC examples start at 2; CPS says not under 3), and I found no update to the AAP's 2018 statement.

### Cited Findings

- **AAP 2018 "Effective Discipline to Raise Healthy Children"**: released 5 Nov 2018, December 2018 Pediatrics. Calls for an end to spanking and addresses shaming and humiliation; cites children spanked more than twice a month at 3 being more aggressive at 5, with lingering effects at 9. Recommends rewarding positive behaviour, clear rules in advance, consistent follow-through, limit setting, redirection, setting expectations; paediatricians should counsel and refer — [AAP summary](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/AAP-Updates-Corporal-Punishment-Policy.aspx). The journal page itself returned 403.
- **AAP update status:** a search found only 2018 coverage and no reaffirmation or replacement **[search result]**; AAP's stance page is still dated 5 Nov 2018 — [AAP Where We Stand](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Where-We-Stand-Spanking.aspx). Status of a formal reaffirmation is **unverified**.
- AAP's stance also rejects threatening, insulting, humiliating and shaming; physical punishment raises injury risk under 18 months — [same page](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Where-We-Stand-Spanking.aspx).
- AAP's 10 strategies: show and tell, set limits, consequences, hear them out, give attention, catch them being good, know when not to respond, prepare for trouble, redirect, time-out. Age notes: toddlers, praise and ignore where safe, avoid taking sides, short time-outs; 3-5, chores, choices, anger is okay but hurting isn't; 5-12, discuss choices and consequences, state rules, balance privileges and responsibility — [AAP, 5 Nov 2018](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Disciplining-Your-Child.aspx).
- **CDC:** as above; reviewed 30 Apr 2026. CDC says avoid spanking, yelling or embarrassing — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/index.html).
- **NHS:** consistency across carers, stay calm, praise, smacking has no lasting benefit — [NHS behaviour, reviewed 3 Apr 2023, review due 3 Apr 2026](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/dealing-with-child-behaviour-problems/). Tantrums page reviewed 7 Nov 2022, due 7 Nov 2025 — [NHS tantrums](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/). **Several NHS pages cited here are past their stated review date; recheck.**
- **Canadian Paediatric Society:** time-outs should not be used under 3, are a later option after other methods, should be short and in a safe quiet place; time-in is an alternative; physical punishment and shaming should never be used — [CPS, Jan 2020](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/positive-discipline-for-young-children). The separate CPS position statement URL returned 404.
- **Germany:** BZgA's site now carries the name Bundesinstitut für Öffentliche Gesundheit (BIÖG). Its advice: warmth, understandable and consistent rules, never criticise the child as a person, do not slap or insult, and be willing to apologise — [BIÖG, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.kindergesundheit-info.de/themen/entwicklung/psychische-gesundheit/erziehung/). On the emerging will from the second year: routines, praise, calm understanding, and a clear yes and a friendly but firm no — [BIÖG, 8 Feb 2026](https://www.kindergesundheit-info.de/themen/entwicklung/entwicklungsschritte/geistige-entwicklung/ich-entwicklung/). The dedicated Trotzphase page was not found, and German law was not stated on those pages.
- **WHO:** see above. Recommends enforcing bans alongside norm change and parent support — [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/corporal-punishment-and-health).
- **UNICEF Parenting:** distress signs by age, belly breathing with a child, and when to seek specialist support (undated) — [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/how-to-recognize-signs-of-distress). Bullying guidance also undated — [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/bullying).
- **Raising Children Network, NSPCC:** returned 403, so **no claims are made from them**.

### Inferences

- For a global app, the conservative rule on the youngest time-out age is the safer default. See the age-band section.

### Gaps

- CPS's 2004 position statement, NSPCC's positive-parenting guidance, Raising Children's situation pages, and German law were not read. Raising Children would likely fill gaps on car seats, biting and whining if a human can open it.

## For each situation: drivers by age, what to do now, what to avoid, wording, follow-up, when to get help

### Takeaway

Most situations have good authority-level guidance, and the same pattern recurs: make it safe, regulate yourself, name the feeling briefly, hold the limit or offer a choice, teach later when calm. Direct evidence for specific situations is thin. Some cards have **no dedicated verified source** (car-seat refusal, hair wash, "I hate you", backtalk, restraint collapse, interrupting), and these are flagged.

### Cited Findings

Each card lists a **Strength** tag. **A** = principle taken from RCT-tested programme content. **B** = named authority guidance on this situation. **C** = authority guidance on a neighbouring topic, applied by me. **D** = no dedicated verified source.

#### 1. Tantrum in public (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Start around 18 months, far less common by 4 ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/)); most common 1-3, tied to needs children cannot express, hunger and tiredness ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)). Ages 2-4 have limited language and get overwhelmed quickly ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/understanding-the-early-years/index.html)). School-age: meltdowns past the typical age are often a sign of distress ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)).
- **Now:** Ignore the stares and stay calm ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/)). If near danger such as a street, hold firmly ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)). Move somewhere more private if you can and sit with them ([CDC time-in](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-in.html)); a bathroom stall or going home are the fallback ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-out-challenges.html)). Say the feeling and the "no" in a few words, then stop talking ([Triple P](https://www.triplep-parenting.net.au/au/articles-and-news/post/how-to-handle-tantrums-try-these-positive-parenting-tips/)).
- **Avoid:** Giving in or bribing with treats ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/); [CDC candy example](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)); shouting back; reasoning mid-meltdown ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Something seems to be upsetting you ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html)). I know this is hard, and the answer is still no ([AAP pattern](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Short trips with a job for the child ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/)); snacks and protected naps ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)); after calming, praise settling and offer a cuddle ([Sheffield handout](https://www.st-marys.sheffield.sch.uk/assets/Positive-Parenting-Tantrums.pdf)). Play: feeling faces; stop-go games.
- **Help:** Serious concern, talk to health visitor or GP ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/)); meltdowns beyond the typical age, or patterns suggesting trauma, anxiety, ADHD or learning problems ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)).

#### 2. Tantrum at home (Strength A/B)
- **Drivers:** As above; temperament matters ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)). Triggers include bedtime, homework, ending play ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)).
- **Now:** Check safety, calm yourself, then choose a path. Distressed or overwhelmed: time-in, sit close, name the feeling, ask before touching ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-in.html)). Not dangerous and aimed at getting something: let it run and withdraw attention ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx); [Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)). You may move to another room if the child cannot get hurt ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/)).
- **Avoid:** Yes/no questions ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)); changing the limit midstream ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)); starting a coaching talk during distress ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html)).
- **Conflict:** CDC says ignoring is not for genuine distress ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)), while Child Mind advises ignoring non-dangerous outbursts ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)). CDC itself suggests time-out for a tantrum with screaming and kicking on one page and not using it when very upset on another ([time-in](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-in.html); [time-out](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-out.html)). NHS notes some parents hold the child firmly when the child seems more upset than angry ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** You've gone quiet, and it looks like you're ready to play again ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx)). It's okay to feel cross, and blocks don't get thrown ([CDC pattern](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html)).
- **Later/play:** Talk once calm ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)); colour-zone chart ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/play-ideas-to-help-your-child-manage-emotions.aspx)); child-led play 5-15 minutes daily ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/child-led-play.html)).
- **Help:** As #1. Also frequent outbursts that injure or scare ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Aggressive-Behavior.aspx)).

#### 3. Hitting (Strength A/B)
- **Drivers:** Under 12 months, exploring. 12-24 months, aggression often peaks around 2 with big feelings and few words. 24-36 months, they may know the rule but impulses win ([ZERO TO THREE, 30 Apr 2025](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). Until about 3 children often don't understand punishment ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Aggressive-Behavior.aspx)). Preschool: more aware of others' feelings ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx)). School-age: consider anger, exposure to violence at home, being bullied ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Understanding-Violent-Behavior-In-Children-and-Adolescents-055.aspx)).
- **Now:** Stop it straight away ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)), at eye level, firm but gentle, with a stop gesture, guiding hands if needed; name the feeling; offer an outlet such as stomping or jumping ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). Separate children who are fighting until calm ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Aggressive-Behavior.aspx)). Time-out is an option for physical aggression at the right age ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-in.html)); AAP says use it sparingly, as a last resort, for young children ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Aggressive-Behavior.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Ignoring it; big emotional reactions, punishment or shaming; demanding an apology a toddler can't understand ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/)); hitting to teach ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Where-We-Stand-Spanking.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** You're really angry, and hitting hurts. Stomp your feet instead ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). It's fine to be angry; I will stop you from hitting ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/)).
- **Conflict:** CDC prefers stating the wanted behaviour rather than "no hitting" for reward targets ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/using-rewards.html)), while ZERO TO THREE and CSEFEL use a short "no" for hurting. CDC's rule format states both: the unacceptable act, then the behaviour wanted ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/structure-rules/rules.html)).
- **Later/play:** Practise words such as asking for a toy back ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)); praise gentle hands; turn-taking games and timers ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/)).
- **Help:** See red-flag table: injuries, attacking adults, being barred from play, aggression for weeks ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Aggressive-Behavior.aspx)); seeming fearless, no apparent reason, aggressive pretend themes, after a trauma ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)).

#### 4. Biting (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Frustration, overwhelm in play, seeing the reaction, exploring by mouth, oral need, teething, copying, and hunger, tiredness, boredom or anxiety ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/biting-parenting_tool.pdf)).
- **Now:** (1) Calmly remove the child; say stop, no biting, biting hurts; show the effect. (2) Give most attention to the bitten child; even negative attention can encourage biting. (3) Name the biter's feeling and give another way. (4) Teach words later ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/biting-parenting_tool.pdf)).
- **Avoid:** Biting back, which CSEFEL says confuses, frightens and can be considered a form of abuse; yelling, lecturing, physical punishment; pushing for an apology, which shifts attention to the biter ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/biting-parenting_tool.pdf)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** You're frustrated; you want that toy. Touch gently or ask for it. Later: next time say "stop" or get a grown-up ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/biting-parenting_tool.pdf)).
- **Later/play:** Observe patterns; stay within arm's reach during play; crunchy snacks or a teething toy; earlier naps by 10-15 minutes; role-play "stop" and "help"; share the plan with caregivers ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/biting-parenting_tool.pdf)).
- **Help:** Biting several times a week ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)) or not decreasing over time ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/biting-parenting_tool.pdf)). First aid for broken skin was not verified.

#### 5. Throwing things (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Frustration at a task; a child who screams and throws a puzzle lacks words for feelings ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_emotions.pdf)). Toddlers also love physical challenges ([Harvard](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-18-to-36-month-olds.pdf)).
- **Now:** CDC's sequence: name the problem and safety, warn with if-then, put the toy away if repeated, say why briefly, then point to how it can return ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/index.html)). Toys can go in time-out instead of the child ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-out.html)). Don't ignore throwing ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Warnings you won't carry out ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/index.html)); long lectures.
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Throwing isn't safe. If the car flies again, it goes away for tonight. You can have it back when it stays on the track ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/index.html)).
- **Later/play:** Ball-throwing with simple rules ([Harvard](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-18-to-36-month-olds.pdf)); teach "say it, don't do it" ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_emotions.pdf)).
- **Help:** Often breaks things or invades space ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)).

#### 6. Sibling fight (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Fairness and jealousy. A toy squabble may really be about feeling left out ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)). Involvement should reduce with age; toddlers need simple repeated rules ([AAP, 6 Apr 2026](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/sibling-synergy.aspx)).
- **Now:** Physical fight: separate until calm ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Aggressive-Behavior.aspx)), in separate spots if using time-out ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-out.html)); remove a fought-over toy ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/index.html)). Minor dispute: let them work it out; if they can't, step in as a calm, neutral party and hear both sides ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/sibling-synergy.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Taking sides ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Disciplining-Your-Child.aspx)); always catering to the younger child ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/sibling-synergy.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** It sounds like you felt sad when your brother hit you and said mean things ([CDC reflective listening](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/index.html)). For older kids: are your feelings red, yellow or green? We solve this at green ([Child Mind stoplight](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)).
- **Later/play:** Turn timers the children set themselves, silly turn-taking drawings, cooperative board games, kids-versus-grown-ups games ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/helping-siblings-play-together-peacefully.aspx)); one-on-one time with each child ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/sibling-synergy.aspx)).
- **Help:** Concern for physical or emotional wellbeing ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/sibling-synergy.aspx)); intense rivalry should not be tolerated ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Normal-Child-Behavior.aspx)). Explicit referral thresholds for sibling violence were not found.

#### 7. Sibling jealousy / new baby (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Toddlers 1-2 need reassurance; 2-4 may feel they compete for attention; 5+ less threatened but may resent attention ([AAP, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/prenatal/Pages/Preparing-Your-Family-for-a-New-Baby.aspx)). Regression is common, and a new sibling can trigger toilet setbacks ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Regression.aspx)).
- **Now:** Give the attention needed and praise grown-up moments rather than telling them to act their age ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/prenatal/Pages/Preparing-Your-Family-for-a-New-Baby.aspx)). Keep the baby safe: stay close, stop rough handling as in #3.
- **Avoid:** Telling a child to act their age; letting all attention go to the baby ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/prenatal/Pages/Preparing-Your-Family-for-a-New-Baby.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Sometimes the baby needs me first, and then it's your turn. I love you just as much ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/prenatal/Pages/Preparing-Your-Family-for-a-New-Baby.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Daily one-on-one time; a doll to care for; helper jobs; older children hold the baby after asking and get praise for gentleness ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/prenatal/Pages/Preparing-Your-Family-for-a-New-Baby.aspx)).
- **Help:** Regression lasting a month or more or with no clear cause ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Regression.aspx)).

#### 8. Won't share (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** At 2 they may refuse to share interesting things and play poorly side by side ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/social-development-2-year-olds.aspx)). Around 3 they learn turns, often needing encouragement; by 4 explanations start to mean something ([AAP, 5 Dec 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx)).
- **Now:** Name the other child's feeling and ask for the toy back at 2 ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/social-development-2-year-olds.aspx)). Older: remind that each gets an equal turn; suggest drawing for first turn or switching activity ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Expecting more self-control than a 2-3-year-old has ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** That's my doll, please give it back, as words to teach ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). Use your words ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Child-chosen turn timer ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/helping-siblings-play-together-peacefully.aspx)); point out adults sharing ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx)); small playgroups of 2-3 ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/social-development-2-year-olds.aspx)).
- **Help:** No dedicated threshold; see friendship and aggression cards. Evidence on forced sharing was not verified.

#### 9. Whining (Strength A/B)
- **Drivers:** Common in the second year ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Emotional-Development-1-Year-Olds.aspx)). Persists if it has worked to delay transitions ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/why-do-kids-have-trouble-with-transitions/)). First rule out hunger, tiredness, sickness ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)).
- **Now:** Pick the behaviour, withdraw attention (no talk, touch or looks, keep them safe), and return attention the moment it stops, with praise. Expect it to get worse at first ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)).
- **Avoid:** Time-out for whining ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-out.html)); giving in during the worse-first phase ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)); ignoring without positive attention at other times ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** I love how you asked for help with your inside voice ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)).
- **Later/play:** About 5 minutes of play to fill the "attention meter" ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx)); all caregivers respond alike ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)).
- **Help:** A 2-year-old highly demanding and rarely satisfied, or persistently sad ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/emotional-development-2-year-olds.aspx)).

#### 10. Constant "no" / defiance (Strength A/B)
- **Drivers:** Under 18 months, impulse, not defiance; 18-36 months, separateness and control, "no" is typical at 2-3 ([ZERO TO THREE, 4 May 2025](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)). Own will from the second year ([BIÖG](https://www.kindergesundheit-info.de/themen/entwicklung/entwicklungsschritte/geistige-entwicklung/ich-entwicklung/)). 5-12: occasional defiance is normal; persistent patterns may reflect expectations, temperament, school, family stress ([AAP, 2009](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/The-Disobedient-Child.aspx)). After big changes kids may push back on what they control ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/defiant-behavior-how-play-can-help-children-cope-with-change.aspx)).
- **Now:** Acknowledge, then a short clear limit, then two acceptable choices; be clear what is not a choice. Ignore the protest; hurting gets a calm stop ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)). Pick battles ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Skipping empathy; adding "okay?" to directions; giving in; changing limits midstream ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)); overusing "no" ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/positive-discipline-for-young-children)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** A clear yes and a friendly, firm no ([BIÖG](https://www.kindergesundheit-info.de/themen/entwicklung/entwicklungsschritte/geistige-entwicklung/ich-entwicklung/)). These pyjamas or those? ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)). For 8-12, outside the moment: I've noticed mornings are hard, what's up? My concern is being late. I wonder if there's a way to sort both? ([Greene](https://www.actcommunity.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Ross-Greene-2022-Vancouver-PDA-2slides.pdf)).
- **Later/play:** Follow the child's lead daily; no-winner back-and-forth games; dance party with turns ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/defiant-behavior-how-play-can-help-children-cope-with-change.aspx)).
- **Help:** Interferes with friendships, learning or the relationship ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)). Ongoing pattern disrupting family or school: evaluation, check for ADHD, anxiety, mood or learning problems ([AACAP ODD, Jan 2019](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-with-Oppositional-Defiant-Disorder-072.aspx)).

#### 11. Won't get dressed (Strength B/C)
- **Drivers:** Seeking control ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)); not understanding the request, or needing a warning ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_cooperate.pdf)). Children of 4 can often dress themselves ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_cooperate.pdf)).
- **Now:** Get close and at eye level, give one direction, offer two options, wait, warn once ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/giving-directions.html)). Slow starter: I'll do one, you do one. Unenthusiastic: beat the clock ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_cooperate.pdf)).
- **Avoid:** Questions that invite refusal ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/giving-directions.html)); doing the task for them, which can reinforce dawdling ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/guide/parents-guide-to-problem-behavior/)); expecting instant results ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/play-and-learning/teaching-your-child-everyday-skills/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** It's time to go to the shop, so shoes on. Grey trousers or blue? ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/giving-directions.html)).
- **Later/play:** Lay clothes out the night before; give the sequence step by step ([AAP, 2009](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Growing-Independence-Tips-for-Parents-of-Young-Children.aspx)); dressing-up play from 24 months ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/play-and-learning/baby-and-toddler-play-ideas/)).
- **Help:** No dedicated threshold; if new loss of skills, see regression table.

#### 12. Won't leave the park (transitions) (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Moving from a preferred to a required activity; learned if protest has delayed things; harder with ADHD, autism, sensory issues or anxiety ([Child Mind, 18 Feb 2026](https://childmind.org/article/why-do-kids-have-trouble-with-transitions/)).
- **Now:** Warn in advance, then remind when it's time ([Triple P](https://www.triplep-parenting.net.au/au/articles-and-news/post/how-to-handle-tantrums-try-these-positive-parenting-tips/)); staged countdowns ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-can-we-help-kids-with-transitions/)); a final 3 more minutes with a timer, then go ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_cooperate.pdf)). Give a choice about the next thing ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). If refusing, gently guide or carry a younger child ([AAP pattern for screens](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Switching without warning ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/guide/parents-guide-to-problem-behavior/)); negotiating after the limit is set ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** I see you want to keep playing. Three more minutes on my timer, then we go. Which book shall we read before we leave? ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_cooperate.pdf); [ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)).
- **Later/play:** Role-play moving between activities; sticker or point reward phased out once smooth; praise what the fast exit made possible ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-can-we-help-kids-with-transitions/)).
- **Help:** Rigid distress at most changes may warrant assessment; see ADHD/autism note ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/why-do-kids-have-trouble-with-transitions/)).

#### 13. Won't get in the car seat / buggy (Strength C/D)
- **Drivers:** Toddler need for control ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)). No source addresses a refusal in detail.
- **Now:** Non-negotiable: the seat is used on every trip, every time ([AAP, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/on-the-go/Pages/Car-Safety-Seats-Information-for-Families.aspx)). Acknowledge the feeling, state the limit, offer a choice about something else ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/coping-with-defiance-birth-to-three/)). Harness snug, no pinchable slack ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/on-the-go/Pages/Car-Safety-Seats-Information-for-Families.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Negotiating car-seat rules ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)); padding under or behind the child; leaving a child alone in or around a vehicle ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/on-the-go/Pages/Car-Safety-Seats-Information-for-Families.aspx)).
- **Wording (*composite*):** You really don't want to sit. The seat keeps you safe, so it's buckle time. Which toy rides with you?
- **Later/play:** AAP says consistent use reduces fussing and complaints ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/on-the-go/Pages/Car-Safety-Seats-Information-for-Families.aspx)); road-trip play ideas exist ([AAP list item](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/road-trip-play-ideas-for-backseat-fun.aspx), content not read); practising buckling a teddy is my inference, not sourced.
- **Help:** What to do when a child unbuckles was not addressed by AAP. Buggy refusal: no source.

#### 14. Screen-off meltdown (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Moving from preferred to less rewarding activity ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/why-do-kids-have-trouble-with-transitions/)). Triple P cites research linking frequent device calming with harder emotion management ([Triple P](https://www.triplep-parenting.net.au/au/articles-and-news/post/how-to-handle-tantrums-try-these-positive-parenting-tips/)); primary study not verified.
- **Now:** Keep your cool; a few calm words with no rule-explaining; invite to a calm spot or gently carry a younger child; offer something more appealing ("trading up"); sit nearby until they reconnect ([AAP, 13 Mar 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Long explanations, matching the meltdown, rules adults don't follow ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)).
- **Conflict:** AAP warns against suggesting a child punch a pillow, yet suggests batting a pillow tossed in the air on the same page ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)), while ZERO TO THREE lists hitting pillows and cushions as an outlet ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). Prefer stomping and jumping.
- **Wording (paraphrase):** I know it's tough, and the tablet is going away now ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)). Sorry I was on my phone just now, as parent modelling ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Device timer or child-set timer, family media plan, device-free times ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)); countdowns and praising quick hand-over ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-can-we-help-kids-with-transitions/)).
- **Help:** Meltdowns nearly every time, changes in sleep, hygiene, eating or social life, withdrawing ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)).

#### 15. Bedtime stalling (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Poor habits or anxiety about bedtime ([AACAP, Mar 2023](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Childrens-Sleep-Problems-034.aspx)); toddler fears of bedtime ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)).
- **Now:** Same bedtime; deal with water, light and door before lights out so they can't become delays; stay understanding ([AAP, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/bedtime-trouble.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Active play before bed ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/bedtime-trouble.aspx)); open-ended routines. School-age: cap at about 30 minutes, one story or song ([AAP, 2013](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/Bedtime-Routines-for-School-Aged-Children.aspx)).
- **Wording (*composite*):** We've had water and our one story. It's sleep time. I'll see you in the morning.
- **Later/play:** Routine with teeth, book, tuck-in, kept short ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/structure-rules/index.html)); give some choice within limits ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/Bedtime-Routines-for-School-Aged-Children.aspx)). **The "bedtime pass" technique could not be verified.**
- **Help:** Loud snoring or mouth breathing, restless legs, daytime sleepiness despite enough sleep ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Childrens-Sleep-Problems-034.aspx)).

#### 16. Night waking and coming into the bed (Strength B, values-dependent)
- **Drivers:** Separation anxiety may bring anxious waking, usually fading around the second birthday ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/separation-anxiety-and-sleeping.aspx)). Anxiety at any age can show as night waking ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/anxiety-in-children/)).
- **Now:** **Conflict within AAP:** one page advises a loving, consistent response with reassurance, night after night, during separation anxiety ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/separation-anxiety-and-sleeping.aspx)); another advises not going back when they call once you've confirmed they're safe, and not letting them sleep in your bed ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/bedtime-trouble.aspx)). CPS advises reassuring and staying until asleep after a nightmare ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)).
- **Avoid:** Negative reactions ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/bedtime-trouble.aspx)). Bed-sharing under 1 year specifically: AAP says avoid under any circumstances; this safety rule applies up to age 1 ([AAP, 11 Sep 2026](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/A-Parents-Guide-to-Safe-Sleep.aspx)).
- **Wording (*composite*):** You're safe. It's night-time, I'm nearby. Back to your bed.
- **Later/play:** German guidance says sleep-training methods should be well considered, and lasting problems rarely resolve alone, so seek advice early ([BIÖG](https://www.kindergesundheit-info.de/themen/schlafen/schlafprobleme/)). Daytime separation games from peek-a-boo onward ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/separation-anxiety/)).
- **Help:** Lasting problems; signs in #15. Any sudden, continued behaviour change deserves attention ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/What-to-Know-about-Child-Abuse.aspx)).

#### 17. Night terrors vs nightmares (Strength B)
- **Tell them apart:** Terrors: early in the night, child asleep, may scream and thrash with eyes open, no memory. Nightmares: later in the night, child wakes and remembers ([NHS, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/night-terrors/); [AAP, 21 Sep 2023](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Nightmares-and-Night-Terrors.aspx)).
- **Ages:** Nightmares can begin around 6 months and peak 3-12; terrors most common in toddlers and preschoolers ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Nightmares-and-Night-Terrors.aspx)). **Conflict:** AACAP says terrors typically begin at 4-12 ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Childrens-Sleep-Problems-034.aspx)). Length: up to 15 minutes ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/night-terrors/)), up to 45 with most shorter ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Nightmares-and-Night-Terrors.aspx)).
- **Now, terror:** Stay calm, wait, keep them safe; don't wake them ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/night-terrors/)). **Conflict on holding:** AAP says gently restrain if trying to get out of bed; NHS says don't restrain unless at risk. Safe combined rule: only touch to prevent harm.
- **Now, nightmare:** Go quickly, reassure, let them talk, say dreams aren't real, check shadows, light can help ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Nightmares-and-Night-Terrors.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** That was a scary dream and it's over. I'm here. Dreams aren't real ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Nightmares-and-Night-Terrors.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Younger children: draw it and tear it up, or a dream-catcher, for a sense of control ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Childrens-Sleep-Problems-034.aspx)). Terrors at the same time nightly: waking 15 minutes before for 2 weeks may stop them ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/night-terrors/)). Tell babysitters what to do ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Nightmares-and-Night-Terrors.aspx)).
- **Help:** Nightmares after 6, frequent, or after an upsetting event; terrors after 12 or starting after 5 ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/night-terrors/)); frequent terrors ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)).

#### 18. Separation at drop-off (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Common 6 months to 3 years ([NHS, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/separation-anxiety/)); toddlers often from 15-18 months, harder when tired or sick; preschoolers know how their pleas affect you ([AAP, 11 May 2026](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Soothing-Your-Childs-Separation-Anxiety.aspx)). Easier by 3 ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/emotional-development-2-year-olds.aspx)).
- **Now:** Short consistent ritual, full attention, then leave quickly even if they cry; return when you said ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Soothing-Your-Childs-Separation-Anxiety.aspx)). Say goodbye cheerfully; a familiar item helps ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/separation-anxiety/)).
- **Avoid:** Lingering; returning at their pleas; sneaking off ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Soothing-Your-Childs-Separation-Anxiety.aspx); [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Emotional-Development-1-Year-Olds.aspx)); scolding ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/emotional-development-2-year-olds.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** I'll be back after nap and before snack; for long gaps, count sleeps ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Soothing-Your-Childs-Separation-Anxiety.aspx)). Tell them what you'll do together on return ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/separation-anxiety/)).
- **Later/play:** Peek-a-boo; practise short separations with someone familiar ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/separation-anxiety/)); rehearse drop-off ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Soothing-Your-Childs-Separation-Anxiety.aspx)); pretend play about school ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/pretend-play-ways-children-can-exercise-their-imagination.aspx)).
- **Help:** Severe distress, long after you leave, over a few weeks, or stops nursery or work ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/separation-anxiety/)); daily beyond preschool ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Soothing-Your-Childs-Separation-Anxiety.aspx)).

#### 19. Refusing food / picky at the table (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Usually a normal toddler stage ([AAP, 26 Apr 2018](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx)).
- **Now:** Take the food away calmly and offer again another time, without forcing ([NHS, 13 Nov 2023, review due 13 Nov 2026](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/weaning-and-feeding/fussy-eaters/)). One family meal including a liked food; no separate meal ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Pressure, punishment, fuss, treat bribes; pressure can make children dislike foods ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx)). Sweets as rewards, comparing with other children, judging on one day's intake ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/weaning-and-feeding/fussy-eaters/)). Withholding meals as a consequence ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Disciplining-Your-Child.aspx)).
- **Wording (*composite*):** You don't have to eat it. It'll come back another day.
- **Later/play:** Reoffer, it can take 10 or more tastes; food bridges; kids help choose and prepare ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx)); praise any eating, small portions, eat together ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/weaning-and-feeding/fussy-eaters/)).
- **Help:** Worried about diet ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/nutrition/Pages/Picky-Eaters.aspx)). NHS reassurance marker: active, gaining weight, seems well ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/weaning-and-feeding/fussy-eaters/)). ARFID is listed among conditions SPACE treats ([SPACE](https://www.spacetreatment.net/)). Satter's division of responsibility could not be verified (404).

#### 20. Refusing to brush teeth (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Toddler independence ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/let-the-brushing-games-begin.aspx)). Under 3 the adult brushes; children usually need help until about 8 ([NHS, 30 Jun 2025](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/taking-care-of-childrens-teeth/); [AAP, 14 May 2025](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Toothbrushing-Tips-for-Young-Children.aspx)).
- **Now:** Song, take turns brushing each other, app or timer; child-chosen mild flavour ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Toothbrushing-Tips-for-Young-Children.aspx)). Toddlers often brush easiest lying down, head on your lap ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Toothbrushing-Tips-for-Young-Children.aspx)). Let them go first, then you check and touch up; name the "smile teeth" and "biting teeth" ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/let-the-brushing-games-begin.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Running with a toothbrush; rinsing after ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/taking-care-of-childrens-teeth/)). Whether to physically hold a child for brushing was not addressed by any source; do not advise it.
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Your turn on my teeth, then my turn on yours ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Toothbrushing-Tips-for-Young-Children.aspx)). Egg-timer race ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/taking-care-of-childrens-teeth/)).
- **Later/play:** Brush, book, bed routine ([AAP list item](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Brush-Book-Bed.aspx), content not read). Let them see you brush ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/play-and-learning/teaching-your-child-everyday-skills/)).
- **Help:** Persistent resistance: ask paediatrician or dentist ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/oral-health/Pages/Toothbrushing-Tips-for-Young-Children.aspx)); early positive dental visits ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/taking-care-of-childrens-teeth/)).

#### 21. Refusing bath or hair wash (Strength C/D)
- **Drivers:** No dedicated source verified. Toddlers and preschoolers may fear loud noises and imagined dangers ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)).

Take a fear seriously and introduce it slowly ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)).

- **Help:** Inconsolable consistent fear may be a phobia ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)). **Hair-wash specifics: gap.**

#### 22. Potty resistance and accidents (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Control is the child's; constant asking can feel controlling ([AAP, 25 May 2022](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Emotional-Issues-and-Bathroom-Problems.aspx)). Bladder and bowel control around 18-24 months; US training usually starts 2-3, most trained by 4 ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/the-right-age-to-toilet-train.aspx)); CPS says most start 2-4 ([CPS, Mar 2023](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/toilet_learning)). Around 3 children may hide accidents out of shame ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Emotional-Issues-and-Bathroom-Problems.aspx)). Regression triggers: new sibling, move, new care, illness, divorce, constipation, UTI ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Regression.aspx)).
- **Now:** Stay calm. Say it's okay, you'll get there, then clean up together ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Emotional-Issues-and-Bathroom-Problems.aspx)). Keep a spare set of clothes ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/toilet_learning)).
- **Avoid:** Punishing, overreacting, rushing, negative words such as dirty ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/toilet_learning)); power struggles ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Praise-and-Reward-Your-Childs-Success.aspx)).
- **Conflicts:** Back to nappies: AAP advises against major concessions ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Emotional-Issues-and-Bathroom-Problems.aspx)); CPS allows a return and diapers for stool refusal ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/toilet_learning)); NHS says don't treat regression as failure ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/play-and-learning/teaching-your-child-everyday-skills/)). Rewards: one AAP page says skip treats and punishments ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Praise-and-Reward-Your-Childs-Success.aspx)), another lists sticker charts ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Regression.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** You had an accident. That's okay. Let's change together and work out next time ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Emotional-Issues-and-Bathroom-Problems.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Sitting routine after waking, meals and naps ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/toilet_learning)); potty books; let them flush ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Praise-and-Reward-Your-Childs-Success.aspx)); a break of 1-3 months if not ready ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/toilet_learning)).
- **Help:** Not using the toilet by 4; regression after 6+ months dry; withholding stool, pain, blood; cloudy urine, urgency, redness ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/toilet_learning)). New bedwetting after 6 months dry, or with mood change ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Bedwetting.aspx); [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bedwetting/)).

#### 23. Lying (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Under about 6 the line between fantasy and truth blurs; after about 6 a lie is a conscious act, often from fear of disappointing or of punishment; very high parental standards can push lying; children notice parents' white lies ([AAP, 18 Mar 2025](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/When-Children-Lie.aspx)).
- **Now:** Say you know it isn't true; stay calm ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/When-Children-Lie.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Harsh punishment; extreme negativity, which can drive more lying ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/When-Children-Lie.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** I know that's not what happened. We tell each other the truth so we can trust each other, and telling me the truth means less trouble than lying ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/When-Children-Lie.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Model honesty ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/When-Children-Lie.aspx)).
- **Help:** Chronic lying, or lying with stealing and peer trouble ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/When-Children-Lie.aspx); [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Swearing.aspx)).

#### 24. Stealing / taking things (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Very young: curiosity, not stealing until about 3-5, when they can grasp it is wrong. Older: evening things up, showing bravery, gifts, fitting in ([AACAP, Dec 2017](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-Who-Steal-012.aspx)).
- **Now:** Say stealing is wrong, help them return or pay, make sure they gain nothing ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-Who-Steal-012.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Lecturing, predicting future misbehaviour, calling them a thief, bringing it up again once resolved ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-Who-Steal-012.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** That belongs to the shop. Taking it isn't okay in our family. We'll take it back together. Then it's done ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-Who-Steal-012.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Teach property rights and consideration early ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-Who-Steal-012.aspx)).
- **Help:** Continues, or comes with other problems; may point to trust or relationship difficulties ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-Who-Steal-012.aspx)).

#### 25. Swearing and rude words (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** At 3-4, potty talk is funny and shock makes it funnier ([AAP, 2014](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Potty-Talk.aspx)). Younger children repeat words without knowing them; in middle childhood it is almost developmentally normal and not by itself a sign of disturbance ([AAP, 19 Oct 2018](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Swearing.aspx)).
- **Now:** No big reaction; redirect; ignore if the aim is a reaction. Do not tolerate swearing aimed at someone in anger ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Swearing.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Washing a mouth with soap, which AAP calls improper, extreme and ineffective; your own angry cursing ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Swearing.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** That word sounds funny to you, doesn't it? ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Potty-Talk.aspx)). In our home we don't use that word ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Swearing.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Silly-joke redirect; teach where words fit ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/toilet-training/Pages/Potty-Talk.aspx)); star chart or coin jar for swear-free days ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Swearing.aspx)).
- **Help:** With chronic lying, stealing or peer trouble ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Swearing.aspx)).

#### 26. Backtalk at 6-12 (Strength C)
- **Drivers:** No dedicated source. Defiance sources in #10.
- **Now:** Stay calm, consistent and respectful ([AAP, 2009](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/The-Disobedient-Child.aspx)); take a break if it's escalating; avoid adding consequences for arguing ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-with-Oppositional-Defiant-Disorder-072.aspx)). AAP's teen advice: active ignoring, you'll listen when they speak respectfully; 15-20 minutes to calm ([AAP, 2015, teen focus](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Disciplining-Older-Children.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Sarcasm, teasing, belittling, "you" messages ([AAP, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/communication-dos-and-donts.aspx)); harsh verbal discipline ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Disciplining-Your-Child.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Yes, that does sound frustrating ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/guide/parents-guide-to-problem-behavior/)). An "I" message about how you felt ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/communication-dos-and-donts.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Plan B talk ([Greene](https://www.actcommunity.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Ross-Greene-2022-Vancouver-PDA-2slides.pdf)); family meeting ([AAP list item](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/How-to-Have-a-Family-Meeting.aspx), content not read).
- **Help:** Long-standing at home and school; with aggression or destructiveness; signs of deep unhappiness; family pattern of abuse in arguments ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/The-Disobedient-Child.aspx)).

#### 27. Homework standoff (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Fatigue ([Child Mind, 6 Feb 2026](https://childmind.org/article/strategies-to-make-homework-go-more-smoothly/)); task difficulty and heavy pressure ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/competition-and-cheating.aspx)).
- **Now:** Stop arguing. Let them choose task order, take a scheduled break ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/strategies-to-make-homework-go-more-smoothly/)). Regulate first, then reason ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Labels such as lazy or bad attitude; penalties you can't enforce; late-night work; arguing over complaints ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/strategies-to-make-homework-go-more-smoothly/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Which piece first? Then a break at the time we agreed ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/strategies-to-make-homework-go-more-smoothly/)).
- **Later/play:** Same start time after a snack; simple space; do your own paperwork alongside ([AAP, 2019](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/school/Pages/Ten-Tips-for-Your-Childs-Success-in-School.aspx)); estimates and schedule, incentives, homework contract ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/strategies-to-make-homework-go-more-smoothly/)).
- **Help:** Earning more penalties than rewards: see a school psychologist or counsellor ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/strategies-to-make-homework-go-more-smoothly/)); persistent trouble: check for learning problems ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-with-Oppositional-Defiant-Disorder-072.aspx)).

#### 28. "I hate you" (Strength D, built from neighbours)
- **Drivers:** No dedicated source. Toddlers aren't trying to upset you ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)); feeling angry or guilty about a child's behaviour is a normal parenting experience ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/A-Perfect-Parent.aspx)).
- **Now:** Don't take it personally; stay calm ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). Name the feeling, keep the limit ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html)). Give space to an angry child and talk later ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/talk-to-children-about-feelings/)).
- **Avoid:** Telling them to stop feeling it ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html)); hurtful replies such as saying they're driving you crazy ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx)).
- **Wording (*composite*):** You're really angry with me right now. That's allowed. The answer's still no, and I still love you.
- **Later/play:** Say-it-don't-do-it words ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_emotions.pdf)); draw the anger, give it a name for younger children ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/help-your-child-with-anger-issues/)).
- **Help:** Mean or hateful speech as part of a persistent pattern ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-with-Oppositional-Defiant-Disorder-072.aspx)). **Any "I wish I was dead" talk goes to the self-harm path, not this card** ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Teen-Suicide-010.aspx)).

#### 29. Sore loser / cheating at games (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** At roughly 5-7 cheating in peer games is common and the desire to do well is strong; pressure or too-hard tasks can trigger it; family honesty models it ([AAP, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/competition-and-cheating.aspx)).
- **Now:** Address each episode at roughly 8-12 and discuss the pressure behind it, including your expectations ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/competition-and-cheating.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Excessive punishment ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/competition-and-cheating.aspx)).
- **Wording (*composite*):** Losing feels rotten. The rule stays the same for everyone. Want to play again or take a break?
- **Later/play:** No-winner games such as ball toss, turn-based chalk drawing, for kids who melt down over losing ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/defiant-behavior-how-play-can-help-children-cope-with-change.aspx)); chance-based family games ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/competition-and-cheating.aspx)); kids-versus-adults ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/helping-siblings-play-together-peacefully.aspx)); coin flips to settle disputes ([Harvard](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-5-to-7-year-olds.pdf)).
- **Help:** Chronic cheating or being labelled a cheater ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/competition-and-cheating.aspx)).

#### 30. Meltdown after school ("restraint collapse") (Strength C/D)
- **Drivers:** **The term could not be verified.** Three likely URLs failed, and I found no peer-reviewed or authority use, so present it as a popular label only. Related: children show stress through behaviour; strong emotion can take over thinking ([AAP, 17 May 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)). Autistic and ADHD children may find transitions harder ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/why-do-kids-have-trouble-with-transitions/)). AAP notes 2-year-olds often behave better with sitters because they feel less need to test limits there ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/emotional-development-2-year-olds.aspx)); no source I read extends this to school-age children.
- **Now:** Regulate first: calm voice, get to their height, movement or a hug; relate; reason last ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)). Snack before homework ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/school/Pages/Ten-Tips-for-Your-Childs-Success-in-School.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Jumping to problem-solving ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/creating-calm-how-to-talk-with-your-child-when-theyre-stressed.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** We'll get through this together ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/creating-calm-how-to-talk-with-your-child-when-theyre-stressed.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Parallel activity such as colouring; a calm-place picture when well ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)). Best-part and hardest-part question ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Growing-Independence-Tips-for-Parents-of-Young-Children.aspx)).
- **Help:** Reluctance to go to school, withdrawal, declining grades ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Bullying-080.aspx)); persistent worry ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/anxiety-in-children/)).

#### 31. Shyness / won't say hello (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Some children are naturally slow to warm up; most shy children do well after an adjustment period ([AAP, 2009](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/Shyness-in-Children.aspx)). Strangers can upset babies on and off for two years ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)).
- **Now:** Gently guide, don't assume a problem ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/Shyness-in-Children.aspx)). No source verified on forcing greetings. Criticise behaviour, not the person ([BIÖG](https://www.kindergesundheit-info.de/themen/entwicklung/psychische-gesundheit/erziehung/)). See cross-cultural notes on respect greetings.
- **Avoid:** Letting avoidance become a long pattern ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/Shyness-in-Children.aspx)); forcing without talking first ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/anxiety-in-children/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Express confidence you'll help them through ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/what-to-do-and-not-do-when-children-are-anxious/)).
- **Later/play:** Rehearse social skills ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/Shyness-in-Children.aspx)); help shy preschoolers warm up in play ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/what-parents-can-do-to-support-friendships.aspx)).
- **Help:** Debilitating shyness or withdrawal, repeated rejection, trouble forming friendships ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/Pages/Shyness-in-Children.aspx)); selective mutism is a SPACE target ([SPACE](https://www.spacetreatment.net/)).

#### 32. Fears and worries (Strength A/B)
- **Drivers:** 2-4: imaginary dangers, bedtime, doctors, loud noises. 5+: real-world fears, parents' health or marriage, media ([CPS, Apr 2022](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)). Younger signs: clingy, tearful, bad dreams, bedwetting. Older: angry outbursts, avoidance ([NHS, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/anxiety-in-children/)).
- **Now:** Acknowledge, express confidence, offer comfort, slow breathing ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears); [Child Mind, 2 Jul 2026](https://childmind.org/article/what-to-do-and-not-do-when-children-are-anxious/)).
- **Avoid:** Removing every trigger or avoiding feared situations; promising outcomes you can't guarantee; leading questions; long lead-up talk; dismissing or amplifying ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/what-to-do-and-not-do-when-children-are-anxious/)). Forcing before ready, mocking ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)). Accommodation ([CHADD](https://chadd.org/attention-article/space-a-parent-based-intervention-for-reducing-childhood-anxiety/)). Joking threats toddlers take literally ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Emotional-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** That scared you, and I'm sure you can cope ([CHADD on SPACE](https://chadd.org/attention-article/space-a-parent-based-intervention-for-reducing-childhood-anxiety/)). Practise "I can try this" ([AAP, 24 Sep 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/help-your-child-manage-fears-and-anxieties.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Gradual steps with praise; books, drawing, doll sharing, toy fire truck ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)); worry box; explain anxiety as a wave ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/anxiety-in-children/)); safe-place imagery ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/help-your-child-manage-fears-and-anxieties.aspx)).
- **Help:** Severe, persistent or disruptive ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/anxiety-in-children/)); stops sleeping alone or school; after a scary event; CBT can help ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/help-your-child-manage-fears-and-anxieties.aspx)); physical symptoms; 3-year-old not adapting within weeks; inconsolable consistent fear ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)).

#### 33. Friendship trouble, being left out (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Most children feel pushed away at some point; may reflect a group reaction to difference or behaviour by either child ([AAP, 4 Mar 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/school/Pages/Problems-With-Peers.aspx)).
- **Now:** Acknowledge it's hard; say you're on their side and will work on it together ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/school/Pages/Problems-With-Peers.aspx)). Feelings first, then look for the real source ([Child Mind, 27 Apr 2026](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)).
- **Avoid:** Assuming they'll work it out alone ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/what-parents-can-do-to-support-friendships.aspx)); treating one incident as the end ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** I felt embarrassed when you posted that, as an "I" statement to practise ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)). One thing a friend did doesn't define them ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)).
- **Later/play:** Role-play the conversation; problem-solving "baseball"; smiley-face rating of options ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)); host friends, name feelings then consider the friend's ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/what-parents-can-do-to-support-friendships.aspx)).
- **Help:** If it continues: teacher, principal, counsellor, then paediatrician ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/school/Pages/Problems-With-Peers.aspx)).

#### 34. Being bullied (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** A pattern of intent, repetition and power; not one incident ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/bullying)). Signs: injuries, school fear, lost belongings, sleep trouble, stomach aches, online secrecy ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/bullying)); withdrawal, declining grades ([AACAP, Apr 2017](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Bullying-080.aspx)).
- **Now:** Listen calmly; believe them; not their fault; telling was right ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/bullying); [AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Bullying-080.aspx)). Don't fix it yet ([AAP, 6 Nov 2025](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Encouraging fighting back ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Bullying-080.aspx)); forwarding or liking posts ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** I believe you. I'm glad you told me. This isn't your fault. We'll sort out help ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/bullying)).
- **Later/play:** Rehearse "stop, walk, talk" ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)); stay with friends; work with school on supervision ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Bullying-080.aspx)); clubs and sports; block online ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)).
- **Help:** Withdrawal, depression, school avoidance, falling grades, continued bullying; immediate danger, emergency services; some victims have attempted suicide ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Bullying-080.aspx)). stopbullying.gov was blocked.

#### 35. Mean behaviour to others (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Children who bully may mirror home behaviour or have been hurt, be depressed or angry ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/bullying); [AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Bullying-080.aspx)); may signal trauma ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)).
- **Now:** Act right away; say hurting, shaming or excluding is not acceptable, even toward kids they dislike ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)).
- **Avoid:** Physical discipline, which teaches aggression ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)); calling the child a bad person ([AACAP on stealing, by analogy](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-Who-Steal-012.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Those words hurt her. It's not okay in our family. How do you think she felt? What will you do to make it right? ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx); [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/bullying)).
- **Later/play:** Non-violent consequences such as limiting screens or social events, encourage amends; practise responses; examine adults' behaviour at home ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/bullying)); praise kindness ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)).
- **Help:** Many need professional help to change habits, especially with trauma ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Bullying-Its-Not-Ok.aspx)); bullying continues, seek full evaluation ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Bullying-080.aspx)).

#### 36. Running off in public (Strength C)
- **Drivers:** Ages 2-4 don't understand danger or remember "no" while exploring ([AAP, 29 Sep 2022](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/safety-for-your-child-2-to-4-years.aspx)).
- **Now:** Hold them firmly if near a street ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)). Afterward state the rule briefly with the wanted behaviour ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/structure-rules/rules.html)).
- **Avoid:** Relying on the child's memory of a rule around roads ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/safety-for-your-child-2-to-4-years.aspx)); yelling or embarrassing ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/index.html)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Please walk next to me ([CDC do-not-don't pattern](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/giving-directions.html)). Thank you for being patient and staying by me ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/index.html)).
- **Later/play:** Red Light, Green Light; freeze dance ([Harvard](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-5-to-7-year-olds.pdf)); reins from 18 months ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/play-and-learning/baby-and-toddler-play-ideas/)).
- **Help:** Seems fearless or reckless ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). Wandering with autism or ADHD was not verified.

#### 37. Interrupting (Strength C)
- **Drivers:** Minor attention-seeking behaviour ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)); limited waiting skills ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/)).
- **Now:** Ignore the interruption, return attention and praise when they wait; but meet real needs first ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html)).
- **Avoid:** Responding only with negative attention ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/guide/parents-guide-to-problem-behavior/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Rule: no interrupting; wait for your turn to talk ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/structure-rules/rules.html)). Name the waiting when you praise it ([CDC labelled praise](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/using-rewards.html)).
- **Later/play:** Timers for waiting ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/)); Magic Word game; Simon Says ([Harvard](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-5-to-7-year-olds.pdf)).
- **Help:** Extreme impulsiveness ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Understanding-Violent-Behavior-In-Children-and-Adolescents-055.aspx)). The widely shared "hand on arm" technique was not verified; do not attribute it.

#### 38. Dawdling in the morning (Strength B)
- **Drivers:** Doing tasks for the child reinforces dawdling ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/guide/parents-guide-to-problem-behavior/)); slow starters ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_cooperate.pdf)).
- **Now:** One instruction, up close, say it once and wait ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/guide/parents-guide-to-problem-behavior/)); beat the clock; start one together ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_cooperate.pdf)).
- **Avoid:** Repeating; several instructions at once ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/guide/parents-guide-to-problem-behavior/)); yelling ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Disciplining-Your-Child.aspx)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Let's see how fast you can do it; I'll time you ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_cooperate.pdf)). Jacket: this one or that one? ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/The-Importance-of-Family-Routines.aspx)).
- **Later/play:** Prep the night before, upbeat music, brief hug-and-wave goodbye ([AAP, 28 Jun 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/The-Importance-of-Family-Routines.aspx)); launch pad spot ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/school/Pages/Ten-Tips-for-Your-Childs-Success-in-School.aspx)); a picture chart, adapted from CDC's rules chart for non-readers ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/structure-rules/rules.html)); morning special time is a testimonial only ([Hand in Hand](https://www.handinhandparenting.org/2023/07/a-dose-of-special-time-in-the-morning/)).
- **Help:** ADHD note on shifting attention ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/why-do-kids-have-trouble-with-transitions/)).

#### 39. Overstimulation at parties (Strength C)
- **Drivers:** Big crowds overwhelm young children ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/playful-birthday-party-ideas-and-tips-for-young-children.aspx)); long social events and sensory overload are triggers ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)).
- **Now:** Move to a quieter spot and sit with them ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-in.html)); regulate before talking; offer a hug or quiet ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)). Leaving early is my inference, not sourced.
- **Avoid:** Reasoning mid-overload ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-to-handle-tantrums-and-meltdowns/)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** It seems like something is bothering you; let's sit here together ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html)).
- **Later/play:** Guest count of age plus one for babies and toddlers; under 90 minutes for toddlers, 2-3 hours school-age; 10-15 minutes per activity ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/playful-birthday-party-ideas-and-tips-for-young-children.aspx)); smaller groups and early warning signs ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/caring-for-children-and-young-people/how-to-deal-with-challenging-behaviour-in-children/)); preview the event ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/how-can-we-help-kids-with-transitions/)).
- **Help:** Sensory overstimulation and strong need for order ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/why-do-kids-have-trouble-with-transitions/)); craving intense input ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)).

#### 40. Big questions (Strength B)

**Death.** Under 5 may not grasp it; preschoolers see it as temporary; final by about 5-7; teach permanence, no more suffering, all living things die, not your fault ([AAP, 26 Jun 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/How-Children-Understand-Death-What-You-Should-Say.aspx); [AACAP, Sep 2023](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-and-Grief-008.aspx)). *Say (paraphrase):* she died, she won't come back, and we'll miss her; it's very sad ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/How-Children-Understand-Death-What-You-Should-Say.aspx); [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/talk-to-children-about-feelings/)). *Avoid:* gone to sleep or on a long journey; "at least"; "be strong"; "I know how you feel" ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/How-Children-Understand-Death-What-You-Should-Say.aspx)); forcing funeral attendance ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-and-Grief-008.aspx)). *Later:* candle, prayer, scrapbook, photos ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-and-Grief-008.aspx)). *Help:* lasting depression, acting younger for long, saying they want to join the person, school refusal; self-harm or suicide talk urgently ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-and-Grief-008.aspx); [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/How-Children-Understand-Death-What-You-Should-Say.aspx)).

**Divorce.** Tell together if possible, not at the last minute; simple; only what is asked ([AACAP, Apr 2026](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-and-Divorce-001.aspx)). *Say (paraphrase):* we've decided not to stay married or live together; it has nothing to do with you; we both love you; you'll have two homes ([AAP, 29 Sep 2020](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/How-to-Talk-to-Your-Children-about-Divorce.aspx)). *Avoid:* blaming the other parent, making them pick sides, using them for comfort ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-and-Divorce-001.aspx)). *Help:* young: aggression or withdrawal; older: deep sadness, schoolwork, rejecting a parent ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-and-Divorce-001.aspx)).

**Where babies come from.** Use proper body names at every age. At 4-5 keep it brief; at 5-7 give accurate information before friends or the internet do; use a family pregnancy as a teachable moment; stay calm, check whether that answered the question ([AAP, 28 Mar 2023](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Talking-to-Your-Young-Child-About-Sex.aspx)). Clarify the question first ([AAP, 2013](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/gradeschool/puberty/Pages/Talking-to-Your-Child-About-Sex.aspx)). Questions about bodies are normal curiosity ages 2-6 ([AAP, 17 Apr 2023](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Sexual-Behaviors-Young-Children.aspx)). See culture notes: families will vary.

**Scary news.** Children should hear from you, not peers or media; be direct, without graphic detail; vague statements don't help ([AAP, 28 Aug 2025](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/talking-to-children-about-tragedies-and-other-news-events.aspx)). Set a calm tone, reassure these events are rare, answer honestly, don't volunteer extra, limit repeat exposure ([Child Mind, 26 Jun 2026](https://childmind.org/article/helping-children-cope-frightening-news/)). *Say (paraphrase):* something sad happened and people were hurt; it's okay if this bothers you; the people in charge are working to stop it happening again ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/talking-to-children-about-tragedies-and-other-news-events.aspx)). *Avoid:* trying to block everything, which may raise fears ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/talk-to-children-about-feelings/)). Autistic children may need different comfort ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/talking-to-children-about-tragedies-and-other-news-events.aspx)). *Help:* sleep trouble, regression, clinginess, stomach aches, persistent fear ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/talking-to-children-about-tragedies-and-other-news-events.aspx); [AACAP, Jun 2025](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-And-The-News-067.aspx)).

#### 41. Parent lost their temper: repair (Strength C)
- **Drivers:** Parental anger and guilt are normal; children are generally forgiving ([AAP, 14 Feb 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/A-Perfect-Parent.aspx)).
- **Now:** Pause, breathe, take a short break ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). Acknowledge and reconnect ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)). BIÖG lists unwillingness to apologise among things to avoid ([BIÖG](https://www.kindergesundheit-info.de/themen/entwicklung/psychische-gesundheit/erziehung/)). Let kids see your mistakes and how you recover ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)).
- **Avoid:** Critical or hurtful statements; name your actual feeling instead ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx)); blaming yourself or your child ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/dealing-with-child-behaviour-problems/)). Harsh verbal discipline is a key thing to reduce ([IPD study](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1737034/individual-participant-data-meta-analysis-individu)).
- **Wording (paraphrase):** Please forgive me for that ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/screen-time-and-temper-tantrums-helpful-tips-for-parents.aspx)). *Composite:* I shouted and I'm sorry. I was frustrated. The rule is still the rule, and I love you. Next time I'll take a breath first.
- **Later/play:** Child-led play the same day, even after a hard day ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/child-led-play.html)); "Repair" is also a named Good Inside step (market reference; [Good Inside](https://www.goodinside.com/)).
- **Help:** If things feel too hard to cope with, start with your doctor ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/A-Perfect-Parent.aspx)); UK: Family Lives 0808 800 2222 ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/dealing-with-child-behaviour-problems/)); NSPCC 0808 800 5000 for concern about a child's safety ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/caring-for-children-and-young-people/how-to-deal-with-challenging-behaviour-in-children/)). Check a local number for each market.

### Inferences

- A mid-meltdown card can follow one skeleton: **(1) Safe and calm: you first. (2) Name it and hold the line, in under 15 words. (3) One action: a choice, outlet, consequence or just staying close.** Follow-up teaching belongs to the "later" tab, because sources agree on not teaching during the storm ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html); [Triple P](https://www.triplep-parenting.net.au/au/articles-and-news/post/how-to-handle-tantrums-try-these-positive-parenting-tips/); [ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). The 15-word limit is my suggestion.
- A two-path branch ("upset" vs "testing a limit") resolves the ignoring vs time-in conflict, but parents can't always tell. Default to time-in if unsure and always stop hurting.
- Cards with Strength D should say "general guidance" in-app and be reviewed by a clinician first.
- Night waking should not take a single side: offer "keep them in their own bed" and "shared-sleep family" variants.

## What is safe and appropriate at each age? When is time-out appropriate? What must never be advised? What are the red flags?

### Takeaway

Sources agree on "one minute per year of age" where time-out is used, on keeping it calm and attention-free, and on never hitting or shaming. They disagree on the youngest age. The conservative combined position is: **no time-out under 2, time-in or a short, optional version at 2, and standard time-out from 3**, always supervised, with the backup-room step left out of a self-serve app.

### Inferences

- **Recommended app rule (mine, from the table):** do not show a time-out path under 2. At 2-3 show time-in first, with short time-out as an optional second choice and a note that Canadian paediatricians advise against it under 3. Offer the 1-minute-per-year version only from 3, for hurting or broken safety rules, and only as a parent-practised routine. Leave out barrel carry and backup room. They need coaching and could escalate an upset parent's handling.
- Make the self-harm and abuse paths a hard interrupt, not a card. Crisis numbers must be localised per market; 988, 911, 111, 999 and UK helplines are the only ones verified here, and AAP's depression page shows how a number can go stale.
- The app should not diagnose. Sources say "evaluate" or "talk to the doctor", and so should the app.

### Gaps

- No crisis numbers or child-protection routes were verified for the Middle East, South Asia, East Asia, Africa or Latin America.
- CPS's position statement and any 2024-2026 update on the minimum time-out age were not found.
- Suicide-screening guidance specific to children under 10 was not verified. NIMH's page is general.
- Developmental regression and loss of skills (e.g. autism flags) were not separately verified; CDC's developmental milestone pages were not fetched.

## Which play activities have evidence for building these skills?

### Takeaway

Evidence is strongest for **child-led one-on-one time** as a component of tested programmes, and modest for rule-based self-regulation games: the one RCT I could open found math gains and only a non-significant self-regulation effect. Most game recommendations are expert guidance, so the app should say "good practice" and not "proven".

### Cited Findings

- **Special / child-led time:** Core to PCIT (daily 5 minutes) ([CEBC](https://www.cebc4cw.org/program/parent-child-interaction-therapy/)) and IY (child-directed play; school-age "special time") ([Blueprints](https://www.blueprintsprograms.org/programs/29999999/incredible-years-parent/print/)). CDC: 5-15 minutes daily, same time, describe and imitate, avoid questions, directions and criticism ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/child-led-play.html)). AAP: about 5 minutes for the "attention meter"; 10-15 minutes 2-3 times a week as "sunshine time" ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx); [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/helping-little-people-manage-big-feelings.aspx)). PLH one-on-one: regular, free, phones and TV off ([ForChildren](https://www.forchildren.com/discover/relationships/covid-19-parenting-one-on-one-time-eng)). Effect of this component alone was not verified.
- **Red Light games:** RCT of Red Light, Purple Light (Head Start, ages 3-5, 157 children analysed, 16 sessions of 15-20 minutes over 8 weeks; games included colour-cued stop-go and the Sleeping Game): self-regulation d=0.31, p=0.082, **not significant**; math d=0.38, significant; literacy none. Small sample, only eight teachers, baseline differences; children with low baseline self-regulation gained more (exploratory) ([McClelland et al. 2019, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02365/full)). Teacher-delivered, group format: not a test of parents at home.
- **Harvard Center on the Developing Child guide** (2014 citation; PDFs uploaded 2024), expert-curated with references ([landing page](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/activities-guide-enhancing-and-practicing-executive-function-skills-with-children-from-infancy-to-adolescence/)):
  - 18-36 months: song games with stop-start (Ring Around the Rosie), Head Shoulders Knees and Toes, Follow the Leader, freeze dance with reminders, narrating play, labelling feelings, sorting ([PDF](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-18-to-36-month-olds.pdf)).
  - 3-5 years: imaginary play with play plans (Tools of the Mind), storytelling, freeze dance with poses, fast-slow dancing, yoga with slow breathing ([PDF](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-3-to-5-year-olds.pdf)).
  - 5-7 years: Red Light Green Light, Simon Says, Mother May I?, Magic Word, musical chairs, Duck Duck Goose, Uno, Go Fish, Snap, mancala, checkers, clapping games; adults step back, coin flip to settle disputes ([PDF](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-5-to-7-year-olds.pdf)).
  - 7-12 years: strategy cards and board games including chess, Go, mahjong; sports, jump rope, instruments, dance ([PDF](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-7-to-12-year-olds.pdf)).
- **Conflict on Simon Says:** AAP mentions it for ages 1-3 ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/the-power-of-play-how-fun-and-games-help-children-thrive.aspx)), Harvard for 5-7 ([PDF](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-5-to-7-year-olds.pdf)). A simple copy game fits younger children; the "only when Simon says" rule fits older.
- **Feeling faces:** Make a Face guessing game, mirror game, emotion book, story pause to ask how a character feels; feeling words beyond happy and sad ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_emotions.pdf)). Colour-zone chart and "let it out" zone ([AAP, 19 Dec 2023](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/play-ideas-to-help-your-child-manage-emotions.aspx)). "Zones" may overlap a trademarked curriculum, which I did not check.
- **Role play:** Rehearsing situations such as a doctor visit is said to reduce anxiety, but the AAP page is adapted from a toy company blog, so treat as weak ([AAP/Melissa & Doug](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/pretend-play-ways-children-can-exercise-their-imagination.aspx)). Role-play of tough conversations ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)); practising "stop" and "help" ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/biting-parenting_tool.pdf)); practising time-out when happy ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/time-out.html)).
- **Turn-taking:** Timers, silly drawing, cooperative games ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/helping-siblings-play-together-peacefully.aspx)). Turn-taking games and timers build self-control ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/)).
- **Play and stress:** AAP says research shows play can improve planning, getting along and regulating emotions, and cites one study where anxious preschoolers were twice as likely to feel less stressed after 15 minutes of play than a story; study not named ([AAP, 3 May 2023](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/the-power-of-play-how-fun-and-games-help-children-thrive.aspx)).

### Inferences

- Pick games needing no kit and no reading. Stop-go and freeze games travel well across languages and suit a low-data, mid-day family setting.
- Evidence language in-app should be careful: "games like this are used in preschool programmes" is safer than "builds self-control".

### Gaps

- Could not open Tominey & McClelland 2011, Lillard et al. 2013 on pretend play, PATHS or Tools of the Mind trials, or any parent-delivered game trial. No verified evidence of **Simon Says or Freeze Dance alone** improving behaviour at home.

## What calming tools work for parent and child, and what not to do at 2?

### Takeaway

Co-regulation (a calm adult nearby) is the universal first step and appropriate at every age. Slow breathing is recommended widely, but the only reviews I could read cover adults, so treat it as guidance, not proven for children. Teach it outside the meltdown. At 2, keep to presence, short words and simple movement.

### Cited Findings

- **Order of operations:** Regulate, relate, then reason ([AAP, 2023](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/creating-calm-how-to-talk-with-your-child-when-theyre-stressed.aspx); [AAP, 2024](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)).
- **Parent first:** A few deep breaths, a walk or talk with a friend before responding; a calm presence lowers a child's stress ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/creating-calm-how-to-talk-with-your-child-when-theyre-stressed.aspx)). Pause if you need to, and deal with your own feelings before the child's ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html)). A calm adult helps a child calm faster ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/)).
- **Physical regulation:** soothing voice, get to their height, walking, rocking, music, hugging, jumping jacks, stretching; parallel activities like colouring ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)). Ask first before touch; try a thumbs up or smile instead ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/communication/index.html)). Bear hugs, trampoline, tent, silly walks ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/play-ideas-to-help-your-child-manage-emotions.aspx)).
- **Breathing techniques named by sources:**
  - Slow, long exhalations; belly breathing; 4-7-8; hand on chest and belly. AAP gives no age for these ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)).
  - Slow breaths in and out for a count of three, for anxious children ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/anxiety-in-children/)). Breathing slowly and deeply, counting to 10, clench and unclench fists for anger ([NHS, 24 Apr 2023, review due 24 Apr 2026](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/children-and-young-adults/advice-for-parents/help-your-child-with-anger-issues/)).
  - Hand on stomach, in through nose, out through mouth, together ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/child-care/how-to-recognize-signs-of-distress)). Slow deep breaths with a fearful child ([CPS](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/children_and_fears)).
  - Model it together ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx)). Preschool: deep breath, count to three, then think ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_emotions.pdf)); yoga poses with slow breathing at 3-5 ([Harvard](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-3-to-5-year-olds.pdf)).
- **Evidence:** A systematic review of slow breathing found more heart-rate variability, reduced arousal, anxiety and anger, but **included only adults (18-65)**, and the authors call the evidence limited and heterogeneous ([Zaccaro et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00353/full)). A 2023 breathwork meta-analysis could not be opened ([Sci Rep link](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-27247-y)). **No child RCT of breathing was verified.**
- **Naming feelings:** Part of every emotion-coaching model ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html); [Gottman](https://www.gottman.com/blog/an-introduction-to-emotion-coaching/)); "name it to tame it" is used by AAP ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/why-kids-act-out-tips-to-help-your-child-cope-with-stress.aspx)). Labelling feelings supports regulation at 18-36 months ([Harvard](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-18-to-36-month-olds.pdf)). Parents can guess and let children correct ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/How-to-Shape-Manage-Young-Child-Behavior.aspx)). But naming isn't a must-respond demand: don't require them to talk ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/power-of-play/Pages/play-ideas-to-help-your-child-manage-emotions.aspx)). A stoplight or thermometer helps older children ([Child Mind](https://childmind.org/article/teaching-kids-how-to-deal-with-conflict/)).
- **Teach when calm:** Don't practise strategies during a meltdown; use quiet times ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_emotions.pdf)).
- **What NOT to do at 2:** Teach or reason while they're out of control; expect self-control they don't have; react with anger ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/aggressive-behavior-in-toddlers/)). Demand apologies they don't understand ([ZERO TO THREE](https://www.zerotothree.org/resource/toddlers-and-challenging-behavior-why-they-do-it-and-how-to-respond/)). Rush into problem-solving, or tell them to stop feeling it ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/noticing-and-naming/emotion-coaching.html)). Scold separation protest ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/emotional-development-2-year-olds.aspx)). Rely on screens to calm, which Triple P's article cautions about ([Triple P](https://www.triplep-parenting.net.au/au/articles-and-news/post/how-to-handle-tantrums-try-these-positive-parenting-tips/)).

### Inferences

- Age ladder (mine, untested): **1-2:** parent breathes, child gets your presence and short words. **2-3:** copy parent's big "blow" as a game when calm, without expecting use in a tantrum. **3-5:** one or two slow breaths, stomp, bear hug. **5-8:** belly breath, count to three. **8-12:** longer exhale, picturing stoplight, safe-place imagery, 4-7-8 optional. I found no source setting youngest ages for specific techniques.
- A parent card should always open with a one-line self-calm cue. It is the most consistently cited step.

### Gaps

- Sesame Workshop's belly-breathing activity returned 404 at the redirect. Affect-labelling trials (Lieberman 2007) and child breathing studies (Khng 2017) were not opened. Child Mind's self-regulation article returned 404.

## What phrasing lands, and how does culture change the advice?

### Takeaway

Labelled praise, clear statements instead of questions, two-option choices and when-then framing are consistently recommended, and they fit programme content. **I found no study showing "calm down" backfires**, so don't claim that. Cross-cultural work is the weakest part of this pack: I verified PLH's reach and general caveats, but could not open the Triple P Iran, China, Japan or culturally adapted trial papers.

#### Cross-cultural notes actually verified

- **Physical punishment is common worldwide but harm findings are not limited to the West:** past-month rates vary from 30% to 77% across countries; 49 LMIC analysis found exposed children less likely to be on track ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/20-08-2025-new-report-demonstrates-that-corporal-punishment-harms-children-s-health)). School punishment is high in the Eastern Mediterranean and South-East Asia ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/corporal-punishment-and-health)). Whether normativeness moderates harm was not verified.
- **Programmes do travel:** PLH lists work in Kuwait, Palestine, Malaysia, Thailand, Philippines, China, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, South Africa, Malawi, Mexico, Panama and Guatemala ([PLH](https://parentingforlifelonghealth.org/)). It is built on existing community workers, offline-first mobile delivery for costly data, and co-development with parents and teens in Africa ([PLH impact](https://wordpress.plhdashboard.org/impact)). Its China quasi-experiment report exists, but the page has no findings ([PLH](https://parentingforlifelonghealth.org/china-quasi-experiment-2023/)). PLH did not describe specific cultural changes on the pages I could read.
- **Effects outside Australia:** Li 2021 found larger effects on emotional problems in non-Australian Triple P studies, but only four studies ([Li et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.709851/full)). The 244-trial meta-analysis spans 36 countries ([VU](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/have-parenting-programs-for-disruptive-child-behavior-become-less/)). No culture-level moderator was visible in the abstract.
- **Clinicians are told to consider culture:** PCIT's older-child manual says to explore family cultural identities and values ([WVU](https://sites.google.com/view/wvupcitlab/pcit-resources/pcit-for-older-children)). AAP says what is normal depends partly on family values and cultural background ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Normal-Child-Behavior.aspx)).
- **Multi-generation homes:** Grandparents should match the parents' style, never use physical punishment ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/toddler/Pages/Tips-for-Grandparents-of-a-2-Year-Old.aspx)). All caregivers including grandparents respond alike ([CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/responding-to-behavior/ignoring.html); [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/parenting-toddlers/structure-rules/rules.html); [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/babys-development/play-and-learning/teaching-your-child-everyday-skills/)). CSEFEL uses a grandfather as the model for calm anger ([CSEFEL](https://csefel.vanderbilt.edu/documents/teaching_emotions.pdf)).
- **Sleep norms are culture-bound in these sources:** AAP's advice to keep children out of the parents' bed ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/bedtime-trouble.aspx)) reflects a US norm by my reading. BIÖG treats sleep training as something to consider carefully ([BIÖG](https://www.kindergesundheit-info.de/themen/schlafen/schlafprobleme/)).
- **Faith and meaning:** AACAP includes saying a prayer among remembrance options ([AACAP](https://www.aacap.org/AACAP/Families_and_Youth/Facts_for_Families/FFF-Guide/Children-and-Grief-008.aspx)); AAP includes a trusted religious leader as a parent support ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/Pages/Feeling-Overwhelmed-with-Parenting-Demands.aspx)); it also suggests a relative or family friend can help with sex questions ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Talking-to-Your-Young-Child-About-Sex.aspx)).
- **Home language and shared games:** Harvard encourages home-language stories in bilingual homes and notes clapping games in many cultures, and it names games with non-Western roots such as mancala, Go and mahjong ([Harvard 3-5](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-3-to-5-year-olds.pdf); [Harvard 5-7](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-5-to-7-year-olds.pdf); [Harvard 7-12](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Executive-Function-Activities-for-7-to-12-year-olds.pdf)).

### Inferences

These are design hypotheses to test with local parents and clinicians. **None are verified findings.**

- **Authority and hierarchy:** Avoid framing that sounds like "negotiate with your child". "Firm, warm, and in charge" (BIÖG's friendly but firm no) likely fits settings where a parent's authority is valued. Plan B-style talks might be framed as a parent listening before deciding.
- **Shame:** Sources are universal in rejecting shaming. But culture may see some shame or comparison as ordinary moral teaching. Avoid accusing tone, and offer a positive frame: "our family does X".
- **Eye contact and child-level talk:** CDC's advice to crouch and make eye contact may not fit every setting where children lowering gaze signals respect. Make it "get close and get their attention".
- **Greeting elders:** "Won't say hello" is a higher-stakes issue where greeting elders matters. Offer a graded option (a wave, standing beside you) and a line for the relative.
- **Co-sleeping:** Never frame a shared bed as a problem for ages 1-12 by default. Ask the family's goal, then show the matching path. Keep infant safety rules in the infant section.
- **Multi-generation homes:** Add a "what if grandma disagrees?" line and a one-line script for caregivers. Sources call for consistency but don't say how to raise it with an elder who outranks the parent.
- **Big questions:** Sex and death content needs faith-sensitive and regional variants, and parents should control what's shown.
- **Discipline law:** Do not assume spanking is illegal or legal locally, but never advise it anywhere.
