## 1. Choking: exact current steps per body

### Takeaway
Your understanding is correct: AHA/AAP 2025 changed the child sequence from abdominal thrusts only to repeated cycles of 5 back blows alternating with 5 abdominal thrusts. Bodies still disagree on three points a card cannot blur: ANZCOR uses chest thrusts rather than abdominal thrusts in children, the infant chest-thrust hand position differs by body, and NHS says the order of methods does not matter.

#### What not to do, and anti-choking devices
- **Blind finger sweeps:** RCUK says do not use them, and allows a single sweep only for a clearly visible object ([RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)). NHS says do not poke blindly or repeatedly because it can push the object further in ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/how-to-stop-a-child-from-choking/)). ANZCOR suggests against them ([ANZCOR](https://www.anzcor.org/home/basic-life-support/guideline-4-airway)). American Red Cross says never sweep unless the object is seen ([ARC](https://production.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/infant-choking)).
- **Other don'ts:** do not hang a child upside down by the feet ([ARC](https://production.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/adult-child-choking)). Do not leave a choking child, even with a loud cough ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/how-to-stop-a-child-from-choking/)).
- **Suction devices (LifeVac, Dechoker), RCUK:** current position, reviewed July 2026, makes **no recommendation for or against** while awaiting an updated ILCOR systematic review, and says established treatments should be prioritised because they are immediate and need no equipment ([RCUK position](https://www.resus.org.uk/node/527)). The 2025 guideline text says the same, citing insufficient evidence ([RCUK 2025](https://www.resus.org.uk/library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)). Earlier versions dated May 2024, July 2022, September 2021 and January 2018 are PRIOR ([RCUK position](https://www.resus.org.uk/node/527)).
- **Suction devices, ILCOR:** 2020-21 treatment recommendation found insufficient evidence to recommend either way. Same record: back slaps first, abdominal thrusts in adults and children but not infants, against blind sweeps; all weak recommendations with very low certainty ([ILCOR CoSTR](https://costr.ilcor.org/document/removal-of-foreign-body-airway-obstruction-tfsr-costr)). PRIOR to the 2025 CoSTR, which I could not retrieve.
- **Suction devices, London Ambulance Service:** does not endorse any individual device and supports the RCUK position; updated 10 Aug 2026 ([LAS](https://www.londonambulance.nhs.uk/2026/01/05/london-ambulance-service-statement-on-the-use-of-suction-based-airway-clearing-devices-on-choking-individuals/)).

### Inferences
- Choking must be a region-locked card. Blending, for example AHA's heel-of-hand with RCUK's two thumbs, would produce a protocol no body has published.
- The NHS choking page pre-dates the 2025 round and still teaches two fingers, so UK cards should follow RCUK and St John on technique. Consider reporting the mismatch to NHS content editors.
- Suggested device line for all regions: do not delay back blows and thrusts to find a device. This is my wording drawn from RCUK's "prioritise established treatments", and it needs clinician sign-off.

## 2. CPR for infants and children

### Takeaway
Rate and depth are aligned, but the first action and infant hand position are not. UK, Europe and St John start with 5 rescue breaths; US sources start with compressions. AHA has dropped the two-finger technique and ERC/RCUK use two thumbs, while the NHS page, reviewed May 2026, still teaches two fingers.

### Inferences
- A parent card should show one ratio. 30:2 is the untrained-rescuer figure wherever it could be verified, and 15:2 belongs in training content.
- ANZCOR lay guidance (2021, two-finger) and professional guidance (2026, two-thumb) differ in age as well as content. ANZ cards need an explicit clinician decision.
- Dispatcher instructions override the card. Add "follow the call handler" to every CPR card, since RCUK and ERC both build on dispatcher guidance.

## 3. Burns, poisoning, batteries, magnets, water beads, drowning, and other conditions

### Takeaway
Cool running water for 20 minutes, never making a child vomit, and going straight to hospital for a swallowed battery or magnet are consistent across bodies. The real conflicts are honey for batteries, raising a bleeding limb, cooling during a febrile seizure, and how urgently to act on fever in babies under 3 months.

### Cited Findings

#### Burns and scalds
- **NHS (reviewed 31 Mar 2026; next due 31 Mar 2029):** cool running water for 20 minutes, as soon as possible and within 3 hours. Remove nearby clothing and jewellery, not anything stuck. Cover loosely with cling film, not wrapped round. No creams, oils, butter, plasters or sticky dressings; do not burst blisters. A&E or 999 if very large or deep, on face, genitals or bottom, or from acid, chemical or electricity. Call 111 for advice on an under-5 ([NHS burns](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/)).
- **NHS conflict:** the NHS child accident page (reviewed 18 Dec 2025) says 20 to 30 minutes, and adds no ice, no toothpaste, and that a cool drink such as milk can be used if there is no running water ([NHS accident page](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/what-to-do-if-your-child-has-an-accident/)).
- **ANZCOR 9.1.3 (approved Mar 2023):** at least 20 minutes; may help up to 3 hours after. No ice, lotions, ointments, creams or powders. No cling wrap on the face, round a limb, or on chemical burns. Small children become hypothermic quickly, so keep unburnt areas warm. All infants and children with burns need medical assessment. Ambulance for over 10% of body, or face, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum or major joints, or electrical, chemical or inhalation burns ([ANZCOR 9.1.3](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-1-3-first-aid-for-burns)).
- **St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025):** at least 20 minutes or until pain eases; no ice, creams or gels; cling film lengthways ([St John burns](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/burns-and-scalds/)).
- **AAP (updated 26 Jul 2023):** cool water, no duration given. No ice, butter, grease or powder. Cover with sterile gauze or a clean dry cloth. Immediate care for electrical burns and burns to hands, mouth or genitals ([AAP burns](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Treating-and-Preventing-Burns.aspx)).

#### Poisoning
- **NHS (reviewed 12 Jun 2025):** 999 or A&E if something harmful has been swallowed, touched or breathed in, or if unconscious, not breathing, severe breathing difficulty or seizure. Call 111 if unsure. Do not try to make them sick. Do not give food or drink. Keep packaging and any vomit for staff ([NHS poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/poisoning/)).
- **ANZCOR 9.5.1 (approved Jun 2025; amended Jul 2026):** ambulance early, remove the source, recovery position if unresponsive and breathing, CPR if not breathing normally, contact Poisons Centre. Swallowed: rinse mouth, do not induce vomiting, nothing to neutralise. Eye: flush 15 minutes. One third of cases involve ages 1 to 4 ([ANZCOR 9.5.1](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-5-1-first-aid-management-of-poisoning)).
- **St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025):** call 999/112, keep packaging, do not make them vomit, no food or drink ([St John poisoning](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/poisoning/)).
- **US:** Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/medication-safety/Pages/Poison-Prevention-One-Pill-Can-Kill.aspx)). Numbers by country are in section 5.

Commercial honey. Not a substitute for immediate removal; never delay going to the ER. | Do not wait for symptoms; burns can occur in as little as 2 hours. Do not induce vomiting. Battery Hotline 1-800-498-8666; Poison 1-800-222-1222. Immediate X-ray for age 12 and under. |
| [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/what-parents-need-to-know-about-button-and-lithium-coin-batteries.aspx) (updated 6 Jun 2024) | Over 12 months, 2 teaspoons, up to 6 doses about 10 minutes apart, while waiting for care. Stop if the child vomits. | ER or 911; nothing else to eat or drink. Do not delay care to look for honey. |
| [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Button-Cell-Coin-Battery-Information-Center) (undated) | Repeats NCPC: 12 months and older, within 12 hours, 10 mL every 10 minutes, up to 6 doses, on the way to the ER. | Burns in as little as 2 hours. |
| [ANZCOR 9.5.1](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-5-1-first-aid-management-of-poisoning) (Jun 2025; amended Jul 2026) | Over one: 10 mL honey every 10 minutes, or jam if none. **Under one: 10 mL jam every 10 minutes, no honey.** Nothing else by mouth; do not delay referral. | Seek care immediately. |
| [Kidsafe Australia](https://kidsafe.com.au/button-batteries/) (undated) | **No honey mentioned; says no eating or drinking until X-ray.** Conflicts with ANZCOR 2025-26. | Poisons 13 11 26; 000 if breathing difficulty; do not induce vomiting. |
| [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/what-to-do-if-your-child-has-an-accident/) (reviewed 18 Dec 2025) | **No honey mentioned.** | 999 if swallowed. Battery in nose or ear: leave in place and go to A&E or urgent treatment centre. |

#### Magnets
- NHS: 999 if a magnet is swallowed ([NHS accident page](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/what-to-do-if-your-child-has-an-accident/)).
- AAP (updated 26 Jul 2023): contact pediatrician or closest ED right away. More than one high-powered magnet can be life-threatening. Symptoms such as abdominal pain, vomiting and fever are common in children, so a swallowed magnet may not be recognised right away ([AAP magnets](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/Dangers-of-Magnetic-Toys-and-Fake-Piercings.aspx)).
- CPSC: seek immediate medical attention. Magnets can be up to 8 times stronger than toy magnets, and multiple magnets can look like a single object on X-ray ([CPSC magnets](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Magnets)).

#### Water beads
- CPSC: beads can grow up to 100 times their size. Swallowed: vomiting, dehydration, blockage, possibly surgery. In ear: possible hearing loss. Seek treatment right away, Poison Help 800-222-1222. A 10-month-old died in 2023 ([CPSC water beads](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Water-Beads-Information-Center)).
- AAP (updated 28 Jan 2025): may not show on X-ray. Signs: refusing food, lethargy, drooling, vomiting, wheezing, throat or chest complaint, abdominal pain ([AAP water beads](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/Water-Beads-Harmful.aspx)).

#### Drowning and near-drowning
- **AHA 2025:** after removal from water, CPR with rescue breaths and compressions for all. Start CPR with breaths before the AED. Compressions only if unwilling or unable to give breaths ([AHA interim, §8](https://cpr.heartandstroke.ca/resource/Guidelines2025InterimHeartsaverStudentWorkbookEN)).
- **ERC 2025:** unresponsive and not breathing: 5 rescue breaths, then standard CPR. Bystanders should not enter deep water; throw or reach instead ([ERC lay booklet, p. 58](https://www.erc.edu/media/p5ymaeej/gl2025_layperson_book_ipdf-v11-e.pdf)).
- **ANZCOR 9.3.2 (Nov 2021):** ambulance for everyone involved, even if apparently recovered; compression-only strongly discouraged; do not drain the lungs or press on the stomach; vomiting is common ([ANZCOR 9.3.2](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-3-2-resuscitation-in-drowning)).
- **St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025):** 5 breaths, then 30:2. Anyone who nearly drowned should get medical advice even if they seem well ([St John drowning](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/drowning/)).

#### Head injury red flags
- **NHS (reviewed 29 May 2025):** 999 or A&E for loss of consciousness or cannot stay awake, seizure, fall over 1 metre or 5 stairs, vision or hearing problems, clear fluid or blood from ears or nose, bruising behind ears, new weakness, speech or walking problems, high-speed injury, dent or open wound, **bruise, swelling or large cut in a child under 1**, or noticeable behaviour change. Call 111 for under-5 advice. Adult stays with child at least 24 hours. Child does not need to stay awake if tired ([NHS head injury](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/)).
- **AAP (updated 10 Dec 2024):** 911 for loss of consciousness or worsening at home; call doctor right away for worsening headache, confusion, vomiting more than 2-3 times, unequal pupils, unusual sleepiness and more. Guidance excludes under-2s ([AAP head injury](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Head-Injury.aspx)).
- **ANZCOR 9.1.4 (Oct 2025):** red flags are loss of consciousness, seizures, worsening headache, repeated vomiting, neck pain, penetrating injury ([ANZCOR 9.1.4](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-1-4-head-injury)).
- **St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025):** baby page with minor and severe signs ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/baby-head-injury/)).

#### Febrile seizure
- **NHS (reviewed 21 Jun 2023; OVERDUE since 21 Jun 2026):** note the time, cushion the head, clear hazards, recovery position afterwards. Do not restrain, do not put anything in the mouth, **do not cool by undressing or sponging**. 999 or A&E if first seizure, over 5 minutes, breathing trouble, one side only, unusually sleepy over an hour, or more than one in 24 hours ([NHS febrile seizures](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/febrile-seizures/)).
- **AAP (updated 5 Dec 2025):** floor or bed, turn head to side, nothing in mouth, call the doctor; 911 if not stopped at 5 minutes ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/fever/Pages/Febrile-Seizures.aspx)).
- **ANZCOR 9.2.4 (Jun 2024):** ambulance if first seizure, over 5 minutes, or not conscious within 5 minutes; no restraint; nothing in mouth; **warns against putting a child in a bath during a seizure** ([ANZCOR 9.2.4](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/9-2-4-first-aid-management-of-a-seizure)).
- **St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025):** for a baby, call 999/112, remove bedding and reduce clothing to cool, once the seizure allows; record video if safe. This conflicts with NHS on cooling ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/febrile-seizure/)).

#### Fever in a baby under 3 months
| Source | Threshold and action |
|---|---|
| [NHS fever page](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-children/) (reviewed 3 Jan 2024) | Under 3 months, 38C or higher: **call 111**. 3 to 6 months, 39C or higher: call 111. 999 for stiff neck, non-fading rash, cold hands and feet, hard to wake, breathing trouble and similar. |
| [NHS sepsis page](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sepsis/) (reviewed 14 May 2026) and [NHS meningitis page](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/meningitis/) (reviewed 12 Jun 2026) | The same 38C and 39C thresholds appear under **999 or A&E** lists for under-5s. Stronger than the fever page. |
| [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/fever/Pages/When-to-Call-the-Pediatrician.aspx) (updated 2 Dec 2022) | Under 3 months (12 weeks), 100.4F (38.0C) or higher: **call the doctor right away**. |
| [AAP EMS page](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/When-to-Call-Emergency-Medical-Services-EMS.aspx) (updated 11 May 2026) | Fever of 100.5F or higher under 2 months: seek prompt care, ambulance not necessarily needed. Differs from AAP's own threshold above. |
| [Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne](https://www.rch.org.au/kidsinfo/fact_sheets/Fever_in_children/) (reviewed Mar 2026) | Over 38C: **under 1 month go to hospital**; 1 to under 3 months see a doctor as soon as possible. |
| [Healthdirect Australia](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/fever) (reviewed Oct 2026) | Fever defined as 38C or higher; for a young baby contact a doctor or child health nurse, or call 1800 022 222. |

#### Anaphylaxis
- **NHS (reviewed 21 Jun 2023; OVERDUE):** throat or tongue swelling, breathing or swallowing trouble, wheeze, faint or confused, cold skin, blue, grey or pale skin or lips, sometimes rash. Auto-injector, call 999, second injector after 5 minutes if no better, remove any sting. Lie down with legs raised; sit up slowly if breathing is hard; do not stand or walk ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/)).
- **RCUK 2025 First Aid (27 Oct 2025):** autoinjector to outer thigh as soon as possible; second dose at 5 minutes if symptoms persist, ideally the opposite leg ([RCUK First Aid](https://www.resus.org.uk/library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/first-aid-guidelines)).
- **ANZCOR 9.2.7 (approved 2019):** includes pale and floppy in young children, abdominal pain and vomiting. Dose under 5 years 0.15 mg, over 5 years 0.3 mg ([ANZCOR 9.2.7](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-2-7-first-aid-management-of-anaphylaxis)).
- **St John (reviewed 25 Apr 2025):** similar steps, repeat at 5-minute intervals ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/anaphylaxis/)).

#### Severe bleeding
- **St John (reviewed 8 Sep 2026):** firm direct pressure with a clean non-fluffy cloth; call 999/112 on speaker if alone; do not pull out embedded objects, press either side; add pads on top if soaked ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/severe-bleeding/)).
- **NHS (reviewed 2 Apr 2026):** similar, and **raise the wound above heart level** ([NHS cuts](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/)).
- **ANZCOR 9.1.1 (Apr 2021; editorial update Jul 2025):** bleeding control takes priority; firm pressure; **elevation is not shown to help and may cause harm** ([ANZCOR 9.1.1](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-1-1-first-aid-for-management-of-bleeding)).
- **Tourniquet, RCUK 2025:** as soon as possible for life-threatening limb bleeding not controlled by pressure, 5-7 cm above, not over a joint, do not release ([RCUK First Aid](https://www.resus.org.uk/library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/first-aid-guidelines)). ANZCOR limits it to trained rescuers with one available and gives no child sizing ([ANZCOR 9.1.1](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-1-1-first-aid-for-management-of-bleeding)).

#### Nosebleed
- NHS (reviewed 5 Dec 2023): lean forward, pinch just above nostrils 10-15 minutes, breathe through mouth. A&E if longer, excessive, or after a head blow ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/nosebleed/)).
- AAP (updated 30 Sep 2019; old): slightly forward, 10 minutes, do not look during that time, no tissue in nose; call if a second attempt fails ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/How-to-Stop-a-Nosebleed.aspx)).
- St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025): 10 minutes, repeat twice; 999/112 if severe or over 30 minutes; do not lean back ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/nosebleed/)).

#### Broken bone
- St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025): 999/112 for open or long-bone fractures or suspected back, neck or pelvis. Support above and below. Do not raise the legs if leg, hip or pelvis injured ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/fractures-and-broken-bones/)).
- AAP (updated 5 May 2026): improvised sling or splint; nothing by mouth; no ice on babies or toddlers; do not push bone back; 911 for a broken leg ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Children-And-Broken-Bones.aspx)).
- NHS (reviewed 18 Dec 2025): neck, spine or pelvis: 999 and do not move. Cannot be moved without pain: 999 ([NHS accident page](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/what-to-do-if-your-child-has-an-accident/)).

#### Breathing difficulty red flags
- NHS (reviewed 19 Feb 2026): 999 for grunting, tummy sucking under ribs, fast breathing, pauses, blue or grey skin, tongue or lips, or floppy and will not wake. On brown or black skin, check palms, soles, lips, gums and inside eyelids ([NHS bronchiolitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bronchiolitis/)).
- AAP (updated 11 May 2026): EMS for difficulty breathing or speaking, or blue, purple or gray skin or lips ([AAP EMS](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/When-to-Call-Emergency-Medical-Services-EMS.aspx)).
- ERC 2025: struggling, added noises, too fast or slow, cannot speak or cry; in darker skin check palms and soles ([ERC lay booklet, p. 34](https://www.erc.edu/media/p5ymaeej/gl2025_layperson_book_ipdf-v11-e.pdf)).
- ANZCOR 9.2.5 asthma (Jul 2023): 4 puffs, 4 breaths each, wait 4 minutes, repeat, ambulance if no better (AU 4x4x4; **NZ 6x6x6**) ([ANZCOR 9.2.5](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-2-5-first-aid-for-asthma)).

#### Dehydration
- NHS (reviewed 1 May 2026): babies: sunken soft spot, sunken eyes, few or no tears, fewer wet nappies, unusually drowsy or irritable. 111 for those; 999 if also blue, grey or blotchy, cold, breathing trouble, confused or hard to wake ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dehydration/)).
- AAP: EMS for sunken eyes, no tears or urination, and lethargy ([AAP EMS](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/When-to-Call-Emergency-Medical-Services-EMS.aspx)).

#### Meningitis signs
- NHS (reviewed 12 Jun 2026): babies and under-5s: not feeding, high-pitched cry, stiff or floppy or unresponsive, bulging soft spot. Glass test: spots stay visible through a pressed glass. On brown or black skin check palms, soles and inside eyelids. Do not wait for all symptoms ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/meningitis/)).
- St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025): 999/112 immediately ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/meningitis/)).

#### Sepsis signs
- NHS (reviewed 14 May 2026): fast or laboured breathing or grunting, seizures, blue, grey, pale or mottled skin, non-fading rash, unusual drowsiness, no urine in 12 hours under 5, refusing feeds, persistent vomiting, weak or high-pitched cry, temperature below 36C. Do not drive to A&E ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sepsis/)).
- UK Sepsis Trust (undated): rapid breathing, fit, mottled, bluish or pale skin, non-fading rash, very drowsy, unusually cold. Under 5: not feeding, repeated vomiting, no urine for 12 hours. Phrase: "Just Ask: Could it be Sepsis?" ([UK Sepsis Trust](https://sepsistrust.org/about/about-sepsis/)).
- ANZCOR 9.2.3 (Dec 2025): first aiders need not diagnose sepsis and should act on signs of serious illness. A parent's worry is itself a red flag ([ANZCOR 9.2.3](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-2-3-shock-first-aid-management-of-the-seriously-ill-or-injured-person)).

#### Heatstroke and hot car
- NHS (reviewed 28 May 2026): move somewhere cool, remove excess clothing, water, cool the skin and fan. 999 if not better in 30 minutes, very high temperature, hot non-sweating skin, confusion, seizure or loss of consciousness ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/)).
- AAP (updated 28 Jan 2026): 911 at once, cool (not cold) bath ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/extreme-temperature-exposure.aspx)).
- ANZCOR 9.3.4 (Sep 2020): 5 years and under, tepid bath with frequent sponging, otherwise moisten skin and fan. Over 5, cold immersion from the neck down for 15 minutes is most effective ([ANZCOR 9.3.4](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-3-4-heat-induced-illness-hyperthermia)).
- RCUK 2025: whole-body cold water immersion is the gold standard; "cool first, transfer second" ([RCUK First Aid](https://www.resus.org.uk/library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/first-aid-guidelines)). No child-specific limit was seen on this page.
- Hot car, AAP (updated 22 Apr 2025): child unresponsive or in pain: call 911, get the child out, spray with cool water, no ice bath. Responsive: stay until help comes, find the driver. Children heat 3-5 times faster than adults; 20F rise in 10 minutes; cracking a window does little ([AAP hot cars](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/on-the-go/Pages/Prevent-Child-Deaths-in-Hot-Cars.aspx)).

#### Electric shock
- NHS (reviewed 18 Dec 2025): turn off power; if you cannot, push child away with a wooden or plastic object. No response: 999 and CPR ([NHS accident page](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/what-to-do-if-your-child-has-an-accident/)).
- AAP (updated 26 Sep 2019; old): power off first, no bare-hand contact, move child as little as possible, medical evaluation for every shock. Mouth burns from a cord can bleed hours or days later ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Electric-Shock.aspx)).

#### Dog bite
- NHS (reviewed 27 Oct 2025): wash with soap and warm water, press if bleeding heavily, dry, cover. 111 for bites on hands, feet, face or head, debris, infection signs, or tetanus not within 10 years. A&E if large, deep or will not stop bleeding. No aspirin under 16 ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/animal-and-human-bites/)).
- St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025): similar, plus rabies risk as a reason for medical help ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/human-and-animal-bites/)).
- AAP: bites that break the skin need prompt care ([AAP EMS](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/When-to-Call-Emergency-Medical-Services-EMS.aspx)).

#### Tooth knocked out
- AAP (updated 7 Oct 2026): baby tooth, do not replace. Permanent tooth: hold by crown, rinse gently with milk or saline, **not tap water**, reinsert if comfortable, bite on gauze. Otherwise store in milk, saline or saliva, never tap water. Best within 15 minutes, may still be saved within the first hour ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/First-Aid-for-a-Knocked-Out-Permanent-Tooth.aspx)).
- IADT (undated): best within first 20 minutes. Hold by crown, **rinse briefly in water**, replant, bite on gauze, otherwise milk or saliva. Never place a baby tooth back ([IADT](https://iadt-dentaltrauma.org/knocked-out/)). Conflicts with AAP on water rinse.

#### Eye injury
- NHS (reviewed 12 Mar 2026): chemical: rinse with clean, not hot, water at least 20 minutes, holding eye open. Pierced: do not remove, 999 or A&E. High-speed hit, vision change, severe pain, blood or pus: A&E or 999 ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/eye-injuries/)).
- St John (reviewed 28 Apr 2025): chemical at least 20 minutes, do not rub, do not remove embedded objects, cover with pad ([St John](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/eye-injury/)).
- ANZCOR poison eye exposure: 15 minutes ([ANZCOR 9.5.1](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-5-1-first-aid-management-of-poisoning)).

#### Stings
- NHS (reviewed 1 Jun 2023; OVERDUE): brush or scrape stinger sideways, not tweezers. Wash, cold compress at least 20 minutes. 999 for lip, mouth, throat or tongue swelling, breathing trouble, or a limp child ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insect-bites-and-stings/)).
- ANZCOR 9.4.3 (Nov 2021): scrape bee sting without compressing the sac; cold compress; ambulance for multiple stings to the face or tongue, or anaphylaxis history ([ANZCOR 9.4.3](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-4-3-envenomation-from-tick-bites-and-bee-wasp-and-ant-stings)).

### Inferences
- **Burns:** 20 minutes is the safe common figure. NHS's second page saying 20-30 is the outlier, and a UK card should confirm with a clinician.
- **Honey:** this requires a regional decision. UK-sourced cards should not add honey, since neither NHS nor St John pages read mention it. The under-1 rule is absolute wherever honey appears. Jam under 1 is ANZCOR-only.
- **Fever:** show "38C or higher under 3 months = get help now" with the regional route. The NHS fever and sepsis pages route differently, so clinicians should pick the route for UK.
- **Cooling during seizures:** the safest card text is what most bodies agree on: do not restrain, nothing in the mouth, time it. Do not add cooling steps.
- **Bleeding:** omit "raise the limb" for ANZ. For UK it remains on the NHS page, so clinician choice is needed.
- **Colour signs:** add a skin-tone note wherever colour is a sign, since NHS, ERC and UK Sepsis Trust all do so.

### Gaps
- NICE NG143 (fever) returned 403, so its thresholds are NOT VERIFIED directly.
- CAPT refused connections, so its button-battery guidance is NOT VERIFIED. No UK primary source on honey was found. Web search was unavailable by then.
- ERC First Aid 2025 chapter: only the RCUK 2025 First Aid page and the lay booklet were read. The RCUK page did not cover burns, seizures, general poisoning, nosebleed or dental avulsion.
- 2024 AHA/ARC First Aid Guidelines: abstract only ([record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1161/CIR.0000000000001281&format=json&resultType=core)). Burn, bleeding and child-specific text NOT VERIFIED.
- St John electric shock page and NHS knocked-out-tooth page were not found at the URLs tried.
- National Capital Poison Center magnet and water-bead pages were not found at the URLs tried.

## 4. Triage wording: ambulance vs urgent care vs doctor, and what can be reused

### Takeaway
NHS.uk and AAP triage wording could be verified. Healthier Together's red/amber/green cards and Raising Children could not, as both returned 403. NHS website text is the only medical source here with an open licence, and its conditions are awkward for an offline app.

### Cited Findings
- **NHS.uk structure:** condition pages use tiered callouts, seen as "Immediate action required" (999) and urgent advice (urgent GP or 111) ([NHS bronchiolitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bronchiolitis/)). Common lines: "Do not drive to A&E" ([NHS sepsis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sepsis/)).
- **NHS service definitions:** 999 is for life-threatening emergencies, reviewed 3 Feb 2023, OVERDUE since Feb 2026 ([NHS 999](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/)). A&E is for serious injuries and life-threatening emergencies ([NHS A&E](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-go-to-ae/)). 111 is for medical help right now; **111 online is for age 5 and over, so call 111 for under-5s**; reviewed 14 Nov 2022, OVERDUE ([NHS 111](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-use-111/)).
- **NHS child list (reviewed 18 Dec 2025):** 999 if stopped breathing, struggling to breathe or choking; unconscious, limp or will not stay awake; seizure; blue, grey or pale; swollen mouth or throat; heavy bleeding; severe injury; swallowed poison, too much medicine, button battery or magnet. A&E or urgent treatment centre if cannot use a limb, or battery in nose or ear ([NHS accident page](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/what-to-do-if-your-child-has-an-accident/)).
- **AAP two tiers (updated 11 May 2026):** "Call EMS immediately" and "Seek prompt medical care" without necessarily calling an ambulance. Also advises having your address ready because mobile calls cannot always be traced ([AAP EMS](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/When-to-Call-Emergency-Medical-Services-EMS.aspx)).
- **Australian wording:** 000 for struggling to breathe, blue or very pale, cannot be woken, first-ever seizure, seizure over 5 minutes ([RCH Melbourne](https://www.rch.org.au/kidsinfo/fact_sheets/Fever_in_children/)). Healthdirect line 1800 022 222 ([Healthdirect](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/fever)).
- **Spotting the Sick Child is not a parent resource.** It is an RCPCH course for clinicians and requires registration; no licence is stated on the page ([Spotting the Sick Child](https://spottingthesickchild.com/)).
- **Healthier Together:** what0-18.nhs.uk now redirects to healthiertogether.nhs.uk, which returned 403 ([Healthier Together](https://www.healthiertogether.nhs.uk/)).

#### How to read the table
- `general` is an all-services number. Other columns are service-specific. A slash means alternatives.
- `check` values:
  - `agree`: FCDO and Canada do not contradict each other.
  - `UK-only` or `CA-only`: one source only.
  - `complementary`: one lists a general number and the other lists different specific numbers. Both may be valid. Service columns may mix sources. Verify.
  - `CONFLICT`: both give numbers for the same service that do not overlap, general numbers differ, or one body states no such service exists. FCDO values are shown, with Canada in `gac_alt`. **Do not ship without local verification.**
  - `NONE`: no central number found, or the source states there is none.
- `iso2` is ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, with these exceptions: `XK` (Kosovo, user-assigned), and subdivision or non-standard labels `ES-CN`, `PT-AZ`, `AQ-BAT`.
- Counts: 117 agree, 37 UK-only, 11 CA-only, 34 complementary, 30 CONFLICT, 9 NONE, 1 checked against primary (UK).
- FCDO warns that some services are unreliable, for example Mauritania, Sudan, Liberia, Equatorial Guinea and Central African Republic. Consider a "may not answer" flag.

### Inferences
- Display the `general` number first, then the ambulance number, with one-tap dialling. Bundle the table offline, and show its data date.
- Ask the user to confirm their country at setup instead of relying on location. Apple's guidelines restrict using location-based APIs to provide emergency services ([Apple 5.1.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
- Review the table at least once a year. FCDO pages change independently.

### Gaps
- No single primary, machine-readable global list was reachable. The table is a merged derivative of two travel-advice services, and its 30 conflicts cannot be resolved from what I could read.
- Some rows may contain parsing errors, even after manual fixes to about 25 rows. Sample QA is required.
- Regional and US state poison numbers were not expanded in this table. Canada has provincial numbers only here: Health Canada's page returned 404, so a national line is NOT VERIFIED.

## 7. App-store and legal constraints; how reputable apps present first aid

### Takeaway
First-aid reference apps are on the safer side of both FDA and MHRA guidance, but interactive symptom-to-action flows may not be. Apple and Google each require specific reminders or disclaimers, and MHRA warns that a disclaimer does not rescue an app that qualifies as a device.

### Cited Findings

#### Store rules
- **Apple 1.4.1:** medical apps that could give inaccurate information or be used for diagnosis or treatment get greater scrutiny, and should remind users to check with a doctor before making medical decisions. Apple 1.4.2 covers dosage calculators, which must come from an approved source. Apple 5.1.5 says location APIs should not be used to provide emergency services ([Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
- **Apple Kids Category (1.3):** no links out or purchases without a parental gate; restricted third-party analytics and ads ([Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). A parent-facing app may not need this category; that is a product decision.
- **Google Play, Health Content and Services:** health apps declaration and privacy policy. Non-regulated health apps must disclose they are not a medical device and do not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition, and remind users to consult a healthcare professional. Misleading claims against medical consensus are prohibited ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511)). Page undated; read through the summarising tool, so re-check the exact wording.

#### Medical-device position
- **FDA (current as of 29 Sep 2022):** lists an "encyclopedia of first-aid or emergency care information", first-aid or CPR tutorials and training videos, and lists of emergency hotlines as examples of non-devices ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-software-functions-including-mobile-medical-applications/examples-software-functions-are-not-medical-devices)).
- **MHRA (main guidance v1.10g; Great Britain; last updated 1 Jul 2023 on the landing page):** a disclaimer such as "for information only" or "not a medical device" does not help if the app qualifies or makes implied medical claims. Description, store listing and testimonials count toward intended purpose ([MHRA guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6abf6c8c943e890cb55e5e52/Medical_device_stand-alone_software_including_apps__including_IVDMDs.pdf); [MHRA landing page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/medical-devices-software-applications-apps)).
- **MHRA symptom-checker appendix (v1.10f):** for software where lay users enter symptoms. *May be a device:* outputs a matching subset, likelihood, **treatment recommendations "e.g. first aid treatment"**, or filters by red flag. *Unlikely to be a device:* offers only reference information, lists all matches independent of likelihood, or only signposts to care ([MHRA Appendix 1](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64a7d235c531eb000c650051/Appendix_1_-_symptom_checkers.pdf)).

### Inferences
- **Stay "reference and signpost":** static cards chosen by a named emergency, each with a call action. Avoid "enter symptoms, get an action or severity result" unless you accept possible device classification.
- **Be careful with a red/amber/green feature.** A static list of signs is closer to reference content. A quiz that outputs a colour is closer to MHRA's description of a device. Take regulatory advice before building one.
- **Avoid dosing.** No paracetamol or ibuprofen calculator (Apple 1.4.2 and MHRA decision-support). Be careful stating adrenaline doses; follow the device and prescriber.
- **Suggested disclaimer structure** (my draft, needs legal review): "In an emergency, call [number] first. These cards are general first aid information, not medical advice, and do not replace a clinician or the call handler's instructions. Based on [body, version, date]. PlayNest is not a medical device and does not diagnose or treat any condition."
- Display at card level: source body, version, last clinical review date.

## 8. Suggested card set, structure and illustrations

### Takeaway
Sixteen cards are suggested, ordered by time-critical risk to life and by how likely a parent is to need them. Four of them (CPR and choking, baby and child) need region-locked steps, and original artwork is needed because the sources' images cannot be reused.

### Cited Findings
- Time-critical evidence behind the order: battery burns in as little as 2 hours ([NCPC](https://www.poison.org/battery/guideline)); 999-tier list ([NHS accident page](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/what-to-do-if-your-child-has-an-accident/)); EMS list ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/When-to-Call-Emergency-Medical-Services-EMS.aspx)); drowning in ages 1-4 ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/drowning)).
- Peer pattern: British Red Cross separates a quick "Emergency" section with some timers from "Learn" content, and works offline ([BRC app](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/baby-and-child-first-aid/id646471621)). American Red Cross includes a CPR metronome and emergency call integration ([ARC app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/first-aid-american-red-cross/id529160691)).
- Images: NHS excludes visuals from its licence ([NHS terms](https://www.nhs.uk/our-policies/terms-and-conditions/)); ANZCOR figures are CC BY-NC ([G12.1](https://www.anzcor.org/home/paediatric-advanced-life-support/guideline-12-1-paediatric-basic-life-support-pbls-for-health-professionals)); St John and British Red Cross reserve rights.

### Inferences
Everything in this section beyond the citations above is my suggestion, for clinical and editorial review.

#### Card anatomy, maximum 6 steps
1. Title and one-line trigger ("Can't cough, cry or breathe").
2. Call banner with one-tap local number, always first or second.
3. Up to 6 numbered imperative steps, one action each, in a single body's wording.
4. One "Do not" line.
5. "Get seen even if better" line where sources say so.
6. Footer: source body, version, reviewed date.

- **Second-tier reference, reachable but not on the emergency home screen:** broken bone, nosebleed, electric shock, dog bite, tooth knocked out, eye injury, stings, dehydration.
- **Must fit in 6 steps:** CPR is the tight one. RCUK's sequence fits if grouped: safe and respond; call on speaker; open airway and check up to 10 s; 5 breaths; 30:2; AED when it arrives, and follow the call handler. Confirm per region.
- **Region selection rule:** UK uses RCUK, NHS and St John. Europe uses ERC. US uses AHA, AAP and American Red Cross. Canada uses Heart & Stroke and AHA. Australia and NZ use ANZCOR. Elsewhere, choose one explicitly and state it on the card; WHO's 2013 chart is hospital guidance and too old to default to.
- **Do not auto-select by locale without confirmation.** A UK parent travelling should keep UK steps, with local numbers swapped.
- **Re-review triggers:** ILCOR's pending suction-device review, per [RCUK](https://www.resus.org.uk/node/527); AHA's final 2025 manuals; NHS updates to infant technique; overdue NHS pages for febrile seizure, anaphylaxis and stings.
- **Commission original illustrations,** reviewed by a clinician, since infant hand position differs by region and sources' images are not reusable.
