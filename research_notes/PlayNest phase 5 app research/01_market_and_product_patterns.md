> **Method and reliability. Read this first.**
> - Research date: 4 November 2026. This extends `docs/RESEARCH-2.md` section 1 and does not repeat it.
> - **The shared WebSearch budget ran out partway through (limit 200 per turn across all agents).** After that I only opened direct pages that had already surfaced or whose URL I knew. I ran no more searches, so some planned sweeps did not happen. They are listed under Gaps.

## 1. What do the current products actually do, and what do parents praise and hate?

### Takeaway
Three shifts since the earlier research:
- **Convergence.** Play apps are adding trackers and trackers are adding guides, so "everything apps" are getting more alike.
- **AI chat is now a standard paid feature**, and it is cloud-based.
- **Offline is still an afterthought** at several leaders (Huckleberry, Good Inside).

The products getting attention for calm (Tiimo, Readmio, Yoto, Moshi) share one idea: set things up once, and then daily use is small, quiet and needs little attention. That leaves PlayNest's "one big pick" as a real point of difference, as long as it does not grow into a dashboard.

### Inferences
- **Convergence is real and PlayNest should not chase it.** Kinedu added a tracker, Nara added guides, and Bebbo added Quick Links. Each step probably makes those home screens busier. A single "one big pick" gets more distinctive as they do this.
- **Offline is still a differentiator.** Huckleberry only now added an offline banner, and Good Inside's offline gap is a reviewer request. Even Bebbo's offline-first model has data-loss reviews, so offline only counts if data survives.
- **Attachment raises the cost of failure.** Finch's angriest reviews are about losing progress, and Bebbo's about lost records. PlayNest's garden and journal live on one device, so backup is a trust feature rather than a settings detail.
- **The most valuable interaction pattern in this set is "adult curates, child or daily use is tiny".** See Yoto Green Button, Goally photo steps and Readmio.

## 2. What leads outside English, and what do those products offer that English apps do not?

### Takeaway
I could not establish a verified "market leader" for most countries. What I could verify points to six things English apps mostly lack:
- messaging-first delivery
- free state or UN programmes
- kin-like and faith-aware framing
- physical keepsakes
- childcare-integrated channels
- culturally rooted art

For PlayNest the most usable are WhatsApp sharing, printables and culturally rooted content. Persian and Arabic look underserved by quality play products, but my evidence there is thin.

## 4. Which retention mechanics work without guilt?

### Takeaway
Streaks do work, and that is why they are dangerous. The evidence supports three things: highlight what exists rather than what was missed, let people rest or repair without penalty, and keep nudges to a moderate dose. A guilt-free design is achievable, but I found no controlled evidence that it retains as well as Duolingo-style pressure. PlayNest should accept that trade openly.

### Cited Findings
- **Streak effects are mostly in the display.** Across seven studies, people were more likely to repeat a behaviour when an intact streak was highlighted in their log than a broken one, and the effect depends on how the log represents behaviour rather than on actual past behaviour. It is **stronger when people blame themselves** for the break and **weaker when they can repair** it — [Silverman & Barasch, JCR 2023, abstract only](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/49/6/1095/6590647).
- **Duolingo's own published numbers (31 Jan 2022, company data).** The measured experiment effects are small. Streak animations gave +1.7% day-7 retention for new learners. A 7-day streak meant 3.6× course completion (correlational). Equipping up to two Streak Freezes gave +0.38% daily actives "without encouraging more days off" — [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/).
- **Backlash.** A marketing professor describes being flooded with notifications after a 52-day streak broke. Duolingo defends guilt-tinged notifications as effective. The same article cites a 2010 study where guilt appeals provoked defensiveness — [Business Insider NL](https://businessinsider.nl/duolingo-is-mean-whiny-and-annoying-gen-z-loves-it/) (snippet; direct fetch blocked). Notifications are compared to a "toxic ex", though many say reminders help — [The Sun Malaysia](https://thesun.my/going-viral/netizens-say-duolingo-notifications-feel-like-toxic-ex-texts) (snippet). I found **no peer-reviewed 2026 study** on Duolingo guilt.
- **Apple retrofitted forgiveness.** watchOS 11 (10 Jun 2024) lets users pause rings "for a day, week, month, or more" without affecting award streaks, and set goals per weekday — [Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/06/watchos-11-brings-powerful-health-and-fitness-insights/).
- **Finch is not simply "guilt free".**
  - Reviewers describe not wanting to let the bird down; one 2023 review calls it too cute to disappoint. It also has visible streaks, and streak loss drives the angriest reviews — [Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/finch-app-reviews-emotional-attachment-user-retention-product-loyalty/).
  - One reviewer says users "don't get penalized" for incomplete goals, but this is not a stated developer policy — [listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/finch-self-care-pet/id1528595748).
  - No retention figures are public — [Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/finch-app-reviews-emotional-attachment-user-retention-product-loyalty/).
- **Dose evidence specifically for parents.** In an early-childhood text experiment, **one text a week and five texts a week were each less effective than a set of three** (information, actionable advice, encouragement). A single weekly activity lowered literacy scores 0.15 SD for the lowest pre-treatment quarter relative to the original programme, five a week showed no positive literacy effects, and higher-scoring quarters did marginally better with one tip a week, so the dose is not one-size-fits-all — [Cortes et al., NBER 2018](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24827).
- **Calm technology.** Principles include "require the smallest possible amount of attention", use the periphery, keep working when it fails, use the minimum technology. Calm Tech Institute launched May 2024, with no 2025–26 data on the page — [calmtech.com](https://calmtech.com/).
- **Parent sentiment on load.** 58% of US parents say they are "doing the best they can" on screen time and 42% say they could do better — [Pew, 8 Oct 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/10/08/how-parents-manage-screen-time-for-kids/). On Mumsnet, trackers cut both ways: "helped me feel more in control" against "fuelled my PND" and "making me more anxious" — [Mumsnet thread sample](https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/parenting/4261897-best-baby-tracking-apps) (snippet; quotes span several threads, many over a year old).
- **Caregiver check-ins can help the caregiver without changing parenting**: audio-only calls in Jordan reduced depressive symptoms (2023) — [NYU](https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/news/children-affected-syrian-refugee-crisis-are-effectively-learning-numbers-letters-and-emotional).
- **Signals of what is rewarded.** Tiimo's listing has no streak language and it won App of the Year — [listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tiimo-daily-ai-planner-to-do/id1480220328). Structured prioritises breaks — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/). Bebbo is itself planning gamified elements — [UNICEF posting](https://jobs.unicef.org/es/job/581354/homebased-bebbo-mobile-technology-consultant-160-working-days-europe-and-central-asia-regional-office-ecaro) (snippet).

### Inferences

**Rules for PlayNest (my judgement from the above):**
1. **Count only upward.** Show flowers planted, never days missed. Past flowers stay.
2. **No visible run of consecutive days at all**, including "positive" ones. A run creates the broken state that causes the harm.
3. **Rest is a state, not a failure.** Offer "Garden is resting" for illness, travel or a new baby, and auto-rest after silence without asking.
4. **Dose: at most three optional prompts a week**, on a day and time the parent picks. Then stop entirely after about two weeks of no response, until they return. The three-a-week figure comes from one experiment on texts, so test it rather than treat it as settled.
5. **Pip never suffers.** Pip is never sad, hungry, waiting or lonely because a parent was busy.
6. **Back up early.** The more people care, the more loss hurts.

**Copy.** Avoid: "You missed…", "Noor is waiting", "Don't lose your…", "Pip misses you", "It's been 5 days", "Only 2 minutes!".

Use these patterns instead:
- "A rainy-afternoon idea for Noor, if today has room."
- "Your garden kept every flower. Here's one small game for whenever you're ready."
- "Busy week? Nothing to catch up on."
- "Three minutes in the car counts. So does not playing today."

**Honest trade-off.** Dropping streaks probably gives up some retention. I found no evidence quantifying that for parent apps. Local-only analytics cannot measure it well either, so the decision rests on values and on fit with `docs/EXPANSION.md` principle 10.

### Gaps
- **Search budget ended before I could collect:** JMIR scoping reviews of parenting-app engagement (2025–26), parental-burnout research, Surgeon General's *Parents Under Pressure* (direct fetch 403), Headspace streak changes, Baumel 2019 app-retention base rates (fetch returned no content), ICO Children's Code nudge standard (fetch 403).
- No controlled trial found comparing guilt-free and streak designs in a parent app.

## 6. Top 15 recommendations and the "do not build" list

### Takeaway
Do the small, server-free things first: context on Today, share, print, a forgiving garden and backup. These are mostly exposing data PlayNest already has. Be wary of matching rivals on trackers, AI chat or audio libraries, where they are ahead and PlayNest's constraints count against it.

Repo state for sizing: see the repo check under Q3.
