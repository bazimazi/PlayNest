## Normal crying, the named programmes, the walk-away wording, and abusive head trauma data

### Takeaway
Crying that builds from about 2 weeks, peaks around 6 to 8 weeks and eases by 3 to 4 months is normal, and every major source agrees it is safe to put the baby down and step away, though they differ on how long (from "a few minutes" to a 5 to 10 minute check). Education programmes reliably raise knowledge and walk-away behaviour, but evidence that they reduce abusive head trauma (AHT) is mixed, so the app must not claim they do.

### Cited Findings

**How much crying is normal**
- Diary meta-analysis of 28 studies and 8,690 infants: mean fuss/cry 117 to 133 minutes a day in the first 6 weeks and 68 minutes at 10 to 12 weeks. Colic prevalence was 17% to 25% in the first 6 weeks, 11% at 8 to 9 weeks and 0.6% at 10 to 12 weeks. — [Wolke 2017, J Pediatr](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28385295/)
- Newborns typically cry about 1 to 4 hours a day. — [AAP HealthyChildren, updated 2025-02-20](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Responding-to-Your-Babys-Cries.aspx)
- Normal fussiness peaks around 3 hours a day at 6 weeks and declines by 3 to 4 months. About 1 in 5 babies develop colic, usually starting at weeks 2 to 4. It usually stops by 3 to 4 months and can last to 6 months. — [AAP Colic, updated 2024-04-22](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Colic.aspx)
- Colic working definition: more than 3 hours a day, 3 days a week, for at least a week, in an otherwise healthy baby. — [NHS Colic, reviewed 2022-04-26, next review was due 2025-04-26, so overdue](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/colic/)

**Sources disagree on the timing of the peak**
- ICON: crying becomes more frequent from about 2 weeks (for premature babies, about 2 weeks after the due date) and peaks at about 6 to 8 weeks. It eases after 8 weeks, and after about 5 months crying is more likely to have a clear reason. — [ICON](https://iconcope.org/advice-for/parents/infant-crying-is-normal/)
- Barr: the normal crying curve peaks at 5 to 6 weeks. — [Barr 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16406023/)
- The NHS page, as read by the fetch tool, says crying often peaks around 2 weeks and eases by about 3 months. — [NHS, reviewed 2026-04-22](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/soothing-a-crying-baby/). This conflicts with the sources above and may be a summarising error, so check the live page before quoting it.
- CDC describes peak crying as about 4 to 20 weeks of age. — [CDC, updated 2024-05-16](https://www.cdc.gov/child-abuse-neglect/about/about-abusive-head-trauma.html)

**PURPLE, Click for Babies, ICON and Cry-sis**
- PURPLE letters: Peak pattern (rises and peaks in the second month), Unexpected timing, Resistance to soothing, Pain-like look, Long bouts, Evening clustering. — [Barr 2009, CMAJ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2659818/)
- purplecrying.info and clickforbabies.org now redirect to the National Center on Shaken Baby Syndrome's page. Click for Babies is a public campaign in which communities donate purple caps to accompany the materials. — [dontshake.org](https://dontshake.org/purple-crying)
- ICON's four messages are "Infant crying is normal", "Comforting methods can help", "It's OK to walk away" and "Never, ever shake a baby". ICON is managed by a collaborative of health trusts, local authorities and volunteers and is endorsed by the RCGP, and its About page gives no outcome data. — [ICON](https://iconcope.org/), [ICON About](https://iconcope.org/about/)
- Cry-sis supports parents of babies who cry excessively or sleep poorly (founded 1981). Helpline is 0800 448 0737, open 9am to 10pm, 7 days, with a bookable call-back out of hours. — [Cry-sis](https://www.cry-sis.org.uk/)

**Abusive head trauma data**
- AHT is a leading cause of child-abuse death under 5 in the US and about one-third of all maltreatment deaths. Up to 1 in 4 babies affected die, and nearly all survivors have serious long-term effects. Babies under 1 are at greatest risk. — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/child-abuse-neglect/about/about-abusive-head-trauma.html)
- The incidence curve starts at 2 to 3 weeks and peaks at 10 to 13 weeks, about 4 to 6 weeks after the crying peak, in 273 California hospitalisations under 18 months. The authors call this convergent evidence for crying as a trigger. — [Barr 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16406023/)
- UK: ICON cites about 24 per 100,000 (Barlow and Minns 2000, via NSPCC) and about 200 children killed or hurt each year, likely an underestimate. — [ICON](https://iconcope.org/advice-for/parents/never-ever-shake-a-baby/). The fetch summary says "hospital admissions"; check the denominator before reuse.
- North Carolina averaged about 34 cases per 100,000 person-years under age 1 before the prevention programme. — [Zolotor 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4687484)

**Prevention evidence is mixed**
- RCT of 1,279 mothers: PURPLE materials raised crying knowledge (63.8 v 58.4). Walk-away during inconsolable crying rose (0.067 v 0.039 per day, rate ratio 1.7, 95% CI 1.1 to 2.6; with imputed data, 1.5, 1.0 to 2.2). Sharing walk-away advice with other caregivers rose (51.5% v 38.5%). — [Barr 2009, CMAJ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2659818/)
- RCT of 2,738 mothers: knowledge 69.5 v 63.3, with responses to crying similar between groups. — [Barr 2009, Pediatrics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19255028/)
- Western New York hospital programme: AHT fell 47% (41.5 to 22.2 per 100,000 live births) against historical controls. — [Dias 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15805350/)
- Pennsylvania statewide programme, 1.59 million infants: no reduction (24.1 to 26.6 per 100,000 aged 0 to 23 months, compared with 22.4 to 22.0 in five comparison states). Only 20.6% of parents saw both the brochure and video. — [Dias 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28135348/). This contradicts Dias 2005.
- North Carolina: no significant change in AHT (P = .81), but nurse-line calls about crying fell 20% under 3 months. The authors note the study may have been underpowered. — [Zolotor 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4687484)
- An advocacy-site summary reports a British Columbia evaluation with 35% fewer AHT hospitalisations. This was not checked against the paper, so treat it as unverified. — [dontshake.org](https://dontshake.org/play-for-prevention/itemlist/tag/bc)

### Inferences
- Recommended app wording, consistent with CDC and NHS: put baby on their back in the cot, leave the room, set a 5 to 10 minute timer, come back. A built-in timer is more concrete than "a few minutes", and the most useful single feature on this screen.
- Say "crying like this is normal and it will pass", with "around 6 to 8 weeks" as the likely peak, and count premature babies from the due date (ICON).
- Honest claim: "This information helps parents know what to expect and what to do." Do not say "this prevents shaking."
- Prompt the parent to share the plan with every carer. Barr's CMAJ trial found this behaviour moves, and CDC warns about leaving babies with easily irritated carers.

### Gaps
- Raising Children Network returned 403. No claims from it are included.
- The exact PURPLE age range and hours ("2 weeks to 3 or 4 months, up to 5 hours") could not be retrieved from dontshake.org, whose page shows the acronym only as an image.
- Barr's original 1990 crying-curve paper was not retrieved.
- Keenan 2003 (US incidence) was not retrieved.
- ICON's crying plan template contents were not readable; only a download link appears.

## What actually works for soothing, ranked by evidence

### Takeaway
No method reliably stops all crying. The best-supported low-risk immediate action is carrying while walking for about 5 minutes then sitting and holding for 5 to 8 more before laying down, though it rests on 21 infants. Most remedies sold for colic have weak evidence or known hazards.

**Avoid or do not recommend**
- Herbal agents, sugar, dicyclomine and cimetropium "cannot be recommended". Simethicone showed no benefit (RR 0.95, 0.73 to 1.23). 18 RCTs, 1,014 infants. — [Biagioli 2016, Cochrane](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27631535/)
- Dietary changes: 15 trials, 1,121 infants, sparse and high risk of bias, so none could be recommended. — [Gordon 2018, Cochrane](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30306546/). NHS says breastfeeding mothers need not change diet ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/colic/)), while AAP allows a pediatrician-guided, one-at-a-time trial ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Colic.aspx)), so sources differ.
- Spinal manipulation and cranial osteopathy: little evidence and possible harm per [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/colic/). Cochrane found no definitive conclusion possible. — [Dobson 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23235617/)
- Gripe water is sold as a supplement in the US and has a recall history: Cryptosporidium in Baby's Bliss, 2007 ([FDA, archived](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916230938/http://www.fda.gov/consumer/updates/babybliss092107.html)), microbial contamination in Canada ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/5938716/gripe-water-recall/)), and swallowing difficulty with DG Baby in 2019 ([TODAY](https://www.today.com/health/baby-gripe-water-recalled-dollar-general-stores-because-choking-hazard-t149042)). These three came from search results and were not opened in full.
- Honey before age 1: bacteria that cause infant botulism. — [NHS, reviewed 2026-02-18](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/weaning-and-feeding/foods-to-avoid-giving-babies-and-young-children/)
- Placing the baby on a washer or dryer. — [AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Colic.aspx)

**Swaddling rules**
- Stop at the first sign of trying to roll, which can be as early as 2 months. Leave room for hips to bend up and out and two or three fingers at the chest. Always on the back on a firm flat surface. Watch for overheating (sweating, damp hair, flushed cheeks, heat rash, rapid breathing). No weighted swaddles. — [AAP, updated 2022-07-15](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/diapers-clothing/Pages/Swaddling-Is-it-Safe.aspx)
- Do not wrap legs straight and pressed together. Keep knees slightly bent and consider upper-body swaddling. — [IHDI, undated](https://hipdysplasia.org/infant-child/hip-healthy-swaddling/)
- Use a thin material, never a blanket. Never swaddle when bed-sharing or when baby has an infection or fever. Sleep room 16 to 20°C. — [Lullaby Trust, last updated 2026-10-07 per page](https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/baby-safety/baby-product-information/swaddling/)

### Inferences
- The 3 a.m. flow should be short. Quick illness check, then basic needs, walk 5 minutes, sit 5 to 8 minutes, sing, gentle low noise, and the walk-away timer always one tap away.
- Word the Ohmura finding as "many babies settled in one study", because 21 infants and a single session do not support "this works". A parent who tries it and fails must not feel they did it wrong. ICON's framing helps here: what worked yesterday may not work today.
- Do not promote any product, supplement or remedy from this area.

### Gaps
- The search results said "up to 7 months" and "46% asleep plus 18% in the next minute" for Ohmura, but no fetched source confirmed these. Cell returned 403. Do not cite these figures until checked.
- Harrington 2012 (5 S's for vaccination pain) was not retrieved; several citation lookups failed.
- Hugh 2014's advice on limiting duration appears only in search-level summaries.
- Safe massage oils: Bennett did not analyse oil type. Danby 2013, Cooke 2016 and Lack 2003 were not retrieved. Make no oil recommendation yet.
- No completed Cochrane review of probiotics as colic treatment was found; search results mentioned a 2022 protocol.
- I could not read the 2020 BMJ Open overview by Ellwood and colleagues, because bmjopen.bmj.com returned 403. A search snippet said it ranks probiotics for breastfed infants as the strongest evidence, followed by manual therapy.

## "Why might they be crying" checklist and red flags for calling a doctor

### Takeaway
Use a short needs checklist, then a strict red-flag list using NHS and AAP thresholds, and treat a weak, high-pitched or continuous cry as an emergency sign. Under 3 months, 38°C or above needs immediate action.

### Cited Findings

**Reasons**
- Hunger, wet or dirty nappy, tiredness, wanting closeness, trapped wind, too hot or cold, boredom, overstimulation. Crying during feeds may mean checking positioning and attachment, and can sometimes be reflux. — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/soothing-a-crying-baby/)
- Hunger, a dirty nappy, wind, reflux and constipation can look like colic. — [NHS Colic](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/colic/)

**Red flags, UK**
- Call 999 or A&E for a baby or child under 5 with: swollen lips, tongue or throat; very fast or difficult breathing, grunting or sucking in the tummy; blue, grey, pale or blotchy skin (check palms and soles on dark skin); cold or clammy skin; rash that does not fade when pressed; temperature of 38°C or higher under 3 months, or 39°C or higher at 3 to 6 months; under 36°C or feeling cold; weak, high-pitched or continuous cry; hard to wake; no wee for 12 hours; seizure. — [NHS, reviewed 2026-08-06](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/health/is-your-baby-or-toddler-seriously-ill/)
- Other serious signs on that page: swollen or bulging fontanelle, not feeding, vomiting for 24 hours, red, black or brown vomit, floppy, stiff neck, not using a limb. — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/health/is-your-baby-or-toddler-seriously-ill/)
- Yellow-green or green vomit in a child: call 999 or go to A&E. Vomit with blood or that looks like ground coffee: same. Any baby under 12 months with vomiting or diarrhoea you are worried about: call 111. — [NHS, reviewed 2023-12-21, next review due 2026-12-21](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/diarrhoea-and-vomiting/)
- Meningitis signs in babies: not wanting to feed, high-pitched unusual cry, stiff or floppy, bulging fontanelle. Press a clear glass on any rash. Do not drive yourself to A&E. — [NHS, reviewed 2026-06-12](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/meningitis/symptoms/)
- Soothing page 999 list adds: violent vomiting, and no wet nappy in 8 hours. — [NHS, reviewed 2026-04-22](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/soothing-a-crying-baby/). This conflicts with the 12-hour figure above.
- Conflict on urgency: the older fever page says call 111 for 38°C under 3 months. — [NHS Fever, reviewed 2024-01-03](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/fever-in-children/). The newer seriously-ill page says 999 or A&E.

**Red flags, US**
- Under 3 months: 100.4°F (38.0°C) or higher means call right away. Any age, repeatedly above 104°F (40°C): call right away. Fever over 24 hours under 2 years warrants a call. Also call right away for seriously ill appearance, unusual sleepiness, marked irritability, stiff neck, trouble breathing, unexplained rash, repeated vomiting, or seizure. — [AAP, updated 2022-12-02](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/fever/Pages/When-to-Call-the-Pediatrician.aspx)
- Before treating colic, ask a pediatrician to rule out a hernia or illness. — [AAP Colic](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Colic.aspx)
- NHS also says to get advice if nothing helps, you are finding it hard to cope, baby is not gaining weight, or symptoms continue past 4 months, and to trust your instincts. — [NHS Colic](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/colic/), [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/soothing-a-crying-baby/)

### Inferences
- Where sources conflict, the app should use the stricter guidance: 999 or the local emergency number for 38°C under 3 months, and 8 hours as the "worry" point for a wet nappy on the crying screen.
- Replace "999" and "A&E" with the local emergency number from the country table, and keep "If you are worried, get help. You do not need to be sure" as the closing line.
- Show red flags first on the crying helper, as a compact list, because it also protects against the risk that a parent walks away from a sick baby.

### Gaps
- AAP wording for green or bilious vomit was not found; two HealthyChildren vomiting pages either did not exist or did not address it.
- NICE NG143 (traffic-light fever guidance) returned 403.
- Rarer causes such as hair tourniquet were not sourced, and are excluded.

## Toddlers: comfort and soothing beyond infancy

### Takeaway
After infancy the driver shifts from unexplained crying to frustration and limited language. Parents can name feelings, stay calm, set limits, keep things safe and still step away.

### Cited Findings
- Tantrums usually start around 18 months and become far less common by age 4 as language grows. Triggers are frustration, tiredness, hunger and jealousy. — [NHS, reviewed 2022-11-07, next review was due 2025-11-07, so overdue](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/)
- NHS suggestions: distract early, stay calm and wait, do not reward tantrums, hold firmly if the child is more upset than angry and you are calm enough, name feelings. At home you can go to another room for a while once the child cannot hurt themselves. Do not hit back. — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/babys-development/behaviour/temper-tantrums/)
- AAP: most often ages 1 to 3, improving after 3. Offer small choices, redirect, plan around hunger and naps. When your frustration rises, take a break and hand off care to a trusted person. Do not ignore hitting, biting or throwing. — [AAP, updated 2021-04-21](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/family-dynamics/communication-discipline/Pages/Temper-Tantrums.aspx)
- Responsive back-and-forth, including naming what a child is seeing, doing or feeling, supports development. — [Harvard Center on the Developing Child](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/serve-and-return/)

### Inferences

| | Young infant | Toddler |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Often unknown, normal | Often clear: frustration, tired, hungry |
| Parent role | Comfort and wait | Co-regulate, then limit-set |
| You cannot spoil them | Yes ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Responding-to-Your-Babys-Cries.aspx)) | Comfort yes; do not reward the tantrum |
| Stepping away | Baby on back in cot | Room must be safe; child stays out of danger |
| Language | Tone and song | Name feelings, small choices |

- The area needs an age switch, because the 5-minute walk and white-noise advice do not transfer to a 2-year-old, and tantrum advice would be wrong for a 6-week-old.

### Gaps
- No trial evidence for toddler in-the-moment soothing was retrieved; this section is guidance only.
- Zero To Three pages returned 404 at the guessed URLs.

## Overstimulation cues and telling "wants to play" from "wants to stop"

### Takeaway
Reputable sources agree on the basics: play when the baby is alert and relaxed, and pause for looking away, squirming, fussing or crying. Detailed Barnard cue lists exist but sit behind training and were not retrievable, so the app can only safely use this short list until licensed material is obtained.

### Cited Findings
- Play when baby is alert and relaxed, and watch for signs of being tired or fussy so they can take a break. — [CDC, 2026-02-20](https://www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/infants.html)
- Looking away may mean baby wants space. Jerky movements such as kicking or squirming can mean upset. Crying can mean unhappy or tired. You cannot spoil a baby with attention, but look for cues they are tired or need space. — [Pregnancy Birth and Baby, reviewed May 2025](https://www.pregnancybirthbaby.org.au/bonding-with-your-baby)
- Overstimulation is a listed reason for crying. — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/baby/caring-for-a-newborn/soothing-a-crying-baby/). AAP links colic to sensitivity to stimulation and an immature nervous system. — [AAP Colic](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Colic.aspx)
- Infant and caregiver expressions let them mutually regulate, and positive development is tied to "frequent reparations of interactive errors". — [Tronick 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2653124/)
- Coordinated time rises with age but stays a small proportion of interaction (54 pairs at 3, 6 and 9 months). — [Tronick & Cohn 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2702877/)
- Babbles, gestures and cries are "serves"; attentive response builds connections. — [Harvard](https://developingchild.harvard.edu/resources/serve-and-return/)
- Barnard Center (University of Washington) offers Understanding Infants: Keys to Infant Caregiving (handouts on Infant State, Behavior, Cues, State Modulation, and Feeding), BabyCues cards, and PCI Feeding and Teaching Scales with training. Licence terms are not stated; phone 206-543-8528. — [PCR Programs](http://pcrprograms.org/)

### Inferences

Short cue card, limited to what the sources above support:

| Keep playing | Slow down or pause | Stop and comfort |
|---|---|---|
| Alert and relaxed; babbling or gesturing toward you | Looking away; squirming or jerky kicking | Fussing, crying, or looks tired |

- Every PlayNest game could start with "Is your baby alert and relaxed?" and end with "Looked away or squirming? That's a normal 'break please'."
- Do not suggest games on the crying helper, and offer a "not now" option that carries no guilt.
- Tell parents a baby looking away is not rejection. Tronick's work supports treating missed cues as normal and repairable.

### Gaps
- The engagement and disengagement cue lists (the NCAST tradition) were not retrievable and need licensing or a direct request to Barnard Center.
- Brazelton NBAS or NBO primary material was not retrieved. Do not use www.brazelton-institute.com, which now serves a gambling site.
- Raising Children's "baby cues" page returned 403.

## Prevalence and warning signs: parents' mental health

### Takeaway
Depression and anxiety after birth affect a large minority of mothers and a meaningful share of fathers. Postpartum psychosis is rare and an emergency. Unwanted frightening thoughts are very common and must not be treated as evidence of danger, and burnout varies widely by culture.

### Cited Findings

**Depression**
- Mothers, global: 17.22% (95% CI 16.00 to 18.51), 565 studies across 80 countries or regions; highest in Southern Africa at 39.96%. — [Wang 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34671011/)
- US: 13.2% self-reported postpartum depressive symptoms (PRAMS 2018, 31 sites, n = 32,659). — [CDC MMWR, 2020-05-15](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/69/wr/mm6919a2.htm)
- Fathers: 10.4% (95% CI 8.5 to 12.7), with 25.6% at 3 to 6 months postpartum, 43 studies, n = 28,004. Correlation with maternal depression r = 0.308. — [Paulson & Bazemore 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20483973/)
- Fathers, later update: 8.4% (95% CI 7.2 to 9.6), 74 studies, n = 41,480. — [Cameron 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27475890/)
- Symptoms: low mood, loss of enjoyment, hopelessness, guilt, anxiety, irritability, sleep and concentration problems, trouble bonding, and thoughts of suicide or of harming yourself or your baby. Can start in pregnancy or up to a year after birth, and partners can be affected. — [NHS, reviewed 2026-03-18](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/post-natal-depression/overview/)

**Anxiety**
- Mothers, postnatal, 1 to 24 weeks: 15.0% symptoms (13.7 to 16.4), any disorder 9.9% (6.1 to 13.8), 102 studies. — [Dennis 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28302701/)
- Men: postnatal 2.4% to 18.0%, 43 papers. — [Leach 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26590515/)

**Postpartum psychosis (emergency)**
- About 1 in 1,000 mothers. Usually starts suddenly within 2 weeks of birth, often hours or days. Signs: hallucinations, delusions, mania, rapidly changing mood, confusion. See a GP immediately for a same-day assessment; 111 if unreachable; A&E or 999 if there is risk of imminent harm. The person may not realise they are ill, so family may need to act. — [NHS, reviewed 2023-10-18, next review was due 2026-10-18, so overdue](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/post-partum-psychosis/)
- Can worsen very quickly and should always be treated as a medical emergency. — [Action on Postpartum Psychosis](https://www.app-network.org/what-is-pp/)

**Birth trauma**
- Postpartum PTSD 4.0% in community samples (2.77 to 5.71) and 18.5% in high-risk groups (10.6 to 30.38), 59 studies, n = 24,267. — [Yildiz 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27865585/)

**Intrusive thoughts**
- 100 women: thoughts of accidental harm were universal, and close to half had unwanted thoughts of intentionally harming their infant. The authors call this relatively normative and found little evidence linking the thoughts to aggressive parenting. — [Fairbrother & Woody 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18463941/)
- Up to 80% of new mothers report unwanted infant-related thoughts, and over two-thirds of healthy new fathers. Perinatal OCD affects an estimated 1% to 3% of childbearing women. In OCD the person is terrified and resists the thought; the author calls the risk of acting extremely low. In psychosis violent thoughts may fit delusions and are not resisted, and that needs urgent care. — [IOCDF expert opinion](https://iocdf.org/expert-opinions/perinatal-ocd/)
- Obsessions after birth may include worrying about harming the baby, with compulsions such as repeatedly checking breathing. — [NHS OCD, next review was due 2026-04-04, so overdue](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/obsessive-compulsive-disorder-ocd/overview/)
- Perinatal OCD is treatable. — [Maternal OCD](https://maternalocd.org/)

**Burnout**
- 17,409 parents in 42 countries. At the stricter cut-off, rates ranged from 0.0% (Cuba, Pakistan) to 9.8% (Belgium), with Poland 9.6% and USA 8.9%. Individualism explained more variance than inequality or family characteristics. — [Roskam 2021, Affective Science](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7970748/)

**Loneliness and specific groups**
- Loneliness among pregnant women and mothers ranged from 32% to 42% pre-pandemic. In one longitudinal study, 37.2% of fathers had slight increases after birth. Risk groups included immigrants and refugees, adolescent parents, and trans or non-binary gestational parents. Peer support and friendships with other mothers were protective. — [Kent-Marvick 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9451126/)
- NICU parents: routine mental-health assessment should be part of NICU care (scoping review, 66 articles, no pooled figures). — [Roque 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28506679/)
- Grandparents raising grandchildren: literature review with no pooled prevalence. — [Hayslip 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28666363/)

### Inferences
- Copy should name fathers, partners, adoptive parents, NICU parents, grandparents and single parents explicitly, and say "you don't need to have given birth to feel this".
- Use "about 1 in 8 to 1 in 6 mothers and about 1 in 10 fathers" only if a number is needed; a parent at 3 a.m. benefits more from "this is common and it can get better".
- Do not expose the burnout country rankings. They are cut-off-sensitive, and the 0.0% values should not be read as absence.

### Gaps
- 2024 to 2026 meta-analysis updates: I could not search for them because the web-search budget ran out. Nothing newer than 2021 is reported here for mothers, and none newer than 2016 for fathers. Run this search in a follow-up.
- Adoptive parents (Mott 2011) and single parents: no verified numbers.
- VanderKruik 2017 (psychosis incidence) could not be read; the citation lookup returned an unrelated record. Citation-matcher results proved unreliable and all were rechecked.
- Fairbrother 2022 (intrusive thoughts and physical aggression) was not retrieved. It matters for safe wording, so read it before finalising.
- Birth Trauma Association site failed with a certificate mismatch.
- Raising Children, Tommy's and Mind pages returned 403.

## Screening tools and licensing: what can a non-medical app honestly offer?

### Takeaway
An app should not screen or score. An unscored, optional check-in using plain language and ending with "would you like help?" fits the evidence, because even clinical tools are treated as conversation aids. Offer no result, no threshold and no diagnosis.

### Cited Findings
- UK National Screening Committee: EPDS should not be used as a screening tool. It may serve as a checklist alongside professional judgement and a clinical interview, not as pass/fail. — [SIGN 127, March 2012](https://www.sign.ac.uk/media/1065/sign127_update.pdf)
- Insufficient evidence for EPDS or Whooley as accurate tools, though they may help facilitate discussion. EPDS at cut-off 9 or 10 had PPV of 9% to 64% for major depression, at 12 or 13 PPV 17% to 100%. It performs best in English speakers. — [SIGN 127](https://www.sign.ac.uk/media/1065/sign127_update.pdf)
- Whooley wording: "During the last month, have you often been bothered by feeling down, depressed or hopeless?" and "During the last month have you often been bothered by having little interest or pleasure in doing things?" A third question, "Is this something with which you would like help?", gave PPV 32% and NPV 99%. An HTA found insufficient evidence for them. — [SIGN 127](https://www.sign.ac.uk/media/1065/sign127_update.pdf)
- Whooley two-item: sensitivity 96%, specificity 57% in 536 primary-care adults. — [Whooley 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9229283/)
- PHQ-2: stem "Over the last 2 weeks", items "Little interest or pleasure in doing things" and "Feeling down, depressed or hopeless". Score 3 or more suggests likely major depression, to be followed up with PHQ-9 or an interview. — [UW National HIV Curriculum](https://www.hiv.uw.edu/page/mental-health-screening/phq-2). Sensitivity 83%, specificity 92%. — [Kroenke 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14583691/)
- PHQ licensing: "no permission is required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute them." — [PHQ Screeners](https://www.phqscreeners.com/select-screener)
- EPDS was developed with 84 mothers and takes about 5 minutes. — [Cox 1987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3651732/)

### Inferences
- Tools only detect what they target. SIGN notes depression tools do not detect anxiety, OCD or psychosis, so a clean check-in must never read as reassurance.
- At Whooley's 57% specificity many positive results would be false, in a population already short on sleep. A scored result would frighten parents and also falsely reassure others.
- Draft check-in (mine, unscored, and not stored): "Lately, have you been feeling down or hopeless, or finding little pleasure in things? Many new parents do. Is this something you'd like some help with?" Options: "Yes, show me who can help", "Not right now", "I'm scared I might hurt myself or my baby". The third option goes straight to the crisis screen.
- Ask only when the parent opens this area. Do not send push prompts on the topic; Samaritans urges careful thought about push notifications even in a media context.

### Gaps
- EPDS licensing: I could not verify the current terms. The Stanford and perinatology copies did not load. Widely reproduced versions carry a notice allowing reproduction with attribution, as I recall, but that was not verified here and it may not cover commercial or digital use. Ask the Royal College of Psychiatrists and Cambridge University Press. The recommendation above avoids needing it.
- NICE CG192 returned 403, so the NICE wording comes through SIGN's report of the NICE adult guideline, dated 2012. Check the current CG192 text.
- How item 10 of EPDS is handled in digital tools was not retrieved.
- Medical-device regulation (MHRA, EU MDR, FDA) for a scored mental-health check was not researched. It should be reviewed before any scoring is added.

## Supportive wording, avoiding guilt and medicalising, brief interventions, and sleep deprivation

### Takeaway
Good-enough framing has a respectable basis in Winnicott and Tronick. Short breathing practices and micro-breaks have modest evidence, but none was tested in sleep-deprived parents, and the two programmes with best evidence are not freely licensed. The clearest safety message is not to fall asleep with a baby on a sofa.

### Cited Findings

**Framing**
- "Good enough" comes from Winnicott and aims to defend ordinary parents against idealisation. Secondary source citing *The Child, the Family, and the Outside World*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_enough_parent)
- Mismatches are normal and repair is what matters: positive development is linked with "frequent reparations of interactive errors". — [Tronick 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2653124/). Coordinated time is small even in healthy pairs. — [Tronick & Cohn 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2702877/)
- Mindful Self-Compassion improved self-compassion, mindfulness and wellbeing in an RCT with 25 treated and 27 waitlist adults, mostly older women, using an 8-week course rather than a one-minute exercise. — [Neff & Germer 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23070875/)
- Maternal OCD's line: "There is no way to be a perfect mother, and a million ways to be a good one." — [Maternal OCD](https://maternalocd.org/)
- PANDA says plainly it is not a crisis service. — [PANDA](https://panda.org.au/contact). Clear statements like this are a useful honest model.

**Programmes**
- USPSTF Grade B (2019-02-12): counselling for those at increased risk, naming Mothers and Babies (CBT) and ROSE (interpersonal). Update in progress. — [USPSTF](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/perinatal-depression-preventive-interventions)
- Mothers and Babies: manuals, worksheets, mindfulness audio; providers must be trained; terms not stated. — [Mothers and Babies](https://www.mothersandbabiesprogram.org/)
- What Were We Thinking: worksheets on crying, sleep, workload and partner support, from Monash University. Cluster RCT: AOR 0.78 (0.38 to 1.63) intention to treat, not significant; per-protocol AOR 0.36 (0.14 to 0.95). — [Fisher 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26951210/), [WWWT](https://www.whatwerewethinking.org.au/)
- Family Foundations: nine-step online couple programme, Penn State, $25 per couple. The site reports evaluation-body endorsements. — [Family Foundations](https://famfound.net/)
- WHO Doing What Matters in Times of Stress: self-help techniques of a few minutes a day, about 50 languages, with audio. — [WHO, 2020-04-29](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927)
- WHO Thinking Healthy: CBT manual for perinatal depression, delivered by community health workers. — [WHO, 2015](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-MSD-MER-15.1)
- Thrive Mental Wellbeing: employer product using PHQ-9 and GAD-7 with therapist outreach. Its 87% recovery claim is vendor-reported with no method shown, and its page describes no crisis protocol. — [Thrive](https://thrive.uk.com/). The brief's "Thrive app" may mean something else, so confirm.

**Brief practices**
- Daily 5-minute cyclic sighing improved mood and lowered respiratory rate more than mindfulness (both p < .05, remote RCT, adults). — [Balban 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/)
- Micro-breaks: vigour d = .36, fatigue d = .35, performance not significant, 22 samples, n = 2,335, mainly work and study settings. — [Albulescu 2022](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0272460)

**Sleep deprivation and safety**
- Risk of sleep-related infant death is up to 67 times higher on a couch or soft armchair. If tired, move baby to a firm flat surface on their back before you rest. — [AAP, updated 2026-09-11](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/A-Parents-Guide-to-Safe-Sleep.aspx)
- Falling asleep with a baby on a sofa or armchair significantly raises SIDS risk. Suggestions: shift swapping with a partner, friend or relative; stay awake with snacks, cold water, fresh air, a podcast or a vibrating alarm; message other parents at night. Also prepare for accidental bed-sharing: clear adult bedding, and do not sleep with baby after alcohol or sedating medication, if anyone smokes, or if baby was under 37 weeks or 2.5 kg. In its surveys 98% of parents reported sleep deprivation. — [Lullaby Trust](https://www.lullabytrust.org.uk/safer-sleep-advice/sleep-deprivation/)

### Inferences
- Words to prefer: "this is hard", "many parents", "you came back", "ask for backup". Words to avoid: "should", "bad", "failing", "just", "at least", and clinical labels without a request for help.
- "Rupture and repair" gives a usable line: "Babies and parents miss each other all the time. Coming back is what counts."
- Offer a 60-second breathing exercise and a two-minute "put the phone down" break, written originally and described honestly as "may help you feel a bit steadier", since no trial tested them in parents at 3 a.m.
- Offer a "drowsy mode" prompt: "Feel yourself nodding off? Put baby on their back in their cot now." Only for sofas and chairs, and in plain words.

### Gaps
- The 30% in-sync figure could not be confirmed; do not use it without reading *The Power of Discord* or the full 1989 paper.
- Winnicott is cited secondhand. The primary books were not checked.
- The WHO guide PDF returned 403, so its section titles and copyright-page terms are unread.
- self-compassion.org loaded without its body text, so Neff's three-component definition and any reuse terms are unsourced here.
- No trial of split-shift sleeping for parents was retrieved; the shift advice above is Lullaby Trust guidance.

**Why re-verification matters**
- In a review of 69 depression and suicide-prevention apps, 6 (9%) listed crisis numbers that were non-existent, non-functional or linked to a non-evidence-based organisation, and 2 of those had over a million downloads each. 94% included emergency contact information. The authors argue developers can easily verify. — [Martinengo 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6921471/)

### Inferences
- Tiered display: a "right now" crisis line and emergency number first, then a parent line, then "someone to talk to".
- Default fallback when a country is missing: Find A Helpline link with an offline-bundled emergency number, and never a blank screen.
- Separate data file per country with `last_verified`, `verified_via` and an owner. Re-test at least quarterly and test-call before each release.
- Crisis lines are for people in crisis. PANDA, Gidget, Pacific Post Partum and Lullaby Trust state they are not crisis services or are specialist, so the app must not list them as crisis options.
- In parts of the world a general line may involve child-protection reporting, and PANDAS states confidentiality can be broken for safeguarding. Parents with frightening thoughts may fear this, so tell them before they contact a service where it has been verified, and do not promise confidentiality on a service's behalf.

## What the app should do and say for "I might hurt my baby" or "I want to die"

### Takeaway
Treat both as immediate, one-tap routes to real people. Be direct and warm, do not try to assess or diagnose, keep the baby safe first, never ask for or mention methods, and be honest that the app is not monitored. Few apps do all of this well, and many get basics wrong.

### Cited Findings

**Safe-messaging rules**
- Do not describe methods; give help routes such as helplines. Avoid sensational language or suggesting that suicide is a solution, quick or easy. Think carefully about push notifications, and avoid romanticised language. — [Samaritans Media Guidelines 2026](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/Media_Guidelines_2026_DIGITAL.pdf), [10 top tips](https://www.samaritans.org/about-samaritans/media-guidelines/10-top-tips-reporting-suicide/)
- Use: "a suicide", "taken their own life", "die by suicide", "suicide attempt", "person at risk of suicide". Avoid: "commit suicide", "suicide victim", "cry for help", "successful", "unsuccessful" or "failed" attempt, "epidemic". — [Samaritans 2026](https://media.samaritans.org/documents/Media_Guidelines_2026_DIGITAL.pdf)
- Evidence shows that asking someone if they are suicidal can protect them, and being asked tells them they are not a burden. If someone is in immediate danger, call the emergency number. — [Samaritans](https://www.samaritans.org/how-we-can-help/if-youre-worried-about-someone-else/supporting-someone-suicidal-thoughts/)
- Five steps: ask, be there, help keep them safe, help them connect, follow up. Be direct; listen without judging; do not debate or lecture; do not act shocked; avoid glib reassurance; never keep a plan secret. — [988 Lifeline](https://988lifeline.org/help-someone-else/)
- Suicide-prevention messaging frameworks stress safety and positive narrative, with messages promoting help-seeking and resilience. — [suicidepreventionmessaging.org](https://suicidepreventionmessaging.org/). AFSP and others collaborated on the US recommendations. — [SAVE](https://www.save.org/media/media-recommendations/)
- Focus on people who overcome a crisis, and on hope and recovery. Licence CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO. — [WHO 2023](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240076846)

**Baby-safety routes**
- Thoughts of harming yourself or your baby are a listed symptom of postnatal depression. — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/post-natal-depression/overview/)
- Upset with a crying baby: safe spot, step away, ask for help; US parents can call or text 1-833-852-6262. — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/child-abuse-neglect/about/about-abusive-head-trauma.html)
- Psychosis signs need same-day or emergency help, and the person may not realise they are ill. — [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/post-partum-psychosis/)
- For the distinction between frightening unwanted thoughts and psychosis, see the prevalence section ([IOCDF](https://iocdf.org/expert-opinions/perinatal-ocd/), [Fairbrother & Woody 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18463941/)).

**How apps behave**
- 69 apps: only 5 (7%) offered all six strategies; 46 (67%) linked a crisis helpline; 65 (94%) gave emergency contact details. None of the chatbots tested flagged "I am very sad and hopeless". 49 apps were country-specific. Licence CC BY 4.0. — [Martinengo 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6921471/)
- Phone assistants (Siri, Google Now, S Voice, Cortana) responded inconsistently and incompletely to mental-health and violence statements, 68 phones. — [Miner 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26974260/)
- Headspace Ebb: says clinical experts tested it and a proprietary system guides members to crisis support. It states it is not human care, is not monitored in real time, sends those in immediate danger to emergency services, points US and Canada users to 988 and others to international resources. It does not say how detection works. — [Headspace](https://www.headspace.com/ai-mental-health-companion)
- Woebot Health shut its core product around 2025-07-02, citing the cost of FDA authorisation and uncertainty over LLM regulation. — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/07/02/woebot-therapy-chatbot-shuts-down-founder-says-ai-moving-faster-than-regulators/). It is not a live model to copy.
- Thrive's page describes no crisis protocol. — [Thrive](https://thrive.uk.com/)

### Inferences

The wording below is my draft, not sourced text. It needs review by a perinatal clinician and a suicide-prevention body before release.

**Design rules**
- Buttons, not free-text detection. A parent choosing "I might hurt my baby" is more reliable than an algorithm, and it avoids what Martinengo and Miner found. Make no AI response in this area.
- Show one screen with large buttons, and the local number pre-filled. Make it work offline.
- Never auto-call or auto-alert anyone. The parent decides.
- Never ask for reasons, plans or methods. Never ask the parent to rate their risk. Do not offer a mood diary here.
- Say truthfully: "This app can't see what you tap, and no one is watching. These people can help."

**"I might hurt my baby"**

> You did the right thing by pressing this. Let's keep you both safe.
>
> **1. Put your baby on their back in their cot, or another safe place.**
> **2. Go to another room. It's OK to leave them crying for a few minutes.**
> **3. Call someone now.**
>
> [Call emergency: {number}] if you feel you can't stop yourself, or you are seeing, hearing or believing things that feel strange or frightening.
> [Call {parent or crisis line}]
> [Call someone I trust]
>
> Many new parents get frightening thoughts they don't want. Having them is not the same as wanting to act, and help works. Tell a doctor or nurse, even if it feels hard to say.

Why it is worded this way: a blanket "you would never do it" is unsafe, because the app cannot distinguish OCD, depression, overwhelm or psychosis. Normalising comes after safety. The psychosis signals route to emergency care because the person may not recognise them.

**"I want to die"**

> We're really glad you said it. Thoughts like this can come when things are too much, and you don't have to carry them alone.
>
> Are you thinking about ending your life?
> [Yes, or I'm not sure] [No, but I feel awful]
>
> **Yes or unsure:**
> If you might act on this soon, call {emergency} now.
> [Call {crisis line}: free, {hours}]
> [Text {service}] (where one exists)
> [Call someone I trust]
> If your baby is with you, put them on their back in their cot first. Then call.
> Stay with someone if you can. These feelings can pass and people do come through this.
>
> **No:** show the parent lines and the "someone to talk to" list.

**Things to avoid**
- Phrases such as "commit suicide", "you have so much to live for", or "think of your baby". The last can add guilt, especially where a parent believes their baby is better off without them.
- Any hint of a specific method. Any statistic on this screen.
- Follow-up notifications. If a follow-up is offered at all, it should be opt-in, generic and on-device only.
- Any "crisis over" state.

## Privacy: keeping this support private with no logging by default

### Takeaway
Mental-health signals are highly regulated and have already led to enforcement. The safest design collects nothing: bundle content locally, run no analytics here, store no history, and be frank about what a phone keeps on its own.

### Cited Findings
- BetterHelp: FTC alleged it disclosed email addresses, IP addresses and health questionnaire answers (including on suicidal thoughts) to Facebook, Snapchat, Criteo and Pinterest for ads despite privacy promises. The order banned sharing health data for advertising and required $7.8 million for refunds. — [FTC, 2023-03-02](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/03/ftc-ban-betterhelp-revealing-consumers-data-including-sensitive-mental-health-information-facebook)
- Health Breach Notification Rule covers non-HIPAA health apps that are personal health record vendors. Unauthorised disclosure, including sharing with a third party without consent, counts as a breach. July 2024 amendments clarified that it covers health apps. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0)
- GDPR Article 9 prohibits processing data concerning health by default, with exceptions such as explicit consent for specified purposes. — [GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)
- Headspace states encrypted conversations, no targeted advertising use and user control over conversation history. This is a claim, not an audit. — [Headspace](https://www.headspace.com/ai-mental-health-companion)
- In this repo, a source search on 2026-11-04 for analytics, firebase, sentry, posthog, mixpanel and crashlytics in json, yaml, dart, ts, tsx and js files, excluding node_modules, found no matches. PlayNest is a Capacitor app (`capacitor.config.ts`, local file).

### Inferences
- **Default: no logging.** No analytics, crash breadcrumbs, screen-view events, or server calls on any screen in this area. Keep it true at SDK level, and add a build test that fails if a network or analytics call is added to these routes.
- **Offline bundle.** Helplines and copy ship inside the app, so nothing about the person is sent to look them up. The country comes from the device locale or a manual choice, never from an IP lookup or a location prompt.
- **Nothing stored by default.** No "last opened", no history, no timer history, and no check-in answers. If a future opt-in diary is added, make it on-device, explicitly consented (Article 9), and with one-tap delete.
- **Neutral surfaces.** No push notifications from this area, no badge, and a neutral screen name. Give a quick exit that returns to the home screen, and hide the screen in the app switcher where the platform allows.
- **Be honest about limits.** State that the phone's call log, SMS and WhatsApp keep their own records and that calling a line may appear on a phone bill.
- **Plain-language note on screen:** "Nothing you do here is saved or sent anywhere." Only say this if it has been tested and is true.
- **Avoid third-party content on these screens.** That includes web fonts, embedded video or external links opened in an in-app browser. Open links in the system browser.

### Gaps
- Mozilla's Privacy Not Included review of mental-health apps returned 403, and the ICO's special-category guidance also returned 403.
- App-store health-data disclosure rules (Apple, Google) and children's or minors' rules for teen parents were not researched. Check these before submission, along with whether a static helpline list makes the app a personal health record vendor.
- Legal review is needed for each market. These notes are not legal advice.
