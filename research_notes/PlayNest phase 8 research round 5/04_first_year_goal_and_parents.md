# 04 — A "first year with our baby" goal (0–12 months), and the role of fathers and partners

Research notes for PlayNest phase 8, round 5. Researched 17 Nov 2026.
House rules for this shelf: teach, don't diagnose. No doses. Never guilt-trip. Never suggest that a tired or struggling parent is harming their baby. Use proportionate wording ("linked with" for correlations). Persian copy respects tradition and faith and gives no religious rulings.

Already covered by existing guides, so not re-researched here: serve and return, tummy time, the crying curve, safe sleep, first weeks at home, milk feeds, starting solids, and the parent crisis page (postnatal depression and psychosis). This note covers only what a **goal** for 0–12 months should ask parents to *practise*, and the evidence behind each practice.

## 1. Coping with crying and preventing shaking

### Findings
- **Barr 2009 RCTs.** I did not re-open these. [earlier round: phase 5 `05_crying_soothing_parent_wellbeing.md`]
  - CMAJ trial, n = 1,279: knowledge of crying rose. Walking away during inconsolable crying rose (rate ratio 1.7, 95% CI 1.1–2.6). More mothers shared the walk-away advice with other carers (51.5% vs 38.5%).
  - Pediatrics trial, n = 2,738: knowledge rose, but responses to crying were similar in both groups.
- **British Columbia, province-wide PURPLE programme, 2009–2016 (n = 354,477 newborns)** [opened: Barr RG et al., *Child Abuse Negl* 2018, PMID 30077049]
  - Delivery: nurses reached 90% of mothers on the maternity ward. Fathers were present 74.4% of the time. By 2–4 months, 70.9% of mothers and 50.5% of fathers had watched the DVD or read the booklet.
  - Under 12 months: abusive head trauma (AHT) admissions fell from 10.6 to 7.1 per 100,000 person-years. RR 0.67 (0.42–1.07), **not significant**.
  - Under 24 months: admissions fell from 6.7 to 4.4. RR 0.65 (0.43–0.99), just significant.
  - Design: before-and-after surveillance with **no comparison province**.
- **North Carolina statewide programme, 2009–2012 (88.3% of parents of newborns, n = 405,060)** [opened: Zolotor et al., *JAMA Pediatr* 2015, PMID 26501945]
  - Calls about crying to a nurse advice line fell 20% under 3 months (RR 0.80, 0.73–0.87) and 12% at 3–12 months.
  - **AHT rates did not fall.** They were 34.01 per 100,000 person-years before and 36.04 after. The difference-in-difference against other states was not significant (β −1.42, 95% CI −13.31 to 10.45).
  - The authors say further work is needed to show an effect on AHT.
- **Other programmes.** Pennsylvania (Dias 2017) found no reduction. Western New York (Dias 2005) found a 47% fall, but against historical controls only. [earlier round]
- **Honest summary:** Crying education reliably raises knowledge and some coping behaviours. Population injury results are **mixed**: one null result in a large state, one null-to-borderline result without a comparison group, and one positive result with weak controls.
- **ICON** ("Babies cry, you can cope"): the four messages are "Infant crying is normal", "Comforting methods can help", "It's OK to walk away" and "Never, ever shake a baby" [earlier round].
  - The NIHR-funded incidence evaluation was announced in 2021–22. A qualitative study found that parents remember and use the messages [search: ARC West, UWA news Nov 2025]. **No injury-rate result was found.**
- **Walk-away wording** [earlier round]:
  - NHS: put the baby in the cot or pram, go to another room, set a timer (for example 10 minutes).
  - CDC: put the baby in a safe place and check every 5–10 minutes.
  - ICON: "It's OK to walk away if the baby is safe and the crying is getting to you."
  - CDC (archived pages): a calming break while the baby is safe in the crib [search].
- **Crying plans.** Washington State DCYF publishes a "Have a Plan" brochure [search]. ICON's crying-plan template could not be read in round 2.
- **Fathers.** AHT perpetrators are more often male carers [recall, unverified: Barr and others]. The BC programme deliberately reached fathers (74.4% present at delivery of the programme) [opened]. This supports making the crying plan a **two-adult** and **every-carer** practice.

### Sources
- Barr RG, Barr M, Rajabali F, et al. *Eight-year outcome of implementation of abusive head trauma prevention.* Child Abuse & Neglect, 2018. doi:10.1016/j.chiabu.2018.07.004
- Zolotor AJ, Runyan DK, et al. *Effectiveness of a Statewide Abusive Head Trauma Prevention Program in North Carolina.* JAMA Pediatrics, 2015. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4687484
- ICON. https://iconcope.org/ · NIHR ARC West news: https://arc-w.nihr.ac.uk/news/parents-experiences-of-infant-abuse-prevention-programme-icon

**Copy direction:**
- Frame this as a plan, made in a calm moment, for when crying gets too much. Every carer who is left alone with the baby gets a copy.
- The safe step-away is a **skill**, not a failure: "Putting your baby down safely and stepping away is a responsible choice."
- Use a built-in 10-minute timer, and give each partner one person to call.

**Don't say:**
- "This prevents shaken baby syndrome" (the evidence is mixed).
- "If you feel angry you are a danger." Frustration with crying is common and is not a sign of danger. Say instead: "Many parents feel this. Here is what to do."
- Do not use the PURPLE acronym or its branding without permission [earlier round].

## 2. Talking, reading and singing with babies

### Findings
- **Parent coaching RCT, 6–18 months** [opened: Ferjan Ramírez, Lytle & Kuhl, *PNAS* 2020, PMID 32015127]
  - 71 families of typically developing 6-month-olds. Coaching at 6, 10 and 14 months.
  - Coaching increased parentese and parent–child turn-taking, and these increases correlated with language growth up to 18 months.
  - The UW press release gives about 100 vs 60 words at 18 months (parent report) [search]. This is a small trial with a highly educated US sample [recall, unverified].
- **Gilkerson 2018** [opened: abstract, *Pediatrics*, PMID 30201624]
  - 146 children, LENA daylong recordings between 2 and 36 months, followed up at 9–14 years.
  - Conversational turns at **18–24 months** explained 14–27% of the variance in IQ and language 10 years later, after controlling for SES.
  - Adult word counts weakened once SES was controlled.
  - Correlational. The abstract highlights the 18–24-month window. I did not see the result for **2–17 months**, so do not claim that turns in the first year predict later IQ.
- **Romeo 2018** [opened: abstract, *Psychol Sci*, PMID 29442613]
  - 36 children aged **4–6 years**. More conversational turns were linked with Broca's-area activation during a story task, independent of SES.
  - Not a study of babies. Use it only as background for "back-and-forth matters more than word counts".
- **Video Interaction Project (VIP), RCTs that start at birth** [opened]
  - At each well-child visit, a facilitator video-recorded the parent reading or playing with the child and reviewed the video with the parent.
  - Weisleder 2016, *Pediatrics* (n = 463 analysed, 14–36 months): better imitation and play and better attention, and less separation distress, hyperactivity and externalising behaviour. Effect about 0.25 SD overall and about 0.50 SD in families with more psychosocial risk.
  - Mendelsohn 2018, *Pediatrics*: at 4.5 years, VIP 0–3 was linked with better social-emotional scores (d ≈ 0.25–0.30). "Clinically significant" hyperactivity fell (relative risk reduction 69%).
  - These were low-income, mainly immigrant families in New York. The in-person video feedback is what was tested, not an app.
- **Reach Out and Read and book gifting.** Meta-analysis of 44 studies: home literacy environment d = 0.31 [earlier round: phase 5 `09_folk_tales_and_shared_reading.md`, de Bondt et al. 2020]. Reach Out and Read starts at about 6 months [recall, unverified].
- **Singing** [earlier round: phase 5 `08_lullabies_and_songs.md` and `05`]
  - At 8 and 10 months, after a still-face stressor, singing reduced distress more than talking, and familiar songs helped most (Cirelli & Trehub 2020).
  - A soothing version of the same song lowered arousal, while a playful version held attention.
  - Unfamiliar lullabies relaxed infants (Bainbridge 2021).
  - These are lab studies with older infants, not 3 a.m. colic.
- **WHO/UNICEF Care for Child Development counselling card** [opened: UNICEF PDF "4-CCD-Counselling-Cards"]

  | Age band | Play | Communicate |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Newborn, birth to 1 week** | "Provide ways for your baby to see, hear, move arms and legs freely, and touch you. Gently soothe, stroke and hold your child. Skin to skin is good." | "Look into baby's eyes and talk to your baby. When you are breastfeeding is a good time. Even a newborn baby sees your face and hears your voice." |
  | **1 week to 6 months** | "Slowly move colourful things for your child to see and reach for." Sample toys: shaker rattle, big ring on a string. | "Smile and laugh with your child. Talk to your child. Get a conversation going by copying your child's sounds or gestures." |
  | **6 to 9 months** | "Give your child clean, safe household things to handle, bang, and drop." Sample toys: containers with lids, metal pot and spoon. | "Respond to your child's sounds and interests. Call the child's name, and see your child respond." |
  | **9 to 12 months** | "Hide a child's favourite toy under a cloth or box. See if the child can find it. Play peek-a-boo." | "Tell your child the names of things and people. Show your child how to say things with hands, like 'bye bye'." Sample toy: doll with face. |
  | **All ages** | "Give your child affection and show your love. Be aware of your child's interests and respond to them. Praise your child for trying to learn new skills." | |

  The problems page of the card says:
  - "If the mother does not breastfeed … hold the child close when feeding, look at the child, and talk or sing to the child."
  - If caregivers are too burdened: "help them identify a key person who can share their feelings and help them with their child".
  - If caregivers have no time: "combine play and communication activities with other care for the child" and "Ask other family members to help care for the child or help with chores."
  - If the child is not responding: check seeing and hearing, and refer.

  The card fits a gentle goal almost exactly: it adds the practice to care the parent already gives.

- **Iran.** Iranian RCTs of Care for Child Development (Isfahan, n = 181) found small early benefits that faded by 48 months [earlier round: phase 6 `01_parenting_programmes.md`].

**Copy direction:**
- Use "talk *with* your baby" and "copy their sound, then wait". The wait is the turn.
- Fit the practice into existing care: nappy change, feeds, bath.
- A "book" can be a picture, a family photo or a song. Any language counts: Persian, Turkish, Kurdish, Arabic, Luri, Gilaki or English. Grandparents' lullabies («لالایی») count.

**Don't say:**
- "Every word builds your baby's brain."
- "Babies who hear fewer words fall behind."
- "Talk to your baby or their IQ will suffer."
- The "30-million-word gap".
- Never attach a number of words per day.

## 3. Responsive care (and not overclaiming "attachment")

### Findings
- **Bakermans-Kranenburg, van IJzendoorn & Juffer 2003, "Less is more"** [opened: abstract, *Psychol Bull*, PMID 12696839]
  - 70 studies.
  - Randomised interventions changed insensitive parenting (d = 0.33) and infant attachment insecurity (d = 0.20).
  - "The most effective interventions used a moderate number of sessions and a clear-cut behavioral focus."
  - This directly supports **small, specific, behavioural** practices rather than long programmes.
- **VIPP-SD meta-analysis, 25 RCTs, more than 2,000 caregivers** [opened: van IJzendoorn et al., *Dev Psychopathol* 2023, PMID 35034668]
  - Parenting behaviour r = .18, attitudes r = .16, attachment security r = .23.
  - Externalising behaviour r = .07, **not meaningful**.
  - Whether VIPP-SD works *through* better parenting is "an outstanding question".
- **Attachment myths review (47 attachment researchers)** [opened: full text, Opie et al., *J Child Psychol Psychiatry* 2026, PMC13102048]
  - The value of attachment theory lies "not in assigning attachment classifications" but in insights such as "the value of safe, stable, and shared good-enough care".
  - Misconceptions listed include:
    - (2) a child can be "over-attached" or "under-attached";
    - (3) attachment security never changes after it is set. The review says this "can result in caregiver guilt, anxiety, and self-blame";
    - (8) "right-brain" neuro claims;
    - (9) "attachment therapies" such as holding therapy are not evidence-based.
  - VIPP's discipline theme uses a "sensitive interaction pause … followed by interactional repair". Breaks and repair are part of good care.
- **Shared care.** The WHO/UNICEF card advises "Identify at least one person who can care for the child regularly". It also says to get the child used to a new carer gradually [opened]. Babies can and do form attachments to several carers [recall, unverified; consistent with the "shared good-enough care" phrasing above].

### Sources
- Bakermans-Kranenburg MJ et al. Psychol Bull 2003. doi:10.1037/0033-2909.129.2.195
- van IJzendoorn MH et al. Dev Psychopathol 2023. doi:10.1017/s0954579421001462
- Opie JE, Waters E, Duschinsky R, et al. *Practitioner Review: Clinical insights from attachment theory…* JCPP 2026. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13102048

**Copy direction:**
- "Notice, name, respond" is enough. "Good-enough" is the scientific term, not a consolation.
- Missed cues are normal. What helps is coming back and reconnecting (repair).
- Fathers, grandparents and other regular carers all matter.

**Don't say:**
- "Secure attachment is formed in the first year", "the bonding window" or "golden hour or never".
- "Your baby will be insecurely attached if…".
- "Right-brain bonding".
- "Spoiling" a baby by responding. (Also don't tell parents they must respond instantly every time.)

## 4. Responsive feeding

### Findings
- **WHO health-topic page** [opened]:
  - "Caregivers should take active care in the feeding of infants by being responsive to the child's clues for hunger and also encouraging the child to eat."
  - Foods should be "given consistent with a child's signals of appetite and satiety."
  - Complementary foods start "at 6 months of age in addition to breast milk."
- **WHO 2023 complementary feeding guideline** (6–23 months): responsive feeding is one of the seven recommendation areas. It means "recognising and responding to the child's hunger and feeding cues" [opened: ENN Field Exchange summary, 11 Apr 2024]. The exact wording and certainty were not obtained.
  - Implementation note: caregivers need "time to be present while the young child eats or self-feeds" [search].
- **WHO/PAHO *Guiding Principles for Complementary Feeding of the Breastfed Child* (2003)** [recall, unverified]: principle 5 is "practise responsive feeding": feed slowly and patiently, encourage but do not force, minimise distractions, and talk with eye contact during meals.
- **INSIGHT RCT** [opened: Paul et al., *JAMA* 2018, PMID 30088009]
  - 279 first-time mothers. Four nurse home visits in infancy covering feeding, sleep, interactive play and emotion regulation.
  - BMI z at 3 years: −0.13 vs 0.15, difference −0.28 (−0.53 to −0.01), P = .04.
  - Overweight 11.2% vs 19.8%, **not significant** (P = .07).
  - The authors call the result "modest".
- **Milk feeds.** Responsive milk feeding (feeding on cues, for comfort as well as hunger) is covered by the existing milk-feeds guide. UNICEF UK Baby Friendly defines responsive bottle feeding too [recall, unverified].

### Sources
- WHO. *Complementary feeding* (health topic). https://www.who.int/health-topics/complementary-feeding
- ENN Field Exchange 72. https://ennonline.net/fex/72/who-guideline-for-complementary-feeding-of-infants-and-young-children
- Paul IM et al. JAMA 2018. doi:10.1001/jama.2018.9432

**Copy direction:**
- "Your baby's turn": watch for hunger signs, offer, and stop when they turn away, close their mouth or push the spoon.
- Talk and look at them during meals. Mess is learning.
- Also teach how to tell "full" from "upset".

**Don't say:**
- "Make sure they finish" or "one more spoon for Mummy". In Persian, avoid «به‌زور غذا دادن» as an implied norm. Say kindly that encouraging is good and forcing isn't.
- "Responsive feeding prevents obesity". The trial result is modest and is about BMI z, not obesity.
- No amounts or schedules here (the existing solids guide covers this).

## 5. Parent wellbeing as a practice

### Findings: what works, and what a self-help app can borrow
- **Thinking Healthy, Rahman 2008** [opened: full text, *Lancet*, PMC2603063]
  - Cluster RCT, rural Pakistan, 903 depressed mothers. Delivered by Lady Health Workers: 16 sessions from late pregnancy to 10 months after birth.
  - Major depression at 6 months: 23% vs 53% (adjusted OR 0.22). At 12 months: 27% vs 59%.
  - The primary infant outcomes (weight and height) did **not** differ significantly.
  - Techniques: "active listening, collaboration with the family, guided discovery … and homework (ie, trying things out between sessions…)", built into routine mother-and-child health education.
- **Peer-delivered version (THPP), Sikander 2019** [opened: abstract, *Lancet Psychiatry*, PMID 30686386]
  - 570 women.
  - **No significant effect** on symptom severity (SMD −0.13, −0.31 to 0.06) or remission (49% vs 45%) at 6 months after birth.
  - It helped on some secondary measures (other severity and disability measures) and was cost-effective.
  - **Lesson:** a lighter delivery format can lose effect. An app is lighter still, so it should not claim to treat depression.
- **Behavioural activation (BA)**
  - Meta-analysis of postnatal BA vs usual care, only **2 RCTs**: SMD −0.56 (−0.76 to −0.37). Quality is low; "interpret cautiously" [opened: Yisma et al., *Nurs Rep* 2024, PMID 38251185].
  - The SUMMIT trial (BA delivered by non-specialists and by telemedicine) is ongoing work [opened: Kaliush 2025 abstract].
  - Scheduling small, valued activities is the safest element to borrow.
- **Elements an app can borrow safely**, my synthesis from the sources above:
  1. One small valued or pleasant activity a day (BA).
  2. Practical problem-solving on one stressor: name it, choose one step, try it, review.
  3. Bring in a family member or partner (THP "collaboration with the family").
  4. Protect sleep.
  5. Accept help.
  - **Leave out:** scoring, diagnosis and "therapy" language.
- **Sleep loss is large and real** [opened: Richter et al., *Sleep* 2019, PMID 30649536]
  - German panel: 2,541 women and 2,118 men.
  - Sleep was at its lowest in the first 3 months after birth. Mothers lost about 62 minutes a night (d = 0.90) and fathers about 13 minutes (d = 0.19).
  - Sleep satisfaction had not fully recovered 6 years after a first child.
  - This supports **sharing nights** and an extra-gentle goal design.
- **Engagement.** Review of 16 perinatal digital mental-health studies: engagement was reported inconsistently, and only 6 of 16 checked whether skills were used after the programme ended [opened: Davis et al., *JMIR* 2022, PMID 35943773].

### Findings: prevalence (to motivate, never to diagnose)
- **Iranian mothers**
  - Pooled 2013 meta-analysis of postpartum depression: **25.3%** (22.7–27.9), 41 studies. Higher in unwanted pregnancy (43.4%) and with a history of depression (45.2%) [opened: Veisani et al., *Depress Res Treat* 2013, PMID 23936640].
  - Recent single studies:
    - Kerman 2023, 876 women: 29.1% (EPDS > 12 with DSM-5 confirmation) [opened: Dehesh 2026].
    - Ahvaz 2014: 38.8% [opened: Afshari 2020].
  - **These are mostly screening cut-offs, not diagnoses.** Studies vary widely.
- **Fathers, worldwide**
  - Paulson & Bazemore 2010: 10.4%, peaking at 25.6% at 3–6 months.
  - Cameron 2016 update: 8.4% [earlier round].
- **Iranian fathers**
  - 400 fathers: 24.5% (EPDS ≥ 10) or 16.3% (EPDS ≥ 12). "None of the participants had previously been screened" [opened: Yazdanpanahi et al., *J Reprod Infant Psychol* 2024].
  - A smaller study of 150 fathers found 38.7%, linked with marital adjustment and stress [opened: Shariat 2022].

### Iran: screening and the mental-health worker in primary care
- **Routine postnatal screening exists on paper** [opened: Iranian Ministry of Health, *برنامه کشوری مادری ایمن: مراقبت‌های ادغام‌یافته سلامت مادران (راهنمای خدمات خارج بیمارستانی) — ویژه مراقب سلامت/بهورز*, تجدید نظر نهم، ۱۴۰۲; mirrored PDF from Ahvaz University of Medical Sciences]
  - Visit schedule: postnatal care is given in **3 visits**, on days 1–3, days 10–15 and days 30–42.
  - The screening itself:
    - «در مراقبت دوم و سوم پس از زایمان غربالگری (آزمون ادینبورگ) انجام شود» ("Screening with the Edinburgh test is done at the second and third postnatal visits").
    - The booklet reproduces all 10 Persian EPDS items.
    - Action rules: score ≤ 8 is negative. A score of 9–11 (with no self-harm thoughts) means re-screening a month later. A score of 12–13 means a non-urgent referral to a doctor. A score of 14 or more means an **urgent** referral to a doctor.
  - Education at postnatal visits explicitly covers «علائم اندوه پس از زایمان، افسردگی، حمایت عاطفی همسر و وابستگان نزدیک» (signs of baby blues and depression, and emotional support from the husband and close relatives).
  - Pregnancy education includes «مسئولیت‌پذیری همسر» (the husband taking responsibility) and preparation for parenthood.
  - In pregnancy, some risk findings trigger «ارجاع غیر فوری به کارشناس سلامت روان برای غربالگری تکمیلی» (a non-urgent referral to the mental-health worker for further screening).
  - «افسردگی شدید» (severe depression) is listed among postnatal warning signs that need prompt care.
  - The booklet describes postpartum psychosis («جنون پس از زایمان») as very rare and dangerous.
  - **Implementation is unknown.** The fathers study above says none of the fathers had been screened. Fathers are not part of the programme.
- **Mental-health worker («کارشناس سلامت روان»)** [opened: full text, Hajebi et al., *Iran J Psychiatry* 2021, PMC8452841]
  - Under the Health Transformation Plan, urban comprehensive health centres have a mental-health worker. These are psychologists with an MSc in clinical psychology and 150 hours of pre-service training.
  - Staffing is about one per 30,000–40,000 urban residents. Planning had estimated one per 12,500 was needed, and budget limits cut some of their duties.
  - Duties include group education (parenting skills, life skills, self-care), brief psychological interventions and referral.
  - Community health workers («مراقب سلامت»; «بهورز» in rural areas) refer people to them and to GPs.
  - Mental health was first integrated into primary care in 1988 [opened: Dorosti 2024 abstract]. That paper lists 20 weaknesses, including workforce and pay.
- **Iranian husband-support RCT** [opened: Abbaspoor et al., *BMC Womens Health* 2023]
  - Kermanshah, 100 first-time mothers. Four online sessions for husbands.
  - Perceived support, self-efficacy and stress improved, with very large differences (for example, support 79 vs 37).
  - Small, unblinded, with surprisingly large effects. Treat as encouraging only.
- **Iranian qualitative study** [opened: Sharifipour 2023, Kermanshah, 11 mothers]: mothers' main expectation was "the sense of not being alone", with support "especially from the husband".

**Copy direction:**
- Present parent wellbeing as a practice in its own right ("looking after the person who looks after the baby"), never as a reward earned after baby tasks.
- In Iran, tell mothers they can ask the «مراقب سلامت» or «ماما» at their «مرکز خدمات جامع سلامت» about mood, at any visit. Many centres have a «کارشناس سلامت روان».
- Fathers can ask too. Say plainly that low mood after a baby happens to fathers as well.

**Don't say:**
- Do not show EPDS scores or cut-offs. Do not include a self-test.
- Do not say "1 in 4 Iranian mothers is depressed" (screening rates are not diagnoses).
- Do not say "this practice treats depression".
- Do not suggest a parent's low mood harms the baby.

## 6. Fathers and partners in year one

### Findings
- **Sarkadi 2008 systematic review, 24 longitudinal studies** [opened: abstract, *Acta Paediatr*, PMID 18052995]
  - 22 of 24 studies found positive effects of father involvement.
  - "Active and regular engagement with the child predicts a range of positive outcomes, although no specific form of engagement has been shown to yield better outcomes than another."
  - Observational. **Engagement (direct interaction)** is the form with the best support.
- **Father skin-to-skin RCTs** (small)
  - Taiwan, 83 first-time fathers, at least 15 minutes a day for 3 days: higher father–child attachment scores [opened: Chen 2017].
  - Türkiye, 165 fathers: frequent skin-to-skin (15 minutes or more, 6–7 days a week) gave higher paternal bonding scores at 3 months (72.4 vs 65.7 standard care). Nappy changing and joint soothing were also linked with bonding [opened: Yurdal & Özsoy, *Sci Rep* 2025].
  - In preterm babies, father skin-to-skin relieved pain as well as the mother's did [opened: Shukla 2021, n = 64].
- **WHO 2022 postnatal guideline** [opened: BIGG-REC]: "Interventions to promote the involvement of men during pregnancy, childbirth and after birth are recommended…", "provided they are implemented in a way that respects, promotes and facilitates women's choices and their autonomy in decision-making". Certainty is very low [search].
- **Leave and flexibility**
  - Sweden: a reform let fathers take intermittent leave alongside the mother in the postpartum period. Greater flexibility "reduces the incidence of maternal postpartum health complications" [opened: abstract, Persson & Rossin-Slater, *AEJ: Applied* 2024]. Antianxiety prescriptions fell in the first 3 months [search: secondary index, not checked].
  - Older Swedish register studies: fathers' leave was not linked with more child injuries (Laflamme 2012), and fathers who took leave had 16% lower mortality (Månsdotter 2007) [opened: abstracts]. Both are observational.
- **State of the World's Fathers 2023** (Equimundo/MenCare, about 12,000 people in 17 countries) [search]
  - Men want to do more care work but face money and norm barriers.
  - 122 countries give fathers paid leave, versus 186 for mothers.
  - Lack of replacement pay was the most common reason for not taking full leave (49%).
- **Iran.** I found no source on statutory paternity leave this session [gap]. The Ministry of Health maternal booklet builds the husband's emotional support into postnatal education [opened].

## 7. Design: why a baby goal must be extra gentle

- Sleep is at its lowest in the first 3 months, and mothers lose about an hour a night [opened: Richter 2019].
- "Moderate number of sessions and a clear-cut behavioral focus" works best [opened: Bakermans-Kranenburg 2003].
- Perinatal digital programmes rarely sustain skill use [opened: Davis 2022].
- The Care for Child Development card says to "combine play and communication activities with other care" [opened].

My design inferences:
1. Every practice rides on care the parent already does (feed, change, bath, settle). Nothing new to schedule.
2. Keep the core of each practice to **≤ 5 minutes**. The "hard week" version takes ≤ 1 minute and **counts as done**.
3. No streaks and no "you missed 3 days". Pausing is a normal state. Offer a "newborn fortnight: pause the goal" option.
4. Allow 2 weeks per practice, not 1.
5. The crying plan comes first, because it protects everyone and is a one-off task.
6. The wellbeing practice is not optional filler.
7. Make the goal shareable with a partner or grandparent, so each adult can claim a practice.

## 8. Draft goal

**Title:** Our first year together · **«سال اولِ ما با کوچولو»** (or «سال اول با نوزادمان»)
**For:** 0–12 months.
**Promise:** Small, gentle habits that help your baby feel safe and understood and help you cope, on good weeks and hard ones.
**Approach:** Six small practices, about 2 weeks each, built into feeds, changes and baths. Every practice has a 1-minute version for hard weeks, and that counts.

**Evidence strength (honest):** moderate for the building blocks, untested as a package.
- Responsive-interaction programmes improve parenting moderately in RCTs (VIPP-SD, VIP, Ferjan Ramírez).
- Crying education raises knowledge and coping, but its effect on injuries is mixed.
- Health-worker CBT for perinatal depression is strong in Pakistan, but the peer-delivered version was null and the app format is untested.
- Responsive feeding has one modest RCT.
- Father involvement evidence is mostly observational.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Make our crying plan** | Together, write 3 things that soothe your baby and the name of 1 person each of you can call. Agree that it's OK to put the baby down safely and step away for 10 minutes. Share the plan with anyone who minds the baby. | "When the crying gets to me, I'll lay baby on their back in the cot, close the door, set a 10-minute timer and breathe, then go back." | Save one name to call in your phone. | All babies have crying spells. A plan made in a calm moment is easier to follow at 3 a.m. | Barr 2009 (CMAJ); ICON; NHS; CDC; Barr 2018; Zolotor 2015 (honest: injury effect mixed) |
| 2 | **Little conversations** | Face to face, about 30 cm away. Copy your baby's sound or face, then **wait** for their reply. From 6 months, call their name and answer their babble; from 9 months, name things and wave "bye-bye". | "When I change a nappy, I'll copy one sound and wait for an answer." | Say out loud one thing you're doing ("Now the warm water…"). | Back-and-forth "conversations" are linked with language growth, and coaching parents to have them helped in a trial. | WHO/UNICEF CCD card; Ferjan Ramírez 2020; Gilkerson 2018 (correlational) |
| 3 | **A song or a book a day** | Sing the same song at the same moment each day (bath, bed), or look at one picture book or family photo together and point and name. | "When I lay baby down for the night, I'll sing our song once." | Hum one line while you hold them. | Familiar songs calmed babies in studies, and reading and play programmes from birth were linked with better social-emotional development. | Cirelli & Trehub 2020; Weisleder 2016; Mendelsohn 2018; de Bondt 2020 |

**Notes for writers**
- **Practice 5** must not say how to feed. Link to the milk-feeds guide. Also say: "If you breastfeed, your partner can still take the burping, changing and settling." Safe-sleep wording links to the existing guide; for example, don't doze with the baby on a sofa.

### When to get help (for this goal)
- **Your baby's crying sounds different** (high-pitched or weak), or comes with fever, poor feeding, floppiness, breathing trouble or a rash → see the newborn danger-signs guide and get care now.
- **You feel you might shake or hurt your baby, or can't cope right now** → put the baby down safely on their back, step away, and call your person or an emergency number. This is the right thing to do, and a good parent does it.
- **Low mood, worry or no joy most days for 2 weeks or more**, in either parent → talk to your doctor or health worker. In Iran, the «مراقب سلامت» or «ماما» at your «مرکز خدمات جامع سلامت» can talk with you, and many centres have a «کارشناس سلامت روان».
- **Thoughts of harming yourself, or frightening beliefs or confusion after birth** → urgent help. Go to the crisis page.
- **Your baby doesn't seem to react to sounds or faces**, or stops doing things they could do → ask your health worker to check hearing, seeing and development. The CCD card advises exactly this.
- **Feeding worries** (refusing feeds, fewer wet nappies, not gaining) → the feeding guides and a health worker.
- **You feel unsafe at home** → the app's help page and local services.

### Daily tips (0–12 months, ≤ 30 words each)
1. Crying spells are normal in the early months. Putting your baby down safely and stepping away for a few minutes is a responsible choice. *(ICON; NHS)*
2. Give everyone who minds your baby your crying plan, including who to call. *(Barr 2009 CMAJ: sharing the advice increased)*
3. Even a newborn sees your face and hears your voice. Feeds are a good time to look and talk. *(WHO/UNICEF CCD card)*
4. Copy your baby's coo, then wait. Your pause is their turn to "talk". *(CCD card; Ferjan Ramírez 2020)*
5. Babies often turn away when they need a break. Pausing, then coming back, is good care. *(VIPP-SD; Opie 2026)*
6. Sing the same song at the same moment each day. Familiar songs often soothe babies best. *(Cirelli & Trehub 2020)*
7. From about 6 months, a pot and a spoon make a fine toy. Clean, safe household things are enough. *(CCD card)*
8. Around 9–12 months, hide a toy under a cloth and play peek-a-boo. *(CCD card)*
9. Let your baby decide how much to eat. Stop when they turn away or close their mouth. *(WHO complementary feeding)*
10. Talk and look at your baby during meals. Mess is part of learning. *(WHO responsive feeding; recall: WHO/PAHO 2003)*
11. Partners: skin-to-skin, the bath or a nightly song can be *your* thing with the baby. *(Yurdal 2025; Sarkadi 2008)*
12. Take turns guarding each other's sleep. One longer block can help more than many short ones. *(Richter 2019; my inference, unverified)*
13. Accepting help is part of caring for your baby. Name one task someone else can take today. *(CCD card; Sharifipour 2023)*
14. Feeling low after a baby happens to fathers too. Asking "How are you, really?" helps you both. *(Paulson 2010)*
15. Good-enough care is what babies need. You don't have to get every moment right. *(Opie 2026)*
