## 1. Fireworks, bonfires and Chaharshanbe Suri («چهارشنبه‌سوری»)

### 1.1 Iran: national numbers (Emergency Organisation, «سازمان اورژانس کشور»)

| Year (festival) | Injured | Deaths | Eye injuries | Amputations | Burns | Children | Source / status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1403 (Mar 2025)** | **6,419** (up 23% on the year before) | **21** | **1,833** (down 6%) | **258** (down 1%) | **1,705** (up 7%) | **44% of the injured were under 18** | [opened] Aftab News, 29 Esfand 1403, citing IRNA. Figures given by Jafar Miadfar, head of the Emergency Organisation. |
| 1403, interim | 2,466 (2,410 taken to hospital), 1,040 burns, about 933 eye, 172 amputations | at least 14–21 | — | — | — | average age 19, youngest a 5-year-old boy | [search] Radio Farda and others. These look like **counts made before the final report**. Do not use them. |
| **1404 (Mar 2026)** | **945** | **2** (both in Fars) | **240** | **40** | **200** | not given | [opened] Kojaro, 27 Esfand 1404, citing ISNA and Miadfar. He described this as "90% fewer deaths and 70% fewer injured" than the year before. |

Problems to flag:
- 945 is about **85% lower** than 6,419, not 70%. The two years were probably **counted over different windows** (one night versus several days or weeks), or the 1404 figure is interim. **Do not show a year-on-year trend** in the app.
- The 1403 article says **78% of the injured were using fireworks themselves** and **28% were passers-by** ("۲۸ درصد رهگذر"), plus 1% rescue workers. That adds up to more than 100%. Quote neither share alone without the original release.
- Most-injured provinces: Tehran in 1403 [opened]; East Azerbaijan, West Azerbaijan and Ardabil in 1404 [opened].

### 1.2 Iran: research studies (all [opened] abstracts via Europe PMC)

- **National, 2009 festival** (Ministry of Health database; *J Inj Violence Res* 2013, DOI 10.5249/jivr.v5i1.146): **1,817 injured**. Most injured sites: **hand 43.3%, eye 24.5%, face 13.2%**. Types: burns 39.9%, bruises/abrasions 24.6%, cuts 12.7%.
- **Tehran, 2007** (Tehran EMS; *J Emerg Med* 2011, PMID 20097501): 197 patients, mean age 20.9, mostly male. **"Fuse-detonated noisemakers and homemade grenades" («نارنجک دست‌ساز») were the most frequent causes.** Hand injury in 39.8%. Six amputations and 12 long-term disabilities.
- **East Azerbaijan, 2018** (all hospitals; *J Inj Violence Res* 2019, PMC7187064): 318 injured (9 per 100,000). 80% male. **53.8% under 20.** 65% were hurt while using firecrackers, **32% while watching or passing by**, and 3% while making them. 57% burns, 43% cuts/wounds. The authors conclude that most injuries are in "urban boys under the age of 20", so prevention should reach **them and their parents**.
- **Tabriz, 2014** (Sina hospital; *Trauma Mon* 2016, PMID 27921014): 49 burn patients aged **3 to 36** (mean 17). The authors warn that **walking in the street or driving with open windows** is dangerous on the festival night.

### 1.3 Iran: official safety advice

**Ministry of Health package**, «بسته آموزشی و اطلاعاتی چالش‌های چهارشنبه‌سوری» [opened]. Publisher: Ministry of Health, Deputy for Health, Office of Health Education and Promotion. It was hosted by Iran University of Medical Sciences (uploaded Mar 2024). The edition date is not printed. It mentions incidents from 1391, so the text is probably about ten years old.

- **Eye injury, 4 tips:**
  1. Don't rub the eyes or eyelids. Go to an **eye emergency** at the first chance, because some eye injuries show few signs at first but can still be serious.
  2. Cover the eye with something rigid, such as a **disposable cup or a cardboard cone**. **Do not** put tissue, cotton or gauze on the eye.
  3. **Don't use any ointment or eye drops, and don't wash the eye**, because this can make the injury worse.
  4. Don't take painkillers and don't eat until you reach the eye emergency, in case surgery is needed. (The app must not give medicine advice. At most say "don't give food or drink before an eye doctor has seen them". Check this with an ophthalmologist.)
- **Burns:** cool with **cold, clean water for at least 10 minutes**. Remove rings, bangles, belts and watches. Don't pull off clothing that is stuck to the skin; cut around it. Burns to the hands, face, eyes and genitals always need a doctor. (The app's burn card may say 20 minutes, as NHS and ANZBA advice does [recall, unverified]. **Keep the guide consistent with the existing card.**)
- **Parents, 5 tips:**
  - Children often **buy fireworks secretly and hide them in pockets, bags or cupboards**. Explain the risks, keep an eye on pocket money in these weeks, and keep flammable things out of reach.
  - It is hard to keep children away from the excitement completely, but parents can **"moderate it and make the night safer"**.
  - Explain the risks and family rules. **Go with your child, or send a trusted adult.**
  - Keep matches, kerosene and petrol out of reach. Don't let children burn bushes or wood at home or in the car park.
  - At the end of the night, **wait 15–20 minutes** before collecting firework debris, then put it in a bucket of water. Make sure the fire is out.
- **Noise:** spend the night somewhere quieter, or use **ear protection, especially for pregnant women, children and older people**.
- **Other:** keep a powder fire extinguisher nearby. Never pour petrol or kerosene on a fire. Don't carry fireworks in pockets. **Fire service 125.**
- **Wording to avoid:** the package uses the pun **«چهارشنبه‌سوزی»** ("Wednesday of burning"). **Don't copy it.** It reads as mocking the festival.

**Ministry of Health / university advice** (snippets only) [search]:
- Avoid **jumping over the fire, especially in a chador, manteau or loose clothing** («از پریدن روی آتش به خصوص با چادر، مانتو و یا لباس‌های گشاد اجتناب کنیم»).
- Don't light fires in narrow alleys or near electricity substations, gas pressure stations or car parks.
- **Don't relight firecrackers or sparklers that didn't go off.**
- Working parents are advised to come home earlier that day to be with their children.
- Keep gas canisters, insecticide cans and aerosols away from children in the last days of the year.
- One source advises **protective glasses** (or at least sunglasses) near fireworks.

**Red Crescent:** I found no Red Crescent-specific advice this session.

### 1.4 Global: US CPSC

- **2024 figures** [opened] (CPSC press release, June 2025):
  - **11 deaths** and an estimated **14,700 emergency-department-treated injuries**.
  - Sparklers: an estimated **1,700** of those injuries. CPSC says sparklers burn at **"about 2,000 degrees Fahrenheit"** (about 1,100°C). (The brief's figure of "~1,200°C" is close. Some sources give higher numbers [recall, unverified]. **Use "about 1,000°C, hot enough to melt some metals" or simply "hotter than a cooker flame"**, and check the wording.)
  - Body parts: hands/fingers 36%, head/face/ears 22%. Burns were 37% of visits.
  - Tips: never let young children play with or light fireworks, **including sparklers**; keep water nearby; never relight a dud; light one at a time and move back.
- **2023 Annual Report** [opened] (CPSC, June 2024):
  - **Children under 15 had 31% of injuries** in the special study month.
  - Ages **5–9 had the second-highest injury rate** (4.0 per 100,000). Ages 15–19 had the highest.
  - **For children under 5, sparklers caused 51% of injuries.**
  - Eyes were 19% of injuries.
  - CPSC notes that victims are often hurt by fireworks **set off by other people**.

**Eye first aid, international** [search] (multiple US eye-hospital pages; AAO page blocked): **don't rub, rinse or press on the eye; don't remove stuck objects; shield it loosely with a rigid cup; get care immediately**. Gently move a child's hand away from the eye.
- **Chemical splashes are the exception:** they need immediate flushing with water [recall, unverified]. The fireworks advice "don't wash" is for impact and penetrating injuries. **The guide should send parents to the existing eye-injury card, which must handle both cases.**

**Copy direction:**
- Open with respect. Chaharshanbe Suri is an old, joyful family tradition of light and spring. Families can keep it, and the guide is about keeping hands and eyes safe.
- Child-specific rules:
  - Young children **watch from a distance** («تماشا از فاصله»), held or with an adult.
  - **No sparklers for under-5s.** CPSC says never for young children, and half of under-5 firework injuries come from sparklers.
  - **Nothing homemade, ever.** Homemade "grenades" and noisemakers were the leading cause in Tehran.
  - **Never relight a dud.**
  - Fitted clothes, no chador or loose clothing near the fire, and hair tied back.
  - A bucket of water nearby.
- For older children and teens:
  - The data are mostly about **teenage boys**, so speak to the parent of a 9–12-year-old about the coming teen years.
  - Talk about pocket-money purchases. Go with them or arrange a trusted adult.
  - Agree a plan in advance rather than banning everything outright. The Ministry package itself says forced bans usually fail.
- **Bystanders get hurt too.** Iranian studies show many were watching or passing by. Keep children away from groups setting off bangers in the street, and keep car windows closed on the night.
- **Ear protection** for babies and toddlers.
- Safe-celebration ideas:
  - a small, adult-tended fire in an open space;
  - **«قاشق‌زنی»** and family gatherings, nuts and treats (**«آجیل چهارشنبه‌سوری»**), music and lanterns;
  - watching official or organised events from a distance.
- Jumping over fire: sources advise against it in loose clothing. Present it as "**if your family jumps, keep the fire very small and low, only older children, fitted clothes, one at a time, an adult beside the fire**". Never encourage young children to jump. (Note: the Ministry snippet is about loose clothing. No source gave a "safe height"; **don't invent one**.)
- Eye injury: three verbs. **Don't rub, cover with a cup, go now** («نمالید، با لیوان بپوشانید، فوراً به اورژانس چشم»). Link to the eye card. Emergency **115**, fire **125**.

**Don't say:**
- «چهارشنبه‌سوزی», "scene of horror", or any claim that the festival is un-Islamic or "just Zoroastrian" (no religious framing at all).
- Graphic injury descriptions or blinding stories.
- "Sparklers are safe for kids."
- Specific numbers of injured "this year" without a date and source.
- A trend claim built from 1403 vs 1404.
- "Wash the eye" for firework impact injuries.
- Anything that blames parents of injured children.

## 2. Dogs, cats and rabies in Iran

### 2.1 Iran numbers

- **Bites per year:**
  - **386,521** animal bites in **1402**, with **18** human rabies cases (Ministry of Health Centre for Communicable Disease Control, via news) [search].
  - Another official gave about **440,000 in 1403** and 445,000 the year before [search]. The two sources' numbers and time periods differ. **Say "hundreds of thousands a year" («سالانه صدها هزار مورد»).**
- **National registry, Mar 2022–Sep 2023** [opened abstract] (*PLoS Negl Trop Dis* 2026, DOI 10.1371/journal.pntd.0014751):
  - 507,404 recorded exposures. **Dogs 76.2%, cats 20.8%.** Males 73.6%. The largest age group was 31–40.
  - Highest incidence: North Khorasan (773 per 100,000) and Golestan (767 per 100,000).
  - Wound irrigation was documented in 94.3% of cases, and 96.4% got the first vaccine dose.
- **Rabies deaths:**
  - **69 deaths in 2012–2021**, most often in **males aged 10–14** (*Health Sci Rep* 2026, DOI 10.1002/hsr2.72233) [opened abstract].
  - Officials say **10–20 cases a year**. The Global Alliance for Rabies Control estimates **20–90** [search].
  - Dogs cause about **80%** of human rabies cases in Iran, according to the Ministry [search].
- **Cats:** an official said **25,000–30,000 bites a year are from pet cats**, and **no human rabies death from a cat bite has been reported** in Iran [search]. (Still treat every cat bite as an exposure. See 2.2.)
- **Local registry studies** [opened abstracts]:
  - Sarakhs (2017–2024): 790 per 100,000 a year. 90.1% dogs. 84.4% rural. Students were the most affected occupational group (28.1%).
  - Khoy (2021–2024): 91% dogs. Peak in summer and early autumn.
- **Children's share:** no national figure was found.
  - One provincial study (31,825 cases) put the largest share, 18%, in **ages 8–14** [search, garbled text].
  - The 69-death series peaks at 10–14 [opened].
- **Knowledge gap** (Sari, 2024–25; *Arch Acad Emerg Med* 2026) [opened abstract]: among bitten people seen in emergency departments, **only 31.3% knew to wash the wound with soap and water**, and some went to **traditional healers first**.
- **Free care:**
  - Officials and university brochures say people can go to **rabies prevention and treatment centres («مراکز پیشگیری و درمان هاری») free of charge, many open 24 hours** [search]. One source is from 1398, so **check that it still applies**.
  - Centres wash and disinfect wounds and give the vaccine, and rabies serum or tetanus protection when needed.

### 2.2 National guideline and WHO

**National guideline**: «دستورالعمل کشوری کنترل حیوان‌گزیدگی و هاری در انسان», Pasteur Institute of Iran with the Ministry of Health CDC, **1398 (2019)** [opened PDF]:
- Wound care is "the most important part of rabies prevention". **Wash each wound separately for at least 15 minutes** (15–20 minutes) with water, warm if possible, and soap. Then rinse the soap out and disinfect.
- The guideline notes that some rabies cases followed **small scratches** that were not washed.
- **Any contact** with an animal in a rabies-endemic country counts as a possible exposure.
- Teaching people how to behave with animals is needed for everyone, **"especially children"**.
- Advice for children:
  - Assume **unknown dogs are dangerous** until shown otherwise.
  - Don't approach dogs that are chained or tied for long periods.
  - **Never run from an angry dog. "Be like a tree"** («همانند یک درخت باشند»).
  - If a big dog is near and you have a small child, pick the child up slowly, avoid eye contact with the dog, and keep the child quiet.
- (The guideline also has adult self-defence tips, such as using a bicycle as a shield and pepper spray. **Don't use these in the app.**)

**WHO rabies fact sheet** (17 Sep 2026) [opened]:
- **"Immediately and always wash the wound with water and soap for at least 15 minutes and seek medical attention."**
- Dogs cause **99%** of human cases worldwide. **40% of people bitten by suspected rabid animals are children under 15.**
- Once symptoms appear, rabies is **"fatal in 100% of cases"**. Prompt post-exposure care (washing, vaccine, and immunoglobulin when needed) prevents it.
- Exposure categories:
  - **I**: touching, feeding or licks on intact skin. Wash only.
  - **II**: nibbling of uncovered skin, or minor scratches without bleeding. Wash and vaccinate.
  - **III**: bites or scratches through the skin, or saliva on broken skin or mucous membranes. Wash, vaccinate and give immunoglobulin.
- Children aged **5–14** are frequent victims.

### 2.3 Where on the body and why children

- **Pediatric emergency department series**, 2023–2024, 711 children, mean age 7.5 (*Ann Med Surg* 2026) [opened abstract]:
  - **63% had head/face/neck injuries**, and these children were younger and smaller.
  - **Family dogs caused 41%.**
  - **43% followed "inappropriate child–dog interactions".**
- **Review of 50 studies** (*Children* 2024, PMID 39457183) [opened abstract]: younger children often get head/neck bites, and **PTSD symptoms are common afterwards**: nightmares, anxiety, avoidance.
- Iranian registry studies show **lower limbs** as the most common site overall [search]. This is mostly adults and stray dogs outdoors. Children's face bites are a pattern from Western pet-dog studies, and the Iranian child pattern is not known.

### 2.4 Does teaching children about dogs work?

- **Cochrane review** (Duperrex et al., 2009, CD004726) [opened abstract]:
  - Two trials met the criteria. **"No study looked at our main outcome: dog bite rates."**
  - Education improved knowledge and observed behaviour in under-10s.
  - Conclusion: **"There is no direct evidence that educational programmes can reduce dog bite rates."** Education alone is not enough.
- **The Blue Dog** [opened abstracts]:
  - Randomised trial in 76 children aged 3.5–6 (*J Pediatr Psychol* 2012): **better recognition of risky situations**, but **no change in behaviour with a real dog**.
  - Parent trial (*Accid Anal Prev* 2013): **no improvement in parents' supervision**. Parents often **encouraged children to approach an unfamiliar dog**.
  - The authors say to **target parent behaviour, not just children**.

**Copy direction:**
- Two halves: **prevention** and **what to do after any bite or scratch**.
- **After a bite or scratch (Iran):**
  1. Wash every wound with soap and running water for **15 minutes by the clock** (use a phone timer). This includes small scratches and cat scratches.
  2. Go **the same day** to the nearest rabies prevention centre («مرکز پیشگیری و درمان هاری»), at a health centre or hospital. In Iran the service is usually free.
  3. Don't wait to "see if the dog gets sick" before going.
  4. Don't use traditional remedies on the wound instead of care.
  - Link to the dog-bite card.
- **Pet dogs at home:** most bites to young children in the international data come from **familiar dogs**.
  - **Never leave a baby or toddler alone with any dog**, even a gentle one.
  - Give the dog a child-free resting place.
  - Don't let children hug, kiss face-to-face, or take toys or food from a dog.
- **Children's rules** (age-adapted):
  - Ask the owner before touching any dog.
  - Leave alone dogs that are **eating, sleeping, with puppies, tied up or chained**.
  - Never approach street dogs («سگ‌های بدون صاحب / ولگرد»).
  - If a dog comes close: **stand still like a tree, arms folded, look at your feet, don't run, don't scream**. If knocked down, **curl into a ball, protecting the face and neck** [recall, unverified for the "ball" step; the tree step is in the Iranian guideline].
- **Be honest:** teaching children helps them recognise risk, but studies haven't shown it cuts bites on its own. **Adult supervision and managing the dog matter most.**
- **Cats:** cat bites and scratches also need washing and the rabies centre. Don't let children chase, corner or pick up cats they don't know. Also keep away from any animal that is acting strangely, including wild or farm animals (foxes, jackals, livestock, bats) [WHO categories, opened].
- **After a frightening bite:** nightmares and fear are common. Comfort the child. If fear lasts for weeks, talk to the child's doctor.
- **Report** dangerous or roaming dogs to the municipality [Iranian guideline].

**Don't say:**
- "Rabies is rare, so a scratch is nothing."
- "Watch the dog for 10 days instead of going to the centre." (Observing the dog is the health worker's decision, not a substitute.)
- That any breed is "safe" or "dangerous". The evidence on breed-based rules is weak [opened, *Animals* 2026 abstract].
- "Teaching your child will prevent bites."
- Graphic bite or rabies-symptom descriptions.
- Advice to hit or spray dogs.
- Religious statements about keeping dogs.

## 3. Furniture and TV tip-overs

- **CPSC press release, 8 Feb 2024** [opened], covering its tip-over report:
  - About **17,800 people a year** were treated in emergency departments for tip-over injuries (2020–2022). **Children under 18 were 44%: one child every 53 minutes.**
  - **217 tip-over deaths** reported from 2013 to July 2023 (all ages). **47% involved a TV.**
  - **55% of deaths involved children aged 1–3.** The release also cites "137 child fatalities" in the same window. How 55% relates to 137 is unclear from the release, so **check the PDF**.
  - Furniture was involved in 82% of injuries.
  - Advice: anchor TVs and furniture (bookcases, dressers) to the wall. If anchoring isn't possible, use a **sturdy, low base and push the TV back**. Keep cords out of reach. **Don't store toys or remotes where children would climb for them. Keep heavy items on lower shelves.**
  - A mandatory US furniture stability rule now applies under the **STURDY Act** (2023).
- **AAP HealthyChildren** (updated 1 Dec 2021) [opened]:
  - A child is taken to an emergency department **"at least once every single hour"**.
  - **77% of tip-over deaths in 2000–2019 were children under 6.**
  - **About half of children's fatal incidents happen in bedrooms**, sometimes when parents think the child is asleep.
  - Causes: climbing to reach things, **heavy items on top**, **several drawers open at once**, top-heavy designs, and furniture **standing on carpet**.
  - Tips:
    - Use **metal anchors** (L-brackets); plastic can become brittle.
    - Use more anchors for heavier furniture.
    - **Fit drawer stops.**
    - **Never put a TV on a dresser**, especially in a child's room.
    - **Wall-mount flat screens** or strap them down.
    - **Recycle old tube TVs.** Don't move them to a child's room.
    - **Renters:** ask the landlord. Wall holes can be repaired.
- **CPSC recalled millions of anti-tip kits** (Jan 2024) because **plastic zip ties weakened over time** [search]. This supports the metal-anchor advice.
- **Iran:** **no data found** [search]. Iranian furniture and TV habits (wall units, TVs on stands, heavy carpets) make the risk plausible, but this is not evidence.
- **Overlap with the earthquake guide:** the app's earthquake guide already recommends fixing tall furniture [recall from app scope]. **One action serves two purposes**, which makes a strong, non-alarming message for Iranian parents.

**Copy direction:**
- Frame it as a **one-afternoon job** («یک کار یک‌روزه»): metal brackets for wardrobes, bookcases, dressers and the TV; heavy things on low shelves; nothing tempting on top.
- Tie it to **the toddler climbing stage**, from about 1 year (the AAP highest-risk group is under 6).
- Mention the **earthquake double benefit**.
- **Bedrooms first**, because children are alone there.
- Give tips that work for renters.

**Don't say:**
- Graphic crush descriptions.
- "Your toddler shouldn't climb." Climbing is normal development, so make climbing safe instead.
- Iranian statistics (none exist).
- Brand recommendations.

## 4. Drowning in Iranian settings

### 4.1 Global

- **WHO drowning fact sheet** (1 May 2026) [opened]:
  - About **300,000 deaths a year**, 92% in low- and middle-income countries.
  - **"Children aged under 5 years account for nearly a quarter of all drowning deaths."**
  - Drowning is the **4th leading cause of death at ages 1–4** and the **3rd at ages 5–14**.
  - Interventions:
    - **"Covering wells, using doorway barriers and playpens, fencing swimming pools"**, and controlling access to water.
    - **Supervised childcare for pre-school children.**
    - **Teaching school-age children basic swimming, water safety and safe rescue skills.**
    - Flood preparedness and boating rules.
- **WHO Global status report on drowning prevention** (Dec 2024) [search]:
  - Drowning death rate **fell 38% from 2000 to 2021**, but there were still over 300,000 deaths.
  - The under-5 share is given as **24%** in one summary and **21% (over 62,000 deaths)** in another. **Check in the report** (ISBN 978-92-4-010396-2).
- **AAP 2026 policy statement and technical report, "Prevention of Drowning"** (*Pediatrics* 2026, DOI 10.1542/peds.2026-077410 / -077412) [opened abstracts]:
  - **"No single method is effective"**, so **multiple layers** are needed.
  - Proven layers:
    - **4-sided isolation pool fencing** with **self-closing, self-latching gates**;
    - **"close, constant, attentive, and competent supervision"**;
    - **swimming competency**;
    - **life jackets** when boating;
    - **early rescue and resuscitation**.
  - 981 US children under 20 drowned in 2023.
- **AAP HealthyChildren, swim lessons** (updated 18 May 2026) [opened]:
  - Lessons **"can begin for many children starting at age 1"**, depending on readiness.
  - **"No evidence that infant swim programs for babies under 1 year old lower their drowning risk"**. Parent–baby water play is fine for fun.
  - **"Swim lessons don't make kids 'drown proof.'"**
  - At gatherings, choose a **"water watcher"** who keeps eyes on the children and **hands over to another adult about every 15–20 minutes**.
  - For infants and toddlers, an adult stays **within arm's reach ("touch supervision")**, even during lessons.
  - Lessons should teach getting back to the surface and self-rescue.
- **Swimming lessons evidence** (Brenner et al., *Arch Pediatr Adolesc Med* 2009, case-control) [opened abstract]:
  - Ages 1–4: formal lessons were associated with **88% lower drowning risk** (adjusted odds ratio 0.12, 95% CI 0.01–0.97). This was based on only 61 cases, so the **confidence interval is very wide**.
  - Ages 5–19: no significant association (0.36, 95% CI 0.09–1.51).
  - **Say "may lower the risk", not "cuts it by 88%".**
- **Cochrane pool fencing review** (Thompson & Rivara, 2000, CD001047) [opened abstract]:
  - Three case-control studies, no randomised trials.
  - Fenced vs unfenced pool: odds ratio **0.27** for drowning or near-drowning.
  - **Isolation fencing** (pool only) vs three-sided or property fencing: **0.17**. Perimeter fencing still lets a child reach the pool **through the house**.
  - The evidence is old but consistent with WHO and AAP.

### 4.2 Iran

- **National trend** (Legal Medicine registry, 2005–2022; *Inj Prev* 2026, DOI 10.1136/ip-2024-045356) [opened abstract]:
  - **19,547 drowning deaths** over 17 years. The rate fell from **18.69 to 12.87 per million**.
  - High-risk clusters were in the **north, south-east and centre** of the country.
- **Recent years** (Legal Medicine via news) [search]:
  - **Over 1,000 drowning deaths a year in 1400–1403**, and **888 in 1404**, the lowest in five years. The most in 1404 were in Khuzestan (123), Fars (83) and Mazandaran (74).
  - **Swimming pools: 103 deaths in 1403.**
  - **Rivers: 316 deaths in 1400** (27.1% of all drownings that year).
- **Children's share:** under-5s were **about 10%** of drowning deaths in 2017–2020 (provincial trend study, *J Inj Violence Res* 2022) [search; abstract not available].
- **Rank among child injury deaths:**
  - **Mazandaran**, under-5 deaths 2013–2022 (*Sci Rep* 2026) [opened abstract]: of 240 unintentional-injury deaths, transport caused 56.7% and **drowning 10.8%, the 2nd cause**, followed by other threats to breathing at 10.0%.
  - **Southern Iran (Fars)**, ages 0–19, 2004–2019 (*BMJ Paediatr Open* 2023) [opened abstract]: drowning was **the 2nd cause of years of life lost** (7.19%), after transport (67.4%) and ahead of burns (6.7%).
- **Where small children drown: two provincial series** (1–59 months, 2013–2017, both *J Inj Violence Res* 2019) [opened abstracts]:
  - **Hormozgan**, 63 deaths: **pools 30.1%**, natural water 22.2%, **water buckets 15.9%**, **agricultural ponds («استخر کشاورزی») 11.1%**, **bathtub 9.5%**, wastewater wells 4.8%, **yard water storage («منبع آب حیاط») 4.8%**, and one in a household fuel container.
    - In Hormozgan, drowning was the most common cause of unintentional-injury death after traffic.
  - **Tabriz**, 14 deaths: **pool or traditional courtyard pool («حوض») 21%**, **floods 29%**, natural water and rivers 50%. 60% rural. Most were aged **12 months to 4 years**.
    - The authors recommend fencing pools, keeping children away from bridges and flood paths, and never leaving them alone.
- **News and local health advice** [search]:
  - Kerman: two children aged 5 and 6 drowned in an **unfenced agricultural pool**. The parents had not noticed they were gone.
  - Kashan: most drownings happen in **non-designated places**: agricultural pools, dams, rivers and canals.
  - A torn mesh cover on a water tank failed to protect one child.
  - University health pages recommend **fencing household and farm pools and «حوض»**, and educating parents of under-5s.
- **Caspian coast:** the north is a high-risk cluster [opened]. A Guilan study of 600 rescued cases from 2018 to 2023 found **unsafe or unguarded areas carried far higher death risk than safe swimming zones** (reported as 33-fold; abstract truncated, *Arch Acad Emerg Med* 2026) [opened abstract, partial]. Prevention in Guilan focuses on **young men and adolescent boys** (Haddon-matrix focus groups, *Heliyon* 2023) [opened abstract].

**Copy direction:**
- **By age.**
  - **Babies and toddlers** drown in **small amounts of water at home**: bath, bucket, «حوض», the yard water tank, the toilet.
    - Empty buckets and basins right away.
    - Stay **within arm's reach** in the bath and never leave, "not even to answer the door".
    - **Cover or fence the «حوض» and water tanks** with something a child cannot open or tear.
    - Use a closed door or gate to the yard.
  - **Village and farm settings:** agricultural ponds and canals.
    - Fencing is the owner's responsibility, but parents can ask for it.
    - Teach children these are "never" places.
    - Know where your children are when you are near farms.
  - **Holidays:** rivers, dams, the Caspian.
    - Swim **only at guarded beaches with a lifeguard**.
    - Use a **life jacket** in boats and around open water for non-swimmers.
    - Appoint a **water watcher** («نگهبان آب») who has no phone, no tea and no conversation, and rotate every 15–20 minutes.
    - Rivers can rise suddenly, and flood water is never for play.
  - **School-age children:** **swimming lessons** with water-safety and self-rescue skills.
    - Lessons are **one layer**, not a guarantee.
    - From age 1 for many children, if available and when they are ready (AAP).
- **"Layers" metaphor:** barriers + eyes + skills + life jacket + knowing what to do. Link to the drowning first-aid card. Emergency **115**.
- Drowning is **quick and quiet** [search; consistent with WHO and AAP]. Present this as a reason for the water-watcher habit, not to scare.
- Single-sex pools and family settings: the water-watcher idea works in any setting. **Mothers at women's pool sessions can rotate the role too.**

**Don't say:**
- "Swimming lessons make a child safe / drown-proof."
- "Baby swimming classes prevent drowning."
- "88% reduction".
- Inflatable armbands or rings as safety devices. They are not life jackets [recall, unverified; check the AAP wording].
- Specific Iranian yearly totals as current without a date.
- Anything implying the parents of drowned children were negligent. The Kerman story **should not be retold** in the app.

## 5. Optional: helmets (and what was not covered)

- **Cochrane review, bicycle helmets** (Thompson, Rivara & Thompson, 2000, CD001855) [opened abstract]:
  - Five good-quality case-control studies, no randomised trials.
  - Helmets gave a **63–88% reduction in the risk of head, brain and severe brain injury** for cyclists of all ages. Protection was similar in crashes with motor vehicles (69%) and in other crashes (68%).
  - **Upper and mid-face injuries were 65% lower.**
  - The review is old and the evidence is observational. Later debates about helmet laws are a separate question [recall, unverified].
- **E-scooters, skates and scooters:** not researched this round. A helmet for wheeled toys is common advice [recall, unverified].
- **Electric shock / exposed wiring:** not researched. Iranian homes may have exposed wiring and unearthed sockets [recall, unverified]. **This needs its own round before any copy.**

**Copy direction:** a short "helmet on every ride" tip inside an existing road or outdoor guide is enough for now. Fitting: snug, level, straps under the ears [recall, unverified; check the AAP or Raising Children wording].

## Recommended outlines for the new guides

### Guide A: «چهارشنبه‌سوری شاد و امن» / "A safe and happy Chaharshanbe Suri"
1. **Welcome:** a loved tradition; this guide helps keep eyes and hands safe. One line of data: in 1403 the Emergency Organisation reported thousands injured, **44% under 18**, with eye and hand injuries common (dated, sourced).
2. **Before the night:**
   - Talk with older children about fireworks and pocket money; agree a plan.
   - **No homemade explosives.** Check bags and pockets kindly.
   - Make an ear-protection plan for the baby.
3. **On the night, by age:**
   - Under 5: watch from a distance, in an adult's arms, no sparklers.
   - 5–8: hold an adult's hand, stay back from the fire and bangers.
   - 9–12: an adult with them at all times, no relighting duds, fitted clothes.
4. **The fire:** small, open space, away from cars and power boxes, water bucket ready. A jumping note: only if the family does it, small and low fire, older children only, fitted clothes, hair tied, an adult beside.
5. **In the street and car:** windows closed, avoid groups setting off bangers.
6. **After:** wait 15–20 minutes before collecting debris, soak it in water, make sure the fire is out.
7. **If something happens:** eye injury (don't rub, cover with a cup, go now) and the **eye card**; burn and the **burn card**; 115 / 125.
8. **Fun alternatives:** «قاشق‌زنی», «آجیل», lanterns, music, organised events.

### Guide B: «بچه‌ها، سگ‌ها و گربه‌ها» / "Children, dogs and cats"
1. Why it matters in Iran: hundreds of thousands of bites a year; rabies is preventable with fast care; free rabies centres.
2. **If bitten or scratched:** 15 minutes of soap and water by timer, then a rabies centre the same day. Link to the **dog-bite card**.
3. Family dogs: never alone with a baby or toddler, a dog's safe space, no hugging or face-to-face play.
4. Street dogs and unknown animals: the children's rules, "be a tree", cats too.
5. Reading a dog: stiff body, growl, ears back, means walk away slowly (keep it simple) [recall, unverified].
6. Honest box: teaching helps children recognise risk, but adults and dog management prevent most bites.
7. After a scary bite: feelings and reassurance; when to ask for help.

### Guide C: «مبلمان و تلویزیون را محکم کنید» / "Anchor furniture and TVs"
1. Why toddlers climb, and why heavy furniture tips over (drawers as steps, TV on top, carpet).
2. The one-afternoon checklist: metal brackets, TV wall-mount or strap, heavy items low, drawer stops, nothing tempting on top.
3. Bedrooms first.
4. Earthquake double benefit (link to the earthquake guide).
5. Renters and shared housing tips.

### Guide D: «آب و کودک: لایه‌های ایمنی» / "Water safety in layers"
1. Drowning is quick and quiet, and toddlers can drown in very little water. (Data: one of the top causes of injury death in young children worldwide and in Iranian provinces.)
2. **At home:** bath (arm's reach), buckets and basins emptied, «حوض» and water tanks covered or fenced, toilet lid, home pool fenced on four sides with a self-closing gate.
3. **Farms and villages:** agricultural ponds, canals and wells; "never places"; ask owners to fence.
4. **Trips:** guarded beaches only, water watcher rota, life jackets in boats, rivers and floods.
5. **Swimming lessons:** from about age 1 if ready; school-age swim and self-rescue; not drown-proof.
6. **If it happens:** get them out safely, call 115, and use the **drowning card**.

## Daily tips (each ≤30 words)

1. Small fires and big joy: on Chaharshanbe Suri, let young children watch from an adult's arms, well back from the fire and from bangers. (Ministry of Health package; East Azerbaijan 2018 study)
2. Sparklers can burn at about 1,000°C. For children under five, enjoy the lights from a distance, not in their hands. (US CPSC 2025 release; CPSC 2023 report)
3. Firework that didn't go off? Leave it. Never relight or pick one up. Pour water over it once it has sat for a while. (US CPSC 2025; Ministry of Health package)
4. Eye hit by anything on festival night: don't rub, cover gently with a paper cup, and go straight to an eye emergency. (Ministry of Health Chaharshanbe Suri package)
5. Planning to jump over the fire? Keep it small and low, fitted clothes only, hair tied back, and an adult beside the fire. (Ministry of Health advice on loose clothing [search])
6. Any animal bite or scratch: wash it with soap and running water for 15 minutes by the clock, then visit a rabies centre the same day. (WHO 2026; Iran national rabies guideline 1398)
7. Never leave a baby or toddler alone with a dog, even a gentle family dog. Most bites to young children involve dogs they know. (Pediatric ED study, Ann Med Surg 2026)
8. Teach "be a tree": if a strange dog comes close, stand still, fold your arms, look at your feet, and don't run. (Iran national rabies guideline 1398)
9. Dogs that are eating, sleeping, tied up or with puppies need space. Teach children to leave them alone and always ask an owner first. (Iran national rabies guideline 1398; WHO)
10. Anchor tall wardrobes, bookcases and the TV with metal brackets. It protects climbing toddlers and helps in earthquakes too. (AAP HealthyChildren 2021; CPSC 2024)
11. Keep toys, remotes and treats off the tops of furniture, so little climbers have no reason to scale it. (CPSC 2024; AAP HealthyChildren 2021)
12. Babies and toddlers can drown in a bucket or bath. Empty water containers straight after use and stay within arm's reach at bath time. (Hormozgan study 2019; AAP 2026)
13. Cover or fence the «حوض» and the yard water tank with something a child cannot lift, open or tear. (WHO 2026; Hormozgan and Tabriz studies 2019)
14. At the beach or pool, name one "water watcher": no phone, eyes on the children, hand over every 15–20 minutes. (AAP HealthyChildren 2026)
