# Research Brief — Play That Helps Children Grow (0–8 years)

> **Corrections:** [RESEARCH-3.md](RESEARCH-3.md) carries newer findings on kendama, bilingual advantages, the 75% wording and AAP screen limits.

This brief summarises the evidence gathered before designing **PlayNest**. It is the
knowledge base the app's content, recommendation logic and guidance are built on.

---

## 1. Why play matters (the big picture)

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Play is not frivolous: it enhances brain structure and function and builds executive function — "the process of learning rather than the content". Pediatricians are urged to write a **"prescription for play"** at well visits. | AAP clinical report *The Power of Play* (Yogman et al., *Pediatrics*, 2018) |
| Shared, attuned play between parent and child buffers the body's stress response; play matters even more when children face adversity. | AAP *Power of Play* |
| Learning through play is **joyful, meaningful, actively engaging, iterative and socially interactive**. Not every characteristic is needed every time, but over time children should experience all five. | LEGO Foundation white paper *Learning through Play* (2017) |
| **Serve and return** — responsive back-and-forth interaction — is the foundation of brain architecture. Five steps: *notice the serve*, *return the serve*, *give it a name*, *take turns and wait*, *practise endings and beginnings*. Everyday moments (shopping, cooking, waiting) are ideal. | Harvard Center on the Developing Child |
| Back-and-forth **conversational turns** predicted language-area (Broca's) brain activity and verbal skill more strongly than the raw number of words a child heard, independent of family income. | Romeo et al., MIT, *Psychological Science* 2018 (n=36, correlational) |
| WHO/UNICEF **Nurturing Care** & *Care for Child Development* give age-banded "play" and "communicate" advice used worldwide (e.g. newborn: skin-to-skin, look into eyes and talk; 6–9 m: hide a toy under a cloth, peek-a-boo; 12 m–2 y: stack, fill and empty containers, picture books). | WHO/UNICEF Care for Child Development counselling cards (2012) |

### Design implications
- The app must **get the parent off the phone and onto the floor** quickly: glanceable steps, "put the phone down" play mode, no screen-based activities for the child.
- Every activity should include **"things to say"** prompts that model serve-and-return and conversational turns.
- Activities should feel joyful and repeatable (iterative) with **easier / harder variations** so the same game grows with the child.

## 2. Executive function (EF) games by age — Harvard activities guide

EF = working memory, inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility. Built through enjoyable activities at
"increasingly demanding levels"; adults scaffold, then step back.

| Age | Recommended play |
|---|---|
| **6–18 m** | Lap games with a predictable surprise (Peekaboo; Trot Trot to Boston; This is the Way the Farmer Rides; Pat-a-Cake). Hiding games (toy under a cloth → move it visibly → hide without showing → cups on a lazy Susan). Imitation/copy games (wave, line up animals, stack & knock down). Simple role play (sweeping, dusting). Fingerplays (Eensy Weensy Spider, Where is Thumbkin, Open Shut Them). Conversations following the infant's gaze. Bilingual talk is beneficial. |
| **18–36 m** | Active games with simple rules (run to a finish line and back, balance beam, throwing/catching). Follow the Leader, Punchinella, "Follow, follow". Freeze dance; start/stop songs (Jack in the Box, Popcorn, Ring Around the Rosie, Motorboat). Song games with many movements (Hokey Pokey, I'm a Little Teapot, Head Shoulders Knees & Toes). Narrating play; telling stories about shared events; labelling feelings. Sorting by size/shape/colour and *silly sorting* (small shapes → big bucket). Simple puzzles. Imaginary play where the child directs the adult. |
| **3–5 y** | Intentional imaginary play with roles (doctor's office), **play plans** drawn before playing (Tools of the Mind). Making their own props. Storytelling: dictate & make books, group stories, act out stories. Movement challenges: obstacle courses, balance beam, yoga with slow breathing, fast/slow dance, freeze in a pictured pose. Cumulative songs (She'll Be Coming 'Round the Mountain, Five Green and Speckled Frogs). Sorting by one rule then switching; *opposite* bingo/lotto; puzzles; **cooking**. |
| **5–7 y** | Card games: Concentration, Go Fish, Old Maid, Crazy Eights, Uno, Spoons, Snap, Slapjack. Strategy boards: Sorry!, Battleship, Parcheesi, mancala, checkers, Chinese checkers. Physical: freeze dance, musical chairs, Red Light Green Light, Duck Duck Goose, Mother May I?, What Time Is It Mr Fox?, Magic Word Game, four square, Simon Says / "Do This, Do That". Copy & call-and-response songs, Packing for a Picnic memory game, rounds (Row Row Row Your Boat), clapping rhythms (Miss Mary Mack). Mazes, logic puzzles, 20 Questions, Guess My Rule, I Spy. |
| **7–12 y** | Hearts, Spit, rummy; chess, Go; fantasy/strategy games; organised sports (soccer linked to better attention); jump rope, double Dutch, Chinese jump rope; flashlight tag, Ghost in the Graveyard; instruments, rounds, dance; crosswords, sudoku, Rubik's cube. |

## 3. Developmental milestones — CDC *Learn the Signs. Act Early.*

The CDC's checklists (revised with AAP; new 15- and 30-month checklists) list milestones that **most children
(≥75%) reach by each age**, grouped as **Social/Emotional, Language/Communication, Cognitive, Movement/Physical**.
Checklists exist for **2, 4, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 24, 30 months and 3, 4, 5 years**. They are *not* a screening
tool; concerns should be discussed with a doctor, and parents should not wait.

Examples gathered (full set is encoded in the app's milestone data):
- **2 m**: calms when spoken to/picked up; smiles when you talk; makes sounds other than crying; holds head up on tummy.
- **6 m**: laughs; takes turns making sounds; blows raspberries; reaches to grab a toy; rolls tummy→back.
- **9 m**: smiles/laughs at peek-a-boo; lifts arms to be picked up; looks for dropped objects; sits without support.
- **12 m**: plays pat-a-cake; waves bye-bye; looks for things you hide; pulls to stand; pincer grasp.
- **18 m**: points to show you something; tries 3+ words; copies chores; walks alone; scribbles.
- **24 m**: two-word phrases; plays with more than one toy together; kicks a ball; runs.
- **3 y**: two back-and-forth exchanges; asks who/what/where/why; draws a circle when shown.
- **4 y**: pretends to be something else; tells what comes next in a known story; catches a large ball.
- **5 y**: follows rules/takes turns in games; counts to 10; hops on one foot; pays attention 5–10 min.

The CDC parent tips at every age are themselves play prescriptions (copy baby's sounds, rattle to one side,
peek-a-boo, my-turn-your-turn, dump-and-fill blocks, "ready, set, go", follow the leader, red light/green light,
freeze dance, opposites, matching, counting, rhyming games, I Spy, Hot and Cold, Simon Says).

### Premature babies
Use **corrected (adjusted) age** = chronological age − weeks born early (relative to 40 weeks) until about
**24 months**, then chronological age (AAP/CDC guidance via secondary sources).

## 4. Movement, sleep and screens — official guidance

| Topic | Guidance | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Infant activity | Several times a day, especially interactive floor play; **≥30 min tummy time** across the day for non-mobile babies. Not restrained >1 h at a time (prams, high chairs, carriers). | WHO 2019 under-5 guidelines |
| Tummy time | Start from the first days home with short sessions (3–5 min, 2–3×/day), build up; Pathways.org: work toward **≥60 min/day of floor time by 6 months**. Always awake and supervised; back to sleep. | AAP (via secondary sources), Pathways.org |
| Screens | **<18 m**: avoid except video chat. **18–24 m**: if introduced, high-quality and co-viewed. **2–5 y**: ≤1 h/day high-quality, co-viewed. Family media plan; media-free meals and bedrooms. CDC: limit parents' own screen use around babies. | AAP 2016 + CDC milestone tips |
| Sleep | 0–3 m 14–17 h; 4–11 m 12–16 h (incl. naps). | WHO 2019 |

## 5. Evidence on specific kinds of play

| Play type | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Dialogic reading** | Ask, don't just read. **PEER** (Prompt, Evaluate, Expand, Repeat) with **CROWD** prompts (Completion, Recall, Open-ended, Wh-, Distancing). Larger expressive vocabulary gains vs. controls. Recall & distancing are harder — use sparingly with young preschoolers. | Whitehurst et al.; Reading Rockets |
| **Linear number board games** | Four 15-min sessions of a simple linear (1–10) board game narrowed the numeracy gap between low- and middle-income preschoolers; circular boards and colour-only versions did not. RCTs rated by What Works Clearinghouse. | Siegler & Ramani 2008, 2009; Ramani et al. 2012 |
| **Block play / spatial** | Block-building complexity at age 3 predicts concurrent & later spatial and math skills. Children playing *alone* didn't pick up spatial concepts; **guided play with spatial talk** ("on top", "beside", "taller") maximised spatial language. | Verdine et al. 2013; Bower et al. 2020; Ferrara et al. 2011 |
| **Risky play** | Six categories: heights, speed, dangerous tools, dangerous elements (water, fire), rough-and-tumble, wandering out of sight. Linked to more physical activity, better motor competence, spatial skills and risk-management. | Sandseter; Brussoni et al. systematic review |
| **Rough-and-tumble** | Review of 78 studies (Cambridge/LEGO Foundation): physical play (often with fathers) linked to better control of aggression and emotions. Works best when the adult is not overly dominant — let the child win sometimes, stop when it stops being fun. | Amodia-Bidakowska, Laverty & Ramchandani 2020 |
| **Singing & lullabies** | Lullaby singing linked to stronger bonding and fewer newborn crying episodes; infant-directed singing holds attention as well as books/toys and far better than recorded music; lullabies across cultures are inherently soothing. | Persico et al. 2017; Harvard Music Lab; Univ. of Miami |
| **Sensory / messy play** | Early visual-motor skills relate to later math and language; playdough, shredding paper, water, sand build fine and gross motor skills; real textures support concrete vocabulary. Evidence is mostly practitioner-level. | Head Start ECLKC *Designing Messy Play* |
| **Montessori at home** | Treasure baskets of safe natural objects (0–9 m), ball-posting & object-permanence boxes (6–12 m), posting boxes and knobbed puzzles (9–15 m), practical-life tasks (pouring, wiping, sweeping) for toddlers; "follow the child". | Montessori Notebook; Montessori Academy |
| **Bilingualism** | Bilingual children show EF advantages; families should play, sing and tell stories in their home language(s). | Harvard EF guide; Carlson & Meltzoff 2008 |

## 6. Safety rules that every activity must respect

- **Choking**: anything that fits through a **toilet-paper tube** (≈ the CPSC small-parts cylinder, 16 CFR 1501) is too
  small for under-3s. Watch for button batteries, magnets, balloons, small parts that break off.
- **Water**: constant, within-arm's-reach supervision in baths and around water play — never leave a child, even briefly.
- **Tummy time**: only when awake and supervised; always back to sleep.
- **Never shake a baby** (CDC).
- **Mouthing** is normal exploration in infancy — offer clean, non-sharp, non-toxic, large items.
- **Strings/cords** > 15 cm (6 in) are a strangulation risk for babies.
- **Risky play** is valuable but must be *risk*, not *hazard*: adult judges the hazard, child judges the risk.

## 8. Existing apps (competitive scan)

| App | What it does well | Gap PlayNest can fill |
|---|---|---|
| **Kinedu** | Daily plan tailored to age/stage (0–6 y), expert content, milestone assessment. | Subscription-gated; account required. |
| **BabySparks** | Daily parent-led activities (0–3 y) with videos across cognition, language, motor, social-emotional, sensory, self-care; milestone & growth tracking. | Stops at 3 y; reviews mention data loss and pushy content. |
| **Vroom** (Bezos Family Foundation) | 1000+ free "brain building" tips by age; Brain Building Basics: *Look, Follow, Chat, Take Turns, Stretch*; science "Brainy Background"; search by setting; reminders. | Tips only — no tracking, plans, milestones or sibling play. |
| **Lovevery app** | Stage-based play guides tied to physical play kits. | Mostly tied to buying kits. |

**PlayNest's position:** free, private (no account, data stays on the device), works offline, covers **0–8 years**,
combines **activities + science + milestones + balanced weekly plans + sibling play + journal**, and is designed to
minimise screen time for the parent.
