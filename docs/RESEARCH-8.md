# Research round 7: world research, a claims audit, and the worries parents bring at night (release 2.0)

Round 7 did three things. First, it scanned the most important parenting research published around the world from 2024 to 2026,
with a deliberate bias towards studies from Africa, South and East Asia, Latin America and the Middle East, and asked what each
finding should change in PlayNest. Second, it audited the research claims already in the app: about 60 claims about studies,
numbers and effects, checked against their sources and against later replications. Third, it filled gaps parents meet often and
that the app didn't yet cover: serious illness signs that are easy to miss, parents' own minds after a birth, night-time troubles,
online safety, leaving children home alone, feeding a baby when you're apart, slings, school avoidance, moving, a pet dying, and
left-handed children.

Everything is in English and Persian. **No clinician, psychologist or Persian-speaking reviewer has read any of it yet.** The
checklist is in §13 of [REVIEW-1.5.md](REVIEW-1.5.md). Full notes are in `research_notes/PlayNest phase 10 research round 7/`. Every
source is tagged opened, abstract, search-only or unverified, and only opened or abstract facts reached the app.

## What the audit found and fixed

**Nothing that could have caused harm.** Across the eight notes, every research agent also checked the existing guides on its
topic. They found **no P1 problems and 20 P2 problems** (wrong or misleading), all fixed in this release, plus P3 wording points.

**Red flags that waited too long or were filed under worry:**
- New wetting with great thirst sat under "talk to a doctor". It is one of the four signs of type 1 diabetes, and NICE refers
  suspected diabetes the same day. The `toilet-training` guide and the potty goal now say "the same day, and ask for a blood sugar
  test".
- The `tummy-bugs` "get help now" column now includes the ketoacidosis pattern (vomiting or tummy pain after weeks of thirst and
  weeing), a twisted testicle (tummy pain with testicle pain), and intussusception (bouts of screaming, pale or floppy between, red
  jelly poo). Tummy pain moving to the lower right is now "today".
- Green vomit in `constipation-and-poo` was "now or today"; it is now always "now".
- Headaches on school mornings were filed under worry in a goal and in `is-my-child-ok`. Headaches that wake a child or come with
  vomiting on waking are a brain-tumour warning sign (HeadSmart), so the body is checked first.
- `running-on-empty` put thoughts of self-harm under "talk to your doctor soon"; they are now "today". The first-year goal now
  names thoughts of ending your life, and the crisis screen's psychosis line adds seeming unusually high and not sleeping for days.
- The coach card on nightmares called nightmares after 6 a reason to see a doctor, but they are common up to 12 (AAP); the trigger
  is now frequency, distress or a frightening event.

**Research claims that didn't hold up (note 02):**
- **The bilingual advantage.** The guide said children with two languages did better on "attention and self-control tasks". The
  study found no self-control advantage, and a 2021 review of 23,000 children found no effect after publication bias. It now says
  larger studies mostly find little or no difference.
- **Praise for intelligence.** "Lab studies point the same way" hid a large 2019 repeat that failed. The guide now says so.
- **Spatial words.** A finding about lower-income families had been retold as one about parents of children who were behind.
- **Kangaroo care.** "Unless a baby needs breathing support" would have excluded many babies on simple breathing support whom WHO
  doesn't exclude.
- **Jamaica.** The famous trial was with toddlers whose growth was stunted; the guide now says so, and that the New York trial's
  result was parents' reports.
- A home-visiting programme in England "added no measurable benefit" is now "met none of its main goals" (a later follow-up found a
  small school-readiness gain), plus wording fixes on study sizes, populations (tea and iron in adults, myopia surveys mostly in
  China) and refs.

**Persian wording:** the bottle-feeding line «نوزاد را نسبتاً صاف در بغل بگیرید» could be read as "lying flat", the opposite of the
advice. It now says nearly sitting, head higher than the body.

## What the research changed

**World research, 2024 to 2026 (note 01).** Programmes reliably change what parents do: large trials in Kenya, Rwanda and Bangladesh
cut harsh discipline and raised play and fathers' involvement, but did not change children's development or IQ, and gains in calmer
discipline shrink over time. The new `parenting-courses` guide says exactly that. A 2025 review of 189 studies from 92 lower- and
middle-income countries found physical punishment linked with worse outcomes everywhere, and only for hitting and harsh words, not
calm discipline; `instead-of-smacking` now says so, which answers the question Iranian parents ask most: "is this only true in the
West?". Headlines kept out: screens "rewiring" babies' brains (168 children), infant programmes preventing obesity (high-certainty
null), mindfulness boosting parenting courses (Cochrane 2024: no added benefit), and "orchid children" (not replicated in more than
30,000 children).

**Signs not to wait on (note 03).** About 3 in 10 children across 13 countries already had ketoacidosis when diabetes was found;
single-hospital studies in Iran range from 24% to 84%, and in one, half had tummy pain and two children had surgery for
"appendicitis" before diabetes was found. With meningococcal disease, the classic rash came late (13 to 22 hours), after cold hands
and feet and leg pains. A twisted testicle can be saved in over 90% of cases within 6 hours and under 10% after 24, and often shows
as tummy pain. In one large study, parents saying "this illness is different" was strongly linked with serious infection. The guide
opens with "no list can tell you your child is fine".

**Parents' minds (note 04).** In a 2024 cohort, almost all new mothers had sudden thoughts of their baby being hurt by accident and
many had unwanted thoughts of harming the baby themselves; they faded by 6 months, and studies haven't linked them with harm. The
guide puts safety first and normalising second, as the crisis screens do. About 1 in 10 fathers have depression around a birth. The
Persian adds Iran's three postnatal visits, where health workers ask about mood, the 40 days «چله» framed as wanted rest and company,
and 1480, 123 and 115.

**Night-time (note 05).** Night terrors and sleepwalking run in families and are outgrown. A 104-child trial found asking a child to
look after a soft toy eased night fears for months. Two large trials found tonsil and adenoid removal improved behaviour and sleep but
not attention or thinking tests. Melatonin is a doctor's decision; no amounts.

**Online and alone (note 06).** Offenders pose as other children, so the old "tell the police if an adult is involved" is now
"whatever age they seem to be". Restrictions go with fewer risks but fewer skills; talk goes with less harm; neither is proven to
prevent abuse. The widely quoted "sharenting will cause two-thirds of identity fraud" forecast has no published method and isn't
used. No law sets a home-alone age in the UK or Iran; bodies differ (AAP, NSPCC, a stricter NHS page), so the guide gives readiness
signs, not an age.

**Milk and slings (note 07).** Storage times differ between the CDC, NHS, the Academy of Breastfeeding Medicine and Iran's
Ministry; the guide gives the shorter ones (4 hours, 4 days, about 6 months frozen) and says so. No trials exist of workplace
breastfeeding support, and mothers' work is neutral for children on average. Since 2025 the Lullaby Trust says feeding hands-free
in a sling is unsafe. The Persian gives Iran's nine months' paid maternity leave and the daily breastfeeding hour to 24 months.

**School age (note 08).** CBT-based programmes improved attendance moderately in 8 small studies; long absence tends to make the
fear worse, and the body is checked before worry is assumed. A single move is linked with only small differences, mostly with
frequent or later moves and other hardship. Say "died", not "put to sleep". About 1 in 10 children are left-handed; no study shows
switching helps, and family customs about the right hand get no ruling. The Persian left-handed writing step was made general,
because the NHS tips assume left-to-right writing.

## What shipped in 2.0

- **11 new guides (143 in all):** signs not to wait on; your mind after a baby; online safety without the fear; home alone and out
  alone; feeding your baby when you're apart; slings and carriers; night frights, sleepwalking and snoring; when your child doesn't
  want to go to school; moving home, school or country; left-handed children; parenting courses.
- **A "When a pet dies" section** in `children-and-animals`, and pet wording on the `someone-has-died` card.
- **92 new daily tips (621 in all).** Tips about thoughts of suicide stay in guides and on Help, never in the rotating tip.
- **20 P2 fixes** to existing content (above), the Persian bottle-feeding fix, and P3 fixes.
- **[CONTENT-GOVERNANCE.md](CONTENT-GOVERNANCE.md):** three new house rules from the audit (name the population; famous findings
  come with their repeats; red flags are never filed under worry) and a claims audit at the end of every round.

## Not added, and why

- **190 ext. 9, 1490, the cyber police line 096380 and the school line 1570**, until each is test-called.
- **The Ministry of Health's parenting-skills course for parents of 2 to 12-year-olds** at the comprehensive health centre. It is
  the natural Iranian route for `parenting-courses`, but was seen in search results only.
- **Fathers' two-week leave in Iran**, search-only.
- **"A baby who clearly prefers one hand before 12 months"**, one source only.
- **Intrusive-thought percentages** (95.8% and 53.9%), until the full papers are read.
- **Kawasaki disease "to protect the heart"** and **"most headaches aren't serious"**, not verified.
- **Monster spray, either way**: no study.
- **Doses, brands, Iranian prevalence figures with blame attached, and any political framing.** Never.

## Toward being recommended by health services

The audit is the biggest step this round towards what NICE and ORCHA-style reviewers look for: a documented check of every claim
against its source, with corrections stated openly. The next steps are unchanged and more urgent as the content grows: appoint
reviewers and work through REVIEW-1.5.md §8–13; add `reviewedBy` and `nextReview`; build "report a problem"; write the privacy
statement; test-call the pending numbers; confirm the Ministry's parenting-skills course; and test with Iranian parents.
